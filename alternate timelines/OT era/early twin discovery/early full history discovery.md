# Part 1

The celebration on Yavin had lasted long into the night.

Pilots sang loudly over stolen Imperial liquor. Mechanics danced on supply crates. Someone had painted a crude starbird across the wall beside the hangar entrance. Every corridor of the hidden base carried the strange, disbelieving energy of people who had expected to die only hours earlier.

Luke Skywalker should have been among them.

Instead, he sat alone in a quiet observation alcove near the upper levels of the temple, staring through ancient stone arches into the jungle darkness beyond. The medal from Princess Leia rested around his neck, cold against his chest.

The Death Star was gone.

Ben was avenged.

The Rebellion had survived.

Everything he had dreamed about while staring at Tatooine’s twin suns had somehow happened all at once.

Yet the victory felt strangely hollow.

Han Solo was gone. The Millennium Falcon had vanished from Yavin almost immediately after the battle. No farewell. No explanation. Just gone into hyperspace with Chewbacca and the reward money.

Luke told himself he didn’t care.

He had other things now.

Purpose. Destiny.

And Leia.

Footsteps approached softly behind him.

“You disappeared,” Leia said.

Luke turned. She had changed out of her ceremonial gown into a simple white jacket and dark trousers, though her hair still carried traces of the elaborate braids from the ceremony. Without the royal formality, she looked younger. Tired, too.

“I needed quiet,” Luke admitted.

Leia leaned against the stone beside him. For a while neither spoke.

Outside, distant jungle insects hummed beneath the stars.

“It’s strange,” Leia finally said. “Everyone’s celebrating, but all I can think about is Alderaan.”

Luke looked down.

“I know.”

“No,” she said softly. “You really don’t.”

He almost argued, then stopped himself. She was right. He had lost his aunt and uncle. It hurt more than he could describe. But Leia had watched an entire world die.

After a moment, she exhaled shakily.

“I keep thinking I should have done more.”

“You did everything.”

“That’s easy for you to say. You destroyed the Death Star.”

Luke gave a faint smile.

“Barely.”

“But you did it.”

Their eyes met.

For the first time since they had escaped the Death Star, there was no emergency around them. No alarms. No battle plans. No fear of being overheard.

Just silence.

Leia stepped closer.

“When I saw you flying toward that station…” she said quietly, “I thought you were going to die.”

Luke’s heartbeat quickened.

“I thought the same thing about you back there.”

She laughed softly through her exhaustion.

“You came back for me anyway.”

“You would’ve done the same.”

“Yes,” she admitted. “I would have.”

The distance between them disappeared almost without either noticing.

Luke touched her hand first.

Leia did not pull away.

The kiss was hesitant at first—uncertain, searching—but after weeks of danger and grief and impossible survival, restraint gave way to relief. To closeness. To desperately needing someone alive to hold onto.

Then came an explosive electronic SHRIEK.

R2-D2 let out a scandalized series of beeps so loud they echoed through the stone chamber.

Luke and Leia jerked apart.

“Artoo!” Luke exclaimed.

The astromech rolled forward aggressively, dome swiveling back and forth with furious indignation. More sharp whistles erupted from his vocabulator.

Leia blinked at him.

“What is wrong with him?”

“I don’t know,” Luke said. “Artoo, what—”

The droid interrupted with another furious chirp.

Then his projector lens extended.

A blue holographic image flickered into existence between them.

Luke frowned.

“Artoo?”

The image stabilized.

A medical chamber appeared in shimmering blue light.

Luke and Leia watched in confusion as figures materialized within the recording: Obi-Wan Kenobi, Yoda, Bail Organa… and a woman lying exhausted on an operating platform.

Something about her face struck both of them instantly.

Not familiarity exactly.

Something deeper.

The hologram spoke.

“Don’t give up, Padmé,” Obi-Wan said gently.

The woman winced in pain.

A medical droid lifted a newborn infant.

“It’s a boy.”

The woman managed a weak smile.

“Luke…”

Luke froze.

The hologram continued.

“…and a girl.”

The woman struggled to speak.

“…Leia.”

Silence filled the alcove.

Luke and Leia slowly turned toward one another, confusion spreading across both faces.

R2-D2 emitted a smug little beep.

Then the next recording began automatically.

Obi-Wan leaned over the dying woman.

“You have twins, Padmé. They need you… hang on.”

“I can’t…”

Luke felt cold.

Leia’s expression tightened.

“There… is good in him,” Padmé whispered weakly. “I know there is… still…”

Her body went still.

Obi-Wan lowered his head.

The recording dissolved.

Leia stared at the empty air.

“No,” she whispered.

R2 projected another image before either could speak.

Now they saw Yoda, Obi-Wan, and Bail Organa seated together aboard a starship.

“Hidden, safe, the children must be kept,” Yoda said.

“We must take them somewhere the Sith will not sense their presence,” Obi-Wan replied.

“Split up, they should be.”

Bail Organa folded his hands.

“My wife and I will take the girl. We’ve always talked of adopting a baby girl. She will be loved with us.”

“And what of the boy?” Obi-Wan asked.

“To Tatooine,” Yoda said. “To his family, send him.”

“I will take the child and watch over him.”

Luke stopped breathing.

Leia looked physically ill.

The scene changed once more.

Bail Organa approached Captain Antilles in a starship corridor.

“I’m placing these droids in your care,” Bail said. “Treat them well. Clean them up. Have the protocol droid’s mind wiped.”

C-3PO’s horrified voice rang out:

“Oh, no!”

The hologram vanished.

R2-D2 gave several intensely satisfied beeps.

Then, without another explanation, he turned sharply and rolled away down the corridor.

Luke and Leia remained frozen beside one another.

Their hands were still touching.

Neither moved them.

Neither seemed able to.

Leia stared after the departing droid as if the universe itself had suddenly stopped making sense.

Luke’s voice emerged faintly.

“We…”

Leia slowly looked at him.

The truth settled over both of them in terrible silence.

Brother and sister.

The same mother.

The same father.

Every memory of the past weeks suddenly rearranged itself inside their minds with horrifying clarity.

The detention block.

The trash compactor.

The Falcon.

The ceremony.

The kiss from only seconds ago.

Leia abruptly pulled her hand away as though burned.

Luke stepped backward at the exact same moment.

For several seconds neither could even look directly at the other.

Finally Leia spoke, very quietly.

“Why didn’t anyone tell us?”

Luke swallowed hard, still staring at the floor.

“Ben knew.”

“Yes. And my father Bail too.”

Luke leaned heavily against the stone wall, dizzy.

The jungle sounds outside continued as if nothing in the galaxy had changed.

But everything had.

# Part 2

Luke and Leia left the ancient stone alcove in silence.

The revelation R2-D2 had forced upon them changed everything they thought they knew about themselves. They now knew they had been born together, that they had been separated as infants, and that the woman who had given birth to them had called them by name before she died.

They also knew they were alone.

Owen and Beru Lars had been murdered by Imperial stormtroopers on Tatooine.

Bail and Breha Organa had died with Alderaan.

The only family either of them had left was each other.

That knowledge made the mystery of their origins feel even more important.

The next morning, Luke found R2-D2 outside one of the Rebel maintenance bays.

The astromech was happily allowing technicians to polish scorch marks from his battered dome.

Luke folded his arms.

"Artoo."

The droid chirped innocently.

"I want to ask you something."

Another cheerful whistle.

Leia arrived moments later carrying a datapad.

"I thought I'd find you here."

She looked down at R2.

"You've been holding onto those recordings for 19 years."

The astromech let out a short beep.

Luke crouched beside him.

"The woman in the recording. Padmé."

A questioning whistle.

"Who was she?"

R2 emitted a stream of beeps.

Luke sighed.

"I know you know."

More beeping.

Leia knelt beside Luke.

"And our father?"

R2's dome rotated away.

"Who was he?"

The astromech made a long electronic raspberry.

Luke blinked.

"Did he just refuse to answer?"

Leia frowned.

"I think he did."

She tried another approach.

"Please, Artoo. We deserve to know."

The droid answered with several polite but unmistakably evasive whistles.

Luke pointed accusingly.

"You've known all this time."

A brief affirmative beep.

"And you're still not telling us?"

Another affirmative beep.

Leia pinched the bridge of her nose.

"You're impossible."

R2 gave a smug little trill.

Luke tried again.

"Was our father a Jedi?"

Silence.

"Was Padmé a senator?"

Silence.

"Do you know where either of them came from?"

Silence again.

R2 simply extended one manipulator, accepted a fresh polishing cloth from a mechanic, and resumed allowing himself to be cleaned as though the conversation had become beneath his attention.

Luke stood.

"That's it?"

Leia stared at the little droid.

"He'll show us the recordings, but he won't answer any questions."

Luke shook his head.

"I don't think we're getting anything else out of him."

Reluctantly, they gave up.

C-3PO proved even less useful.

"I do apologize," the protocol droid said. "I am told my memory was wiped many years ago. Most unfortunate. I do wish I could be of greater assistance."

Luke forced a smile.

"It isn't your fault."

Over the following days, Luke and Leia searched for anyone who might recognize the people they had seen in R2's recordings.

Obi-Wan Kenobi was dead.

Bail Organa was dead.

The only other living witness they had seen in the recording was the tiny green creature seated beside Obi-Wan.

Unfortunately, they knew nothing about him.

At first they simply described him to anyone willing to listen.

"You've never heard his name?" one aging Rebel pilot asked.

Luke shook his head.

"No. We only know he was there when we were separated."

"The little green fellow?" another veteran murmured. "Pointed ears?"

Leia nodded eagerly.

"Yes."

The older man smiled faintly.

"That's Yoda."

Luke repeated the unfamiliar name.

"Yoda."

"He was Grand Master of the Jedi Order before the Empire."

Leia exchanged a glance with Luke.

"Do you know where he is?"

The veteran's smile faded.

"No."

"Is he alive?"

"I honestly couldn't tell you."

Every lead ended the same way.

Yes, people remembered Yoda.

No, nobody knew where he had gone.

Eventually Luke and Leia accepted that they had reached another dead end.

Instead, they turned their attention to the dying woman whose final words had named them.

Padmé.

Unlike Yoda, nobody seemed to recognize that name.

"Padmé who?" one Rebel officer asked.

"We don't know."

"A family name?"

"We don't know that either."

"Homeworld?"

Luke sighed.

"We don't know."

Without a surname, every conversation dissolved into speculation.

By the end of several frustrating weeks, Luke and Leia had almost exhausted every clue R2-D2's recordings had given them.

Then one afternoon, a transport arrived at the Rebel base carrying several visitors.

Leia smiled the moment she recognized one of them.

"Pooja!"

Pooja Naberrie hurried forward and embraced her warmly.

"It is good to see you alive."

"You too."

The two women had served together in the Imperial Senate for years before open war had made such meetings impossible. Leia had represented Alderaan, while Pooja had represented Naboo. Long hours in committee chambers and countless political debates had slowly turned professional respect into genuine friendship.

Luke greeted her politely before giving the two senators time to catch up.

Later that evening, while they sat together over a quiet meal, Leia mentioned the strange discoveries she and Luke had made.

"We recently learned the name of our birth mother."

Pooja looked up.

"You found records?"

"Not exactly."

Leia briefly explained the recordings R2-D2 had revealed and the secret surrounding her and Luke's birth.

"The only name we learned was Padmé."

Pooja froze.

"Padmé?"

Leia nodded.

"Do you know anyone by that name?"

"What did she look like?"

Leia thought back to the shimmering blue hologram.

"Dark hair. Brown eyes. She looked... graceful. Beautiful. She was dying when we saw her."

As Leia continued describing the woman's face, Pooja's eyes grew wider and wider.

When Leia finished, Pooja whispered,

"I think I know who she was."

Luke leaned forward.

"You do?"

"My aunt."

Leia blinked.

"Your aunt?"

"Padmé Amidala."

Silence settled over the table.

"Are you sure?" Leia asked carefully.

Pooja nodded slowly.

"How do you know?"

Pooja studied Leia's face.

"I've thought it before, but I never said anything. You resemble her. Not exactly... but enough."

Luke and Leia exchanged stunned looks.

"I'm almost completely sure," Pooja continued. "If I'm right..."

She smiled uncertainly.

"...then you should come with me to Naboo."

A few days later, a Rebel transport landed in Theed.

Pooja led Luke and Leia through familiar streets until they reached the Naberrie family home.

She opened the door.

"I'm home!"

Footsteps approached.

A woman somewhat older than Pooja appeared first.

"Pooja?"

She smiled.

"My sister, Ryoo," Pooja said.

Another woman followed moments later.

"And my mother."

The older woman offered a welcoming smile.

"I'm Sola Naberrie."

Pooja gestured toward her guests.

"Mother... these are possible cousins."

Sola looked first at Leia, then at Luke.

"Possible cousins?"

"We're not completely certain yet," Pooja admitted.

Introductions were exchanged, and before long everyone was seated together.

Leia carefully explained everything.

She described the recordings R2-D2 had revealed, the dying woman named Padmé, the decision to separate two newborn children, and the years of secrecy that had followed.

When she finished, Sola sat quietly for several moments.

Finally she spoke.

"Padmé was my sister."

Leia felt her breath catch.

"Our grandparents?" Luke asked.

"My parents were Ruwee and Jobal Naberrie," Sola answered softly. "They both passed away from old age a few years ago."

"I'm sorry," Leia said.

Sola nodded gratefully.

"Padmé died 19 years ago, near the end of the Clone Wars."

Luke looked toward Leia.

"We never knew."

Sola continued.

"There was something that always troubled me."

"What?"

"At her funeral... she appeared heavily pregnant."

Luke and Leia stared.

Sola frowned thoughtfully.

"At the time we believed she had died before giving birth."

Leia slowly shook her head.

"According to the recording, Obi-Wan Kenobi, Bail Organa, and Yoda decided to separate us after we were born."

Understanding slowly spread across Sola's face.

"They wanted to protect you."

Luke nodded.

"They sent me to Tatooine."

"And me to Alderaan."

Sola leaned back.

"Then the pregnancy shown during Padmé's funeral..." She exhaled slowly. "...must have been staged. They wanted everyone to believe the child had died with her."

The room fell quiet.

After a moment Luke asked the question weighing most heavily on both of them.

"Do you know who our father was?"

Sola hesitated.

"To my knowledge... Padmé was never married."

Leia frowned.

"Was she ever close to anyone?"

Sola smiled faintly at an old memory.

"There was one man."

Luke leaned forward.

"Who?"

"His name was Anakin Skywalker."

Luke stiffened.

"He was a Jedi?"

"Yes."

"What do you remember about him?"

"Not very much," Sola admitted. "He came to our home 22 years ago. Padmé insisted he was only her Jedi bodyguard and protector."

Ryoo laughed softly.

"None of us believed that."

Sola smiled.

"My parents certainly didn't."

"The way they looked at each other," Ryoo added. "It wasn't difficult to notice."

Sola nodded.

"They cared about one another. That much was obvious."

Luke swallowed.

"My last name..."

Sola looked at him.

"...is Skywalker."

The room became utterly still.

Sola stared first at Luke, then at Leia.

Finally she spoke quietly.

"Then I think there is very little doubt."

Leia held her breath.

"You are almost certainly the children of Padmé Amidala and Anakin Skywalker."

Neither twin spoke for several seconds.

Luke eventually found his voice.

"What else can you tell us about Anakin?"

Sola shook her head apologetically.

"Very little. He was a Jedi Padawan when I met him. He carried a blue lightsaber."

She smiled sadly.

"I remember him mentioning his master."

Luke looked up.

"Who?"

"Obi-Wan Kenobi."

Luke's thoughts immediately returned to the old hermit on Tatooine.

He remembered Obi-Wan's words as clearly as the day he had spoken them.

"A young Jedi named Darth Vader, who was a pupil of mine until he turned to evil, helped the Empire hunt down and destroy the Jedi Knights. He betrayed and murdered your father. Now the Jedi are all but extinct. Vader was seduced by the dark side of the Force."

Leia looked at him.

"What is it?"

Luke frowned in thought.

"When I first met Ben, he told me that Darth Vader had once been one of his students."

Leia nodded.

"And now we know Anakin Skywalker was one of his students too."

He stared out the window toward the lights of Theed.

"So one of Obi-Wan's students betrayed and murdered another."

Leia considered it.

"Which one did he teach first?"

Luke looked back toward Sola.

"Do you know?"

She slowly shook her head.

"No."

There was nothing more to add.

Some questions had finally been answered.

Others had only become more mysterious.

Sola smiled warmly at the twins.

"You've both been through enough."

Leia looked up.

"You've only just met us."

"You're family," Sola said simply.

She stood.

"Stay here for a few days."

Luke looked toward Leia.

Leia smiled for the first time since arriving.

"We'd like that."

For the first time in their lives, Luke and Leia were surrounded by members of their mother's family, learning the small stories and memories of the woman whose face they had only first seen through the blue light of an old astromech's forgotten recording.

# Part 3

That night, long after the rest of the Naberrie household had gone to bed, Luke and Leia remained awake in the quiet upstairs room Sola had prepared for them.

Moonlight filtered through the window overlooking the streets of Theed.

Neither of them spoke for a long time.

Leia finally broke the silence.

"We know more than we did yesterday."

Luke gave a small nod.

"But not enough."

He sat on the edge of the bed, elbows resting on his knees.

"Let's go through everything."

Leia pulled a chair closer.

"All right."

Luke began counting on his fingers.

"22 years ago, Anakin Skywalker was still Obi-Wan's Padawan. That's when Sola met him."

Leia nodded.

"Then, 19 years ago, the Empire was created."

"The same time Padmé died."

"And the same time we were born."

Luke looked thoughtfully at the floor.

"Ben told me that Darth Vader murdered my father."

"Our father," Leia corrected quietly.

Luke nodded.

"Our father."

He paused.

"So... when could that have happened?"

Leia answered first.

"Not before Padmé became pregnant."

Luke agreed.

"If Anakin died before that, we wouldn't exist."

"And it couldn't have been much after we were born."

Luke frowned.

"If he'd still been alive for months or years afterward..."

"He would have remembered us."

Luke nodded.

"And Ben, Bail, and Yoda wouldn't have immediately started talking about splitting us up and sending us to different families."

Leia folded her arms.

"They would at least have considered letting our father raise us."

Luke slowly reached the same conclusion.

"So if Vader really murdered Anakin..."

"...it had to happen sometime after Padmé became pregnant but before we were born."

Silence settled over the room as they absorbed the narrowness of that window.

Leia leaned back.

"Now let's think about Obi-Wan."

Luke looked up.

"He trained Anakin."

"And according to what he told you..."

"He trained Vader too."

Luke stared into the darkness.

"If Anakin was still his apprentice 22 years ago..."

"...then Vader would have had to be trained after him."

Luke continued the thought.

"That only gives Vader about 2 or 3 years of training."

Leia frowned.

"Could someone become powerful enough in that amount of time to defeat a fully trained Jedi Knight?"

Luke slowly shook his head.

"It doesn't sound likely."

"Especially if Anakin had already completed his own training."

Luke nodded.

"So maybe Vader was Obi-Wan's apprentice first."

Leia considered it.

"That would give Vader much longer to become strong."

"It would."

For a moment the explanation seemed satisfying.

Then Luke's expression changed.

"Wait."

Leia looked at him.

"What?"

Luke rubbed his forehead.

"Ben told me he was 57."

Leia nodded.

"So 22 years ago..."

"He would have been 35."

Luke stood and began pacing slowly.

"On the Falcon, Ben told me training a Padawan usually takes about 10 years."

Leia followed his reasoning immediately.

"If he trained Anakin from about age 25 to 35..."

Luke nodded.

"...then he'd have had to train Vader from about 15 to 25."

Leia blinked.

"Fifteen?"

Luke spread his hands.

"That doesn't leave enough time."

"For what?"

"For Obi-Wan himself."

Leia frowned.

"He would've had to finish his own training, become a Jedi Knight, and then immediately begin training Vader."

Luke nodded.

"At around fifteen years old."

They both sat with that calculation.

"It feels..." Leia searched for the right word.

"...too tight."

Luke agreed.

"Maybe Obi-Wan's own training was unusually fast."

"Or Anakin's."

"Or Vader's."

"Maybe."

Neither of them sounded convinced.

Luke stopped pacing.

"There's another possibility."

Leia looked up.

"What?"

"What if Obi-Wan only trained one apprentice?"

She frowned.

"How?"

"We know Anakin was his Padawan."

"And we know Vader was."

Luke nodded slowly.

"The only way both statements can be true..."

Leia stared at him.

"...is if Anakin and Vader were the same person."

The room became completely silent.

After several seconds Leia spoke.

"But then..."

"...Ben lied."

Luke nodded reluctantly.

"He told me Vader murdered Anakin."

Neither spoke for a while.

Leia finally said, "Is there any explanation without a lie?"

Luke thought carefully.

"One."

"What?"

"If Vader was never Obi-Wan's apprentice."

Leia considered it.

"Then Obi-Wan lied about that instead."

Luke nodded.

"But why?"

Leia searched for an answer.

"I don't know."

Luke shook his head.

"I can't think of any reason he'd invent that."

"No."

Leia looked toward the moonlit window.

"But saying Vader murdered Anakin..."

She hesitated.

"...that at least has a purpose."

Luke looked at her.

"It keeps me from knowing my father became Darth Vader."

Leia nodded slowly.

"It could have been his way of protecting you."

Luke lowered his head.

"He wouldn't have been the first adult to hide the truth from us."

The conversation continued for a long time, circling back over every fact they had learned.

Every possibility produced another contradiction.

Every explanation left another unanswered question.

Eventually Luke leaned back against the wall.

"I think this is the explanation with the fewest problems."

Leia looked at him.

"Anakin and Vader are the same person."

Luke nodded.

"And Ben lied."

Leia quietly reached the same conclusion.

"I think so too."

Neither twin spoke for several minutes.

The implications settled over them with crushing weight.

Leia remembered the cold black armor standing over her in the interrogation chamber aboard the Death Star.

The mechanical voice.

The relentless questioning.

His silent approval while Grand Moff Tarkin ordered Alderaan destroyed.

She closed her eyes.

"My father..."

Her voice nearly failed.

"...did that."

Across the room, Luke remembered the duel aboard the Death Star.

The blue and crimson blades crossing.

Obi-Wan calmly lowering his weapon.

The flash.

His teacher disappearing.

Luke swallowed hard.

"My father killed Ben."

Neither of them tried to soften the truth they now believed.

Leia finally looked toward her brother.

"We can't change what he's done."

Luke nodded.

"No."

"But we can decide what we become."

He met her eyes.

"If Anakin Skywalker became Darth Vader..."

Leia finished the thought.

"...then we'll spend our lives doing the opposite."

"We'll help people."

"We'll stop the Empire."

"We'll undo as much of his damage as we can."

Luke gave a quiet nod.

"No matter how long it takes."

The decision did not erase the grief.

It did not answer every question.

It did not make the mystery surrounding Obi-Wan's words any less troubling.

But it gave them direction.

Whatever blood they had inherited, their choices would be their own.

At last, with the house around them peaceful and silent, Luke and Leia extinguished the light.

For the first time since learning they were twins, they fell asleep beneath the roof of their mother's family, surrounded by people who welcomed them not as strangers, but as kin.

# Part 4

3 years passed.

The Rebel Alliance never remained in one place for long.

Each victory against the Empire was answered with relentless pursuit. Hidden bases were abandoned. Supply depots were evacuated in haste. Fleets disappeared into hyperspace only hours before Imperial task forces arrived. Every safe haven eventually became another battlefield.

Luke Skywalker and Leia Organa matured alongside the Rebellion.

The discovery that they were twins—and the children of Padmé Amidala and Anakin Skywalker—remained known only to a handful of people. Even among their closest allies, the truth stayed hidden.

During those years, Luke and Leia trained together whenever duty allowed. Without a Jedi Master, they relied on instinct, old lessons from Obi-Wan Kenobi, and endless practice against one another. Wooden staffs became lightsabers. Meditation became routine. Little by little, their connection to the Force deepened.

One evening, after an especially intense sparring session, a familiar blue glow appeared between them.

Obi-Wan Kenobi's spirit slowly took shape.

Luke immediately stood.

"Ben."

Leia bowed her head respectfully.

The old Jedi smiled gently.

"You've both come farther than I hoped."

Luke stepped forward.

"We've done everything we could remember."

"I know."

Leia asked the question that had lingered for years.

"Is there anyone left who can teach us?"

Obi-Wan nodded.

"There is."

Luke's eyes widened.

"Yoda."

"You remember him."

"The little green fellow from Artoo's recording," Leia said.

Obi-Wan smiled faintly.

"He is Grand Master of the Jedi Order."

Luke leaned forward eagerly.

"Where is he?"

"On Dagobah."

Neither twin had ever heard of the planet.

"It is there," Obi-Wan continued, "that you must complete your training."

Not long afterward, Luke and Leia departed aboard an Alliance transport with R2-D2 accompanying them. C-3PO remained behind with Han Solo, Chewbacca, and the Millennium Falcon.

Han had returned to the Alliance some time earlier.

After finally settling his debt with Jabba the Hutt, he had discovered that walking away from the Rebellion had become impossible. Somewhere between escaping the Death Star, fighting beside Luke, and watching Leia grow from an impossible princess into one of the Alliance's strongest leaders, his priorities had quietly changed.

He came back.

Chewbacca came with him.

So did the Falcon.

No one complained.

Dagobah was unlike any world Luke or Leia had imagined.

It was damp, ancient, and overflowing with life.

Yoda proved every bit as mysterious as the stories suggested.

Training under him was harder than either of them expected.

He challenged not only their bodies but their thoughts, their fears, and their certainty.

Months stretched onward.

Then, one day, everything changed.

Far across the galaxy, Emperor Palpatine reached a decision.

"The boy who destroyed the Death Star and the Rebel princess continue to evade us," he said from the throne aboard his command ship.

Darth Vader knelt before him.

"They hide well."

"They will reveal themselves."

The Emperor's yellow eyes narrowed.

"They have attachments."

Vader remained silent.

"Capture their friends."

Cloud City floated peacefully above Bespin's endless orange clouds.

Han Solo had convinced Leia that he deserved at least one vacation after years of constant fighting. Luke and Leia remained on Dagobah, while Han, Chewbacca, C-3PO, and the Millennium Falcon accepted Lando Calrissian's invitation to spend several quiet days in Cloud City.

The peace ended with the arrival of an Imperial fleet.

Alarm sirens echoed through the city.

"Lando!" an aide shouted. "Star Destroyers!"

Lando looked through the observation window.

"...Well."

He sighed.

"So much for a quiet week."

Cloud City's security forces fought bravely.

They were hopelessly outmatched.

Imperial troops flooded the platforms and corridors with terrifying speed.

Han fired until his blaster clicked empty.

Chewbacca roared while hurling stormtroopers aside.

Lando organized defensive positions wherever he could.

It made no difference.

One by one, they were overwhelmed.

Vader personally oversaw the prisoners.

Han was separated from the others.
