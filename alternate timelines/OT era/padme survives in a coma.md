# Prologue

The medical center on Polis Massa was quiet except for the soft hum of machinery and the faint electronic chatter of droids. Outside its sealed walls stretched the barren surface of the asteroid, isolated from the turmoil consuming the galaxy. Inside, Padmé Amidala lay exhausted on an operating table after everything that had happened on Mustafar.

Only hours earlier, the Republic she had served as senator had effectively ceased to exist. Supreme Chancellor Palpatine had proclaimed himself Emperor, the Jedi Order had been shattered, and Anakin Skywalker had fallen to the dark side. Padmé had followed Anakin to Mustafar in a desperate attempt to convince him to abandon the path he had chosen. Instead, Anakin had accused her of betrayal after seeing Obi-Wan Kenobi emerge from her ship. In his rage, he had seized Padmé through the Force and strangled her until she lost consciousness.

Obi-Wan had carried her away after defeating Anakin in their duel and leaving him grievously wounded beside the lava river. Padmé had been brought to Polis Massa, where medical droids discovered that she was carrying twins and that the children had to be delivered immediately.

Obi-Wan remained beside her throughout the procedure.

"Don't give up, Padmé."

Padmé winced from the pain.

The medical droid lifted the first newborn child.

"It's a boy."

Padmé's exhausted expression softened into the faintest smile.

"Luke..."

She struggled to raise her hand and gently touched the infant's forehead.

A short time later, the droid lifted the second child.

"...and a girl."

Padmé looked toward her daughter.

"...Leia."

Beyond the operating chamber, Bail Organa, Yoda, R2-D2, and C-3PO watched in silence. None of them could ignore the enormity of what had happened. The children's father had become Darth Vader, servant of the Emperor, while their mother was barely conscious.

Elsewhere, on Coruscant, Anakin himself was undergoing a transformation of another kind.

Medical droids worked around his ruined body inside an Imperial rehabilitation center. The injuries he had suffered on Mustafar had left him dependent upon machinery merely to survive. Prosthetic limbs replaced those he had lost. Black armor enclosed what remained of his body. A mask descended over his burned face and sealed into place.

For the first time, the mechanical breathing apparatus activated.

Darth Vader breathed.

Back on Polis Massa, Obi-Wan leaned over Padmé.

"You have twins, Padmé. They need you... hang on."

Padmé barely managed to answer.

"I can't..."

Another wave of pain passed through her. She reached for Obi-Wan's hand. In her other hand she clutched the small japor snippet Anakin had carved for her years before, when they had first known one another on Tatooine.

"Save your energy," Obi-Wan said.

Padmé's eyes remained fixed weakly upon him.

"Obi-Wan... there... is good in him. I know there is... still..."

Her voice faded.

Her eyes closed.

Obi-Wan waited for the rest of the sentence.

It never came.

"Padmé?"

He tightened his grip on her hand.

"Padmé, can you hear me?"

There was no response.

For a terrible moment Obi-Wan feared the worst. He looked immediately toward the medical droid attending her.

The droid examined the readings.

"She appears to have fallen into a deep coma, sir."

Obi-Wan stared at Padmé.

"A coma?"

C-3PO stood nearby, his golden hands hovering anxiously in front of him.

"Oh dear..."

Yoda approached the bedside. His expression was grave. He studied Padmé for several moments, sensing what machinery could not.

"Devastated by Anakin's fall, she must be," Yoda said. "As long as he remains in the dark side, awake she will not."

Obi-Wan looked from Yoda to Padmé.

The thought seemed almost incomprehensible. Padmé was alive. Her heart still beat. Her children had survived. Yet whatever bond remained between her and Anakin had dragged her into a darkness from which she could not simply be awakened.

Obi-Wan exhaled slowly.

"Well then, what do we do with her?"

Bail Organa considered the problem.

Keeping Padmé alive was one matter. Keeping her existence secret was another.

"Polis Massa is remote enough," Bail said. "Palpatine does not know that we are here. We should stabilize her condition and then keep her here."

Obi-Wan turned to the medical droid.

"Can this be done?"

The droid checked the monitors again.

"Her condition appears stable. We will hook her up to some monitoring and life-supporting devices, and we can move her into an underground bunker on this asteroid."

Obi-Wan looked around the medical center and then back at Padmé.

"Perfect. Sounds like a plan."

Yoda lowered his head.

"May the Force protect her."

For a moment, nobody spoke.

Then Bail's expression changed.

"And what should we tell the public?"

Obi-Wan blinked.

"Oh, right."

Padmé Amidala could not simply disappear.

She had been Queen of Naboo, a senator of the Galactic Republic, one of the most prominent political figures of her generation, and an outspoken public opponent of many of Palpatine's wartime measures. Her absence would immediately be noticed. Questions would follow. Investigations might follow those questions. Any trail leading to Polis Massa could endanger not merely Padmé but her children.

"Padmé is far too famous to suddenly disappear without an explanation," Obi-Wan said. "Let me think..."

He paced a short distance, considering their increasingly limited options.

Then he stopped.

"Tell the public that a strong wind accidentally blew her into a lava river on Mustafar. She was instantly incinerated by the lava. Hence no body."

Bail stared at him.

"That sounds a bit exaggerated."

"It is."

"Will the public believe it?"

Obi-Wan considered the galaxy of the past several days: the Chancellor becoming Emperor, the Jedi being declared traitors, the Jedi Temple burning, clone soldiers suddenly turning against their generals, entire institutions collapsing almost overnight.

"The public has experienced enough normally unbelievable events recently," he said. "The death of one senator through a slightly unusual means should not faze them."

Bail was not entirely reassured.

"More importantly, will Palpatine believe it? And will Anakin believe it?"

At the mention of Anakin, Obi-Wan's face hardened with sadness.

"Palpatine should at least not be surprised that Padmé headed to Mustafar. As for Anakin..."

He paused.

"I have no clue what Palpatine told him about Padmé. But he can't disprove this narrative. So I think it can work."

Bail thought about it for another moment.

"Okay. Let's get to work then."

C-3PO looked from one conspirator to another.

"Oh dear, what have we gotten ourselves into now?"

The medical droids began preparing Padmé for long-term care.

Her children were removed from the operating chamber while additional equipment was brought in. Sensors were attached to monitor her heart, respiration, brain activity, and other vital functions. Life-support systems were prepared to sustain her through what none of them could predict might be a very long sleep.

Padmé did not stir.

The japor snippet was carefully kept with her.

Below the medical complex, Polis Massan personnel prepared an underground bunker where she could remain concealed. The chamber was isolated beneath the asteroid's surface, far from ordinary traffic through the facility. Medical equipment was installed, power connections secured, and monitoring systems arranged so that her condition could be watched continuously without requiring her presence in the more visible portions of the station.

When everything was ready, Padmé was transferred below.

The medical platform carrying her moved through sterile corridors and descended into the hidden levels beneath the facility. Obi-Wan, Yoda, and Bail accompanied her for part of the journey.

At last the platform entered the bunker.

The medical droid connected the final systems.

"Life-support functions stable."

Obi-Wan stood beside Padmé one last time before leaving Polis Massa.

She looked almost peaceful now. Without the pain of childbirth and the strain of consciousness twisting her expression, she might have seemed merely asleep.

But she could not hear him.

At least, Obi-Wan had no way of knowing whether she could.

He looked down at her.

"Your children are safe, Padmé."

The machines continued their steady rhythm.

Nothing else moved.

By the following day, the deception was ready.

Bail Organa appeared in a public broadcast carried across the galaxy. His expression was solemn. Behind him stood the symbols appropriate to the announcement of the death of one of the Republic's most recognizable senators.

"Citizens of Naboo, and citizens throughout the galaxy," Bail began, "it is my painful duty to announce the death of Senator Padmé Amidala."

Across countless worlds, people stopped to listen.

"Senator Amidala traveled to Mustafar during the final hours of the recent crisis. While there, she was caught in a violent and unexpected atmospheric disturbance. A powerful wind threw her from safe ground and into one of Mustafar's lava rivers."

Bail maintained the composed gravity of a statesman delivering terrible news.

"The conditions made recovery impossible. Senator Amidala was instantly incinerated."

There would be no body.

There could be no body.

Bail continued by speaking of Padmé's years of service to Naboo and to the Republic, her time as Queen, and her work in the Senate. He did not mention Polis Massa. He did not mention the twins. He did not mention that beneath the surface of a remote asteroid, Padmé Amidala was still breathing.

The official story spread rapidly.

On Coruscant, it reached the Imperial government as well.

Darth Vader had survived his injuries, although survival had come at an appalling cost. Encased in his black armor, he now depended upon its systems continuously. Emperor Palpatine had already told him what had supposedly happened to Padmé.

When Vader had first regained consciousness after the reconstruction of his body, his first concern had been her.

"Where is Padmé? Is she safe? Is she all right?"

Palpatine had answered him without hesitation.

"I'm afraid she died. It seems, in your anger, you killed her."

Vader had rejected the claim immediately.

"I couldn't have! She was alive! I felt her! She was alive! It's impossible!"

His anguish had erupted through the Force. Machinery and droids had been crushed around him as he screamed his denial.

But Palpatine's claim remained.

Then, the following day, Bail Organa's broadcast presented an entirely different account.

Vader watched it with Palpatine in an office on Coruscant.

As Bail described Padmé's supposed accident on Mustafar, Vader became still.

The broadcast ended.

Vader turned toward his master.

"You told me that in my anger, I killed her!"

Palpatine showed no surprise.

"Yes, Lord Vader, you did."

Vader's mechanical breathing filled the room.

Palpatine continued calmly.

"Senator Organa is only speaking a lie. There is no truth to his words."

Vader stared at him through the expressionless lenses of his mask.

"But why would he lie?"

"Because he does not want to tell the galaxy the truth," Palpatine replied. "Which is that you killed Senator Amidala."

He allowed the accusation to settle before continuing.

"I suppose the 'accident' narrative could be easier for the public to swallow."

Vader said nothing.

He remembered Mustafar.

Padmé confronting him.

Obi-Wan appearing.

His own fury.

His hand rising.

Padmé suspended helplessly in his grip.

Her body collapsing.

He had known she was alive when Obi-Wan carried her away. He had felt it. Yet much had happened afterward. Vader himself had been mutilated, burned, and left dying beside the lava.

He possessed no evidence of what had happened to Padmé after that.

Bail Organa's account contradicted Palpatine's, but it did not give Vader an answer he could prove false. If Bail wished to spare Padmé's reputation by concealing the true manner of her death, the lie served an obvious purpose.

At last Vader answered.

"...Fine."

Palpatine regarded his apprentice.

"Forget about her, my apprentice. We have much work to do."

Vader turned away.

The subject was closed.

For Palpatine, Padmé Amidala was dead.

For Darth Vader, Padmé Amidala was dead.

For the galaxy, Padmé Amidala was dead.

Beneath the surface of Polis Massa, the woman herself remained unconscious as medical machinery sustained her body in silence.

Meanwhile, Obi-Wan, Yoda, and Bail faced another secret that demanded immediate attention.

Luke and Leia could not remain together.

The children were the offspring of Anakin Skywalker, now Darth Vader. If the Emperor discovered their existence, their lives would be in grave danger.

Aboard Bail Organa's starcruiser, the three men sat around a conference table to decide what would become of them.

Yoda spoke first.

"Hidden, safe, the children must be kept."

Obi-Wan nodded.

"We must take them somewhere the Sith will not sense their presence."

"Split up, they should be."

Bail already knew what he wanted to do.

"My wife and I will take the girl. We've always talked of adopting a baby girl. She will be loved with us."

Obi-Wan looked toward him.

"And what of the boy?"

"To Tatooine," Yoda said. "To his family, send him."

Obi-Wan understood immediately. Anakin's stepbrother, Owen Lars, and Owen's wife Beru lived there as moisture farmers.

"I will take the child and watch over him."

He hesitated, then asked the question that none of them could answer with certainty.

"Master Yoda, do you think Anakin's twins will be able to defeat Darth Sidious?"

Yoda considered the children and the burden now placed upon them merely by being born.

"Strong the Force runs, in the Skywalker line. Hope, we can..."

He lowered his head.

"Done, it is. Until the time is right, disappear we will."

Bail rose to attend to the arrangements.

Before Obi-Wan could follow, Yoda stopped him.

"Master Kenobi, wait a moment."

Obi-Wan turned.

"In your solitude on Tatooine, training I have for you."

"Training?"

"An old friend has learned the path to immortality."

Obi-Wan stared at him.

"Who?"

"One who has returned from the netherworld of the Force to train me... your old Master, Qui-Gon Jinn."

"Qui-Gon?"

Obi-Wan could scarcely believe what he was hearing.

"But how could he accomplish this?"

"The secret of the Ancient Order of the Whills, he studied. How to commune with him, I will teach you."

"I will be able to talk with him?"

"How to join the Force, he will train you. Your consciousness you will retain, when one with the Force. Even your physical self, perhaps."

The revelation gave Obi-Wan something he had scarcely possessed since the destruction of the Jedi Order: a purpose beyond mere survival.

Soon afterward, Bail dealt with another loose end.

He walked through a corridor of the starcruiser with R2-D2 and C-3PO following behind him until he reached Captain Antilles.

"Captain Antilles."

"Yes, Your Highness."

"I'm placing these droids in your care. Treat them well. Clean them up. Have the protocol droid's mind wiped."

C-3PO recoiled.

"Oh, no."

R2-D2 responded with a series of whistles.

C-3PO turned indignantly toward him.

"Oh, it's all very well for you."

Preparations continued as the starcruiser approached Naboo.

In Theed, news of Padmé Amidala's death had brought grief on a planetary scale.

People traveled from across Naboo to the capital. Citizens who remembered her as their young Queen stood beside those who knew her as their senator. Officials, soldiers, family members, attendants, and ordinary residents crowded the streets.

The absence of a body altered everything.

There could be no coffin containing Padmé Amidala. There were no remains to place inside one. According to the account announced by Bail Organa, the lava of Mustafar had destroyed her body instantly.

The people of Naboo therefore gathered for a ceremony of memory rather than burial.

At dawn, crowds filled Theed's great avenues and plazas. Flowers covered memorial displays. Images of Padmé from different periods of her public life appeared throughout the city: the young Queen who had resisted the Trade Federation's occupation, the senator who had represented Naboo in the Galactic Senate, and the woman whose political career had been marked by repeated appeals for diplomacy even as the Republic descended into war.

Sio Bibble walked solemnly among the mourners.

Members of Padmé's family joined the procession. Naboo dignitaries followed. Soldiers stood in ceremonial formation.

There was no covered coffin drawn through the streets.

Instead, the procession moved around an empty place deliberately left at its center, a visible acknowledgment that the woman being mourned could not be physically returned to Naboo.

Citizens bowed their heads as the memorial passed.

Some wept.

Others watched in stunned silence.

The explanation of Padmé's death was extraordinary, but these were extraordinary days. The Clone Wars had ended only to be followed by the destruction of the Jedi and the transformation of the Republic into an Empire. Reports, proclamations, accusations, and tragedies arrived faster than many people could absorb them.

Against that background, the bizarre fatal accident on Mustafar became simply one more terrible event.

No one among the crowds in Theed knew that Padmé's heart was still beating.

Far away, beneath the surface of Polis Massa, monitors continued to record that heartbeat.

The medical bunker remained concealed.

Padmé lay motionless beneath its lights, connected to the systems maintaining her condition. There were no cheering crowds, no Senate sessions, no royal attendants, and no children beside her.

Luke and Leia had already begun separate journeys.

Yoda departed alone.

A small escape pod descended through the mist of Dagobah and disappeared into the swamp-covered world. When the pod landed, the ancient Jedi Master stepped down its ramp and surveyed the wet, unfamiliar terrain that would become his refuge.

The Empire, meanwhile, consolidated its power.

Aboard an Imperial Star Destroyer, Emperor Palpatine stood beside Governor Tarkin. Attack cruisers surrounded the vessel.

Darth Vader walked onto the bridge and joined them.

Beyond the viewport, an immense framework hung in space, the beginnings of a colossal Imperial construction project.

Vader stood silently beside his master.

There was no Padmé Amidala now in the life he believed remained to him.

He had accepted that she was dead, whether by his hand as Palpatine insisted or by the accident Bail Organa had described to the galaxy. The distinction could not restore her.

His mechanical breathing continued steadily as he looked out across the growing structure.

Elsewhere, Bail Organa's starcruiser approached Alderaan.

Bail carried the infant Leia to his wife, Breha Organa, Queen of Alderaan. On a balcony overlooking Alderaan's immense mountains, Breha received the baby into her arms.

She held Leia carefully and rocked her.

The child would grow up as their daughter, protected within the royal household of Alderaan and hidden from the Sith who ruled the galaxy.

Her brother was taken somewhere very different.

Obi-Wan traveled to Tatooine with Luke.

The desert world stretched around him beneath its twin suns as he rode an eopie toward the Lars moisture farm. The infant rested securely with him during the journey.

At the homestead, Beru Lars came forward to meet him.

Obi-Wan dismounted and carefully lifted Luke free.

Beru looked at the child.

"This is him?"

Obi-Wan nodded.

"Yes."

She accepted Luke into her arms.

For a moment Obi-Wan looked down at Anakin Skywalker's son.

Then he turned toward Owen Lars, who waited nearby on the ridge overlooking the homestead.

No lengthy explanation was necessary. The arrangement had been made. Luke would remain with his family.

Beru carried him toward Owen.

Obi-Wan watched them together beneath the fading light.

Then he departed.

Owen and Beru stood with Luke as the twin suns sank toward the horizon, while far beyond Tatooine, Leia rested in the arms of Bail and Breha Organa on Alderaan.

Yoda was hidden on Dagobah.

Obi-Wan would disappear into the deserts of Tatooine.

The Emperor ruled from Coruscant, with Darth Vader at his side.

And beneath the barren surface of Polis Massa, unknown to the Empire and believed dead by the galaxy, Padmé Amidala slept.

The monitors around her continued their quiet rhythm.

Her condition remained stable.

She did not open her eyes.

# Part 1

The second Death Star was already beginning to die.

Deep inside the battle station, Rebel starfighters raced toward its main reactor while the fleet outside pounded Imperial warships and the station itself. Explosions shuddered through corridors and decks. Warning sirens screamed. Imperial personnel abandoned their posts and rushed for transports as the enormous structure trembled around them.

Only minutes earlier, in the Emperor's throne room, Luke Skywalker had defeated Darth Vader in combat but refused Emperor Palpatine's demand that he kill his father and take Vader's place. Luke had thrown away his lightsaber and declared himself a Jedi.

Palpatine had answered by torturing him with Force lightning.

Luke had collapsed beneath the attack while Vader stood beside his master, watching his son writhe in agony. Then Luke had called to him.

"Father, please. Help me."

Vader had looked from Luke to Palpatine and back again.

At last, Anakin Skywalker had chosen.

He seized the Emperor from behind and lifted him bodily into the air.

Palpatine's lightning immediately redirected itself. White-blue bolts poured over Vader's helmet, armor, and life-support systems as he carried his master toward the central shaft. Sparks exploded from his suit. His body convulsed, but he refused to let go.

With one final exertion, Vader hurled Palpatine into the abyss.

The Emperor fell into the depths of the battle station and vanished in a tremendous eruption of energy.

Vader collapsed.

Luke, barely able to move himself, crawled to his father and pulled him away from the edge.

For several moments neither could rise.

Then the Death Star shook again.

Luke understood that if they remained there, both of them would die.

He forced himself upright and began the long struggle toward the docking bay, dragging and carrying the massive armored figure of his father through corridors increasingly filled with smoke, fleeing troops, and the thunder of distant explosions.

By the time they reached the main docking bay, chaos had consumed the station.

Imperial personnel ran in every direction. Shuttles departed as quickly as their crews could launch them. Explosions rocked the vast chamber.

Luke staggered beneath his father's weight.

Finally his strength failed him.

He collapsed.

Vader lay beside him, his breathing ragged and distorted by the damage Palpatine's lightning had inflicted upon his armor.

"Luke..."

Luke moved closer.

"Father?"

"Help me take this mask off."

Luke hesitated.

"But you'll die."

Vader's mechanical breathing faltered.

"Nothing can stop that now. Just for once... let me look on you with my own eyes."

Luke slowly removed the helmet and mask.

For the first time he saw his father's face.

Anakin Skywalker was scarred almost beyond recognition by the injuries he had suffered long ago. His pale features were deeply marked, and his eyes struggled to focus.

But when he looked upon Luke directly, he smiled.

Luke stared down at him.

For all the evil Darth Vader had committed, the man before him had just turned against the Emperor and nearly destroyed himself to save his son.

Anakin spoke weakly.

"Now... go, my son. Leave me."

"No. You're coming with me. I can't leave you here. I've got to save you."

Anakin's faint smile disappeared.

"Thank you, but the lightning has damaged my life-support suit beyond repair. Saving me is pointless. There is nothing other than death for me at this point."

"Don't speak like that, Father."

Luke's voice became firm.

"I WILL save you."

Anakin looked at him with tired resignation.

"You are welcome to try, my son. But I assure you, it won't work."

Luke shook his head.

"Master Yoda once told me: 'Do, or do not. There is no try.'"

Another explosion shook the docking bay.

Luke leaned closer.

"I will not try to save you. I WILL save you."

Anakin stared at him.

"..."

Luke reached for the mask.

"Come on. Let's go."

He carefully fitted Vader's mask and helmet back into place.

The damaged respirator resumed its mechanical rhythm. It was unstable, but it was enough to sustain Anakin for a little longer.

Luke hauled him upright.

Imperial officers and troops were too preoccupied with their own escape to question the sight of Luke Skywalker dragging Darth Vader toward a shuttle.

Step by step, Luke forced them across the docking bay.

They reached an Imperial shuttle.

Luke pulled Anakin aboard, secured him as well as he could, and hurried into the cockpit.

Outside, sections of the station were already erupting.

Luke activated the engines.

The shuttle lifted from the deck and shot through the docking bay just as another violent explosion tore through the surrounding structure.

Elsewhere inside the Death Star, Lando Calrissian and Wedge Antilles completed their attack on the main reactor. The Rebel ships turned and raced through the superstructure, pursued by a chain of explosions that spread rapidly through the station.

Luke's shuttle cleared the battle station and plunged toward the forest moon of Endor.

Behind him, the unfinished Death Star exploded in an immense flash that illuminated the surrounding space.

Luke hardly looked back.

His attention remained on the figure lying behind him.

"Stay with me, Father."

Anakin's damaged respirator answered with an uneven mechanical breath.

The shuttle descended through Endor's atmosphere.

Luke deliberately landed away from the main concentration of Rebel forces.

The forest closed around the craft.

When Luke opened the ramp, the night air entered the shuttle. He helped Anakin outside, moving him only a short distance from the landing site before lowering him against the base of a massive tree.

Anakin's suit hissed intermittently.

Luke crouched beside him.

"Stay here, Father. Save your energy. And try not to be found."

Anakin tilted his helmet slightly toward him.

Luke continued.

"I can't take you into the Rebel camp right now. Everybody hates you."

There was no point pretending otherwise.

Darth Vader had spent decades enforcing the Emperor's will. To the Rebellion, his name represented imprisonment, torture, execution, and terror. Few Rebels would care that he had turned against Palpatine only moments before the Emperor's death.

Luke nevertheless refused to believe that hatred had to be permanent.

"In time, I hope the Rebels can come to accept you."

Anakin was silent for a moment.

Then he said, "I understand."

His breathing mechanism rasped.

"Oh, and now the Empire hates me too."

Luke looked at him.

Anakin continued almost bitterly.

"The Rebels hate me because I am Darth Vader."

Luke immediately corrected him.

"You mean 'was.' You are no longer Darth Vader."

Anakin paused.

"...Right."

The word seemed strange to him.

For more than two decades, Darth Vader had been not merely a name but his entire existence.

Now that existence was over.

"The Rebels hate me because I was Darth Vader," Anakin said. "The Empire hates me because I betrayed them and killed their Emperor, and am now openly defecting against them."

He looked out into the dark forest.

"Where in the galaxy do I belong now?"

Luke answered without hesitation.

"With me."

Anakin turned toward him.

The blank black mask concealed his face, but his reaction was unmistakable. His posture changed, and for several seconds he could find no response.

When he finally spoke, his voice had softened.

"Thank you, my son."

Luke placed a hand against his father's armored shoulder.

"Alright. I will go check on my sister, my friends, and the other Rebels. I promise I will be back."

"Go," Anakin said. "Enjoy your celebration... you've earned it."

Luke hesitated.

Leaving Anakin alone in his condition went against every instinct he had.

But bringing Darth Vader into the middle of a Rebel victory celebration would create immediate chaos, and Luke needed to find Leia and the others.

"I'll come back."

Anakin nodded.

Luke turned and disappeared between the trees.

Anakin watched him go.

Soon Luke reached the gathering of Rebel troops and Ewoks.

Han Solo, Leia Organa, Chewbacca, C-3PO, R2-D2, Lando Calrissian, and the surviving Rebels celebrated the destruction of the Death Star and the death of the Emperor. Han learned that Luke was Leia's brother, and the companions who had fought together through so much embraced one another amid the celebration.

Luke joined them, but his attention repeatedly drifted away from the festivities.

He knew that his father was lying alone somewhere beyond the light of the fires.

At the edge of the celebration, Luke eventually saw two luminous figures standing beyond the gathered Rebels.

Obi-Wan Kenobi and Yoda appeared as shimmering manifestations of the Force.

They watched Luke with quiet approval.

Luke smiled at them.

Far away from the music, laughter, and bonfires, Anakin's condition was worsening.

He remained where Luke had left him, resting against the enormous tree near the hidden Imperial shuttle.

The forest was quiet there.

His damaged suit was not designed to withstand what Palpatine had done to it. Several systems had failed completely. Others functioned only intermittently.

His breathing became increasingly labored.

Anakin tried to draw upon the Force.

For years, his connection to it had been steeped in anger, fear, pain, and hatred. Those emotions had always been readily available to him.

Now he rejected them.

But without them, he felt almost empty.

He had returned to the light, yet he scarcely remembered how to live within it.

His head lowered.

The respirator strained.

Anakin realized Luke's determination might not matter after all.

His son had saved him from the dark side.

But saving his body was another matter.

A weak mechanical breath entered his lungs.

Then another.

The next took longer.

Anakin closed his eyes.

Perhaps Luke would return and find only the armor.

Then, through the stillness, a voice called out.

"Anakin."

His eyes opened.

For an instant he wondered whether he had imagined it.

Then the voice came again.

"Anakin!"

Anakin lifted his head.

A pale blue light had appeared among the trees.

It took the shape of a man in Jedi robes.

Anakin stared.

"Obi-Wan!"

Obi-Wan Kenobi stood before him as a luminous spirit of the Force.

He looked much as Anakin remembered him from the final years of the Clone Wars, before everything between them had been destroyed.

Obi-Wan regarded him for a moment.

Then he smiled.

"Congratulations! You actually returned to the light."

Anakin managed a faint sound that might once have been a laugh.

"I didn't know I could."

His thoughts immediately returned to Luke.

"If it wasn't for Luke..."

Obi-Wan raised an eyebrow.

"Now let's hope you STAY in the light."

Anakin leaned his head back against the tree.

"I'm afraid there's not much staying."

His respirator sputtered.

"I won't be alive for much longer..."

Obi-Wan's expression changed.

"What if I told you that Padmé was alive?"

Anakin froze.

His eyes suddenly opened as wide as they could.

For several seconds even his pain seemed forgotten.

"Pa... Padmé?"

Obi-Wan said nothing.

Anakin stared at him.

"How?"

His voice strengthened with disbelief.

"Everyone said she was dead. I think I've heard at least five different contradictory versions of how she died."

"I assure you," Obi-Wan said, "she's still alive."

Anakin tried to push himself upright.

The movement immediately sent pain through him, but he ignored it.

"Then where is she?"

"Patience, Anakin. In time I will tell you. You are not ready for this information yet."

"I need to know."

"She is in a deep coma, Anakin."

On Polis Massa.

Alive.

"I will still support you whenever you need it, Father."

Anakin looked at him.

"Thank you, Luke."

They turned again toward Padmé.

She remained exactly as she had been when they entered the room, lying peacefully beneath the medical lights while the machines around her continued their patient work.

He finally understood why she had fallen into it.

He also understood why merely abandoning the name Darth Vader had not been enough to wake her.

Throwing Palpatine into the Death Star's reactor shaft had been a choice for the light.

It was not yet a lifetime in the light.

That would have to come next.

There would be no instant restoration of everything he had destroyed. No single heroic act could erase the years he had spent serving the Empire. His redemption would have to be proven slowly, gradually, and through choices made again and again.

He would have to learn once more what it meant to use his strength in service of others rather than domination over them.

He would have to choose what was good for the galaxy even when Luke was not beside him to remind him.

And somewhere at the end of that long road was Padmé.

For the first time in 23 years, Anakin knew that she was alive.

For the first time since Mustafar, he understood that there was something he could do for her that did not involve controlling fate, defeating death, or seizing greater power.

He could change himself.

Anakin looked down at Padmé's sleeping face.

One day, he was now confident, she would open her eyes.

Until then, Anakin Skywalker would begin the long journey of proving his redemption and his commitment to the light side of the Force.

# Part 3

Anakin remained on Polis Massa for some time after learning the truth about Padmé.

"Years of devotion to the light side of the Force, you will require, Anakin."

Something difficult.

Something that placed him in service of others rather than himself.

"If you really want to help, I'm sure there are ways."

Anakin gave him a skeptical look.

"You say that as though they're going to welcome me into a command center tomorrow."

Anakin knew the Imperial military from the inside.

For years he had been close to Palpatine and had operated at the highest levels of Imperial command. He began passing it to Luke.

"How sure are you?"

"Very."

"How do you know?"

Anakin looked at him.

"I approved part of it."

Luke nodded.

"Right."

Every battle fought for the New Republic, every life saved with information he once would have used against them, every mission flown beside people who had once feared his name, and every relationship rebuilt through patience rather than force was another choice.

# Part 4

A few years passed.

By then, the long Galactic Civil War had finally ended. The New Republic secured a decisive victory over the remnants of the Empire, bringing the conflict that had consumed the galaxy for years to a close. Under Chancellor Mon Mothma, the New Republic now governed a reunited galaxy through democratic institutions rather than Imperial rule.

For Anakin Skywalker, the end of the war brought an unexpected question.

What came next?

His first years after returning to the light had been defined largely by the struggle against the Empire. He had supplied the New Republic with Imperial intelligence, flown combat missions, helped defeat the regime he had once served, and slowly earned the trust of people who had every reason to distrust him.

Now the Empire was defeated.

There were no longer Imperial fleet movements for him to reveal or major battles in which his piloting could make the difference.

Anakin realized that if his return to the light was genuine, it could not depend upon the existence of an enemy to fight.

Doing good for the galaxy had to mean more than winning wars.

So he began looking elsewhere.

There were still slaves throughout parts of the galaxy, something Anakin understood more personally than most. He devoted time and resources to freeing them wherever he could.

There were children left without homes or families by years of war. Anakin helped them.

There were communities damaged by the Empire that needed rebuilding, people who had been displaced, and countless smaller injustices that could not be solved from the cockpit of a starfighter.

Anakin began helping where he could.

It was different from being a Jedi General.

Different from being Darth Vader.

Different even from being a New Republic pilot.

Often there was no battle at all.

Sometimes doing what was right simply meant helping someone who had no power to help themselves.

Luke noticed the change.

So did Leia.

Anakin himself could feel it most clearly in the Force.

The light no longer felt like something he had to consciously reach for every moment. Gradually, patiently, it was becoming his natural state again.

He still visited Padmé on Polis Massa whenever possible.

She remained exactly as she had been for decades, asleep beneath the medical lights as the machines around her quietly sustained her body.

Every visit ended the same way.

Anakin would sit beside her.

He would speak.

Padmé would not answer.

But he never lost confidence that Yoda's words had been true.

One day, that confidence became something more.

Anakin was meditating when the Force suddenly shifted around him.

The world disappeared.

He saw Padmé.

She was lying in the underground chamber on Polis Massa.

Then her eyes opened.

She breathed deeply.

She moved.

She sat up.

Anakin stared at her.

"Padmé?"

The vision vanished.

Anakin's eyes opened.

He remained perfectly still for several seconds.

Then he understood.

This was different from hope.

Different from imagination.

The Force had shown him Padmé awakening.

Anakin stood immediately.

"It's time."

He contacted Luke first.

Then Leia.

Then R2-D2 and Threepio.

His message was simple.

"Come with me to Polis Massa. I think Padmé is ready to wake up."

Luke needed no convincing.

Neither did Leia.

R2 reacted with a stream of excited whistles.

Threepio, still possessing no memories of Padmé or his earlier life, was considerably more confused but came nevertheless.

Han remained on Chandrila, the capital of the New Republic, with his and Leia's young son, Ben Solo.

Ben was still a toddler.

Leia kissed her son before leaving.

"I'll be back soon."

Han held Ben securely.

"Go meet your mother."

Leia looked at him.

Those words still felt strange.

Then she left with Luke.

Soon Anakin, Luke, Leia, R2, and Threepio were aboard a ship heading toward Polis Massa.

For most of the journey, Leia was unusually quiet.

Luke noticed.

"You alright?"

Leia looked through the viewport.

"I've spent my whole life knowing almost nothing about her."

Luke nodded.

"I know."

"And now we're just going to walk into a room and she'll be there."

"That's what happened to me."

Leia looked at him.

Luke smiled.

"It's strange."

"That isn't very reassuring."

"I didn't say it was bad."

Eventually Polis Massa appeared ahead.

They landed near the medical facility and entered together.

By now Anakin knew the route.

They passed through the familiar corridors and took the long elevator ride beneath the asteroid's surface.

Leia watched the levels descend.

"So she has really been down here all this time?"

"Yes," Anakin said.

"Since Luke and I were born?"

"Since that night."

Leia became quiet again.

For her, this was the first time she would see Padmé since the moments surrounding her own birth, moments of which she possessed no ordinary conscious memory.

For R2, it was the first time he had seen Padmé since those same days.

For Threepio, it was something stranger.

He had once known Padmé extremely well, serving beside her for years, but his memory wipe had erased all of it. As far as Threepio knew, he was about to meet a woman he had never encountered before.

The elevator stopped.

The group continued through the underground bunker.

Anakin reached Padmé's room and opened the door.

The lights came on.

Padmé lay exactly where she always did.

The medical machinery continued its steady rhythm.

Leia stopped in the doorway.

For several seconds she simply stared.

Luke looked toward his sister but said nothing.

R2 gave a soft whistle.

Threepio tilted his head.

Anakin walked forward.

He stood beside Padmé's bed.

The vision returned to his thoughts.

Her eyes opening.

Her body moving.

Her awakening.

Slowly, Anakin reached out with his mechanical hand.

He touched Padmé's skin.

"Padmé."

Her eyelids moved.

Anakin froze.

Luke stepped forward.

Leia did the same.

# Padmé Extension: Part 2

Soon after returning from Naboo, Padmé began paying much closer attention to the politics of the New Republic.

At first, she did so privately.

She watched livestream broadcasts of Senate sessions from the apartment she shared with Anakin on Chandrila. Sometimes she sat through hours of procedural debate, committee reports, appropriations disputes, trade matters, reconstruction proposals, and arguments over planetary representation.

Anakin quickly discovered that Padmé rarely watched any political discussion passively.

She always had an opinion.

Sometimes several.

One evening, a senator finished a long speech proposing changes to intersystem trade rules.

Padmé stared at the screen.

"That makes absolutely no sense."

Anakin looked up.

"You've been awake for only a short time and you're already arguing with the Senate."

"I'm not arguing with the Senate."

"You've been arguing with the screen for the last ten minutes."

"I'm critiquing policy."

"Very loudly."

Padmé gave him a look.

Anakin returned his attention to what he had been doing.

Padmé continued watching.

She frequently had strong opinions about Chancellor Mon Mothma's leadership as well.

Sometimes she agreed with her.

Sometimes she did not.

Padmé knew Mon personally from years of working together before and during the Clone Wars. That made watching her govern especially strange.

"She's still Mon," Padmé remarked one day.

Anakin looked toward the broadcast.

"Meaning?"

"Meaning she hasn't changed nearly as much as the galaxy has."

"Is that good?"

"Sometimes."

Despite all of her opinions, Padmé deliberately stayed out of politics.

She had only recently returned to public life after nearly three decades in a coma. She had no official position, no Senate seat, and no desire to interfere simply because she had once been influential.

So she mostly kept her thoughts to herself.

Occasionally, she shared them with Anakin.

He usually listened.

Sometimes he agreed.

Sometimes he did not.

Most of the time he wisely avoided debating Senate procedure with a former senator.

That arrangement lasted until one particular broadcast.

Padmé was watching from the apartment when Chancellor Mon Mothma rose before the Senate to announce a major new proposal.

Padmé leaned forward.

Mon began explaining that she was introducing legislation called the Military Disarmament Act.

The MDA would reduce the size of the New Republic's centralized military by 90%.

Padmé's expression changed immediately.

Mon continued.

She argued that the Empire had depended upon a massive central military structure to impose authoritarian rule across the galaxy. The New Republic, she said, had to demonstrate that it would not repeat the same model.

Instead of maintaining enormous centralized armed forces, the Republic should rely far more heavily on local planetary defense forces.

Individual worlds would remain responsible for much of their own protection.

The central government would maintain only a greatly reduced military for limited collective defense.

Mon presented the policy as both practical and symbolic.

A democratic Republic, she argued, should not resemble the Empire it had defeated.

Padmé sat in stunned silence.

Anakin noticed.

"Padmé?"

She did not answer.

Mon continued speaking.

Padmé's expression gradually moved from concern to disbelief.

Finally she said, "No."

Anakin looked at her.

Padmé stood.

"No, no, no."

"What?"

She pointed toward the broadcast.

"This is a terrible idea."

Anakin looked toward Mon Mothma on the screen.

"The military reduction?"

"90%."

Padmé began pacing.

"She wants to reduce the central military by 90%."

"That's what she said."

"Anakin, do you realize what that means?"

He folded his arms.

"I assume you're about to explain."

Padmé turned toward him.

"The Republic before the Clone Wars barely had a centralized military."

Anakin remained silent.

Padmé continued.

"That weakness was exactly what allowed Palpatine to manipulate the crisis."

For Padmé, the Clone Wars did not feel like ancient history.

She had awakened only recently.

From her own subjective perspective, she had served in the Senate during the Clone Wars almost yesterday.

She remembered the fear surrounding the Separatist crisis.

She remembered the debates over creating a Republic military.

She remembered the sudden appearance of the clone army.

She remembered how Palpatine exploited every emergency to accumulate more authority.

And she knew what that clone army eventually became.

"The Republic had no properly established central military structure," Padmé said. "Then a manufactured crisis appeared, suddenly there was an army nobody fully understood, and everyone accepted it because there was no alternative."

Anakin watched her.

"Then Palpatine turned that army against the Jedi."

"Exactly."

Padmé looked back toward the broadcast.

"And now Mon wants to recreate a situation where, if some massive threat appears, the Republic has no central force capable of responding."

"Her argument is that local forces can handle most defense."

Padmé shook her head.

"Local planetary forces cannot defend the Republic as a whole."

Mon continued speaking on the screen.

Padmé listened for another few moments.

"I'm going to the next Senate session."

"You haven't publicly revealed that you're alive."
