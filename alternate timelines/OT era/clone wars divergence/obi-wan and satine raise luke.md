# Part 1

Maul strode onto the landing platform just as the Twilight began to rise.

Obi-Wan Kenobi was at the controls, fighting to bring the battered freighter around while Duchess Satine Kryze braced herself behind him. They had made it out of the prison, survived the pursuit through Sundari, and reached the ship with Maul's warriors only moments behind them. Now the Mandalorian commandos flooded onto the docks around their ruler.

Maul watched the Twilight lift away.

His expression hardened.

"Open fire."

The commandos raised their weapons.

Red bolts and heavier projectiles tore across the landing platform and slammed into the Twilight's hull.

The entire ship lurched.

Satine seized the edge of a bulkhead as alarms screamed through the cockpit.

"Obi-Wan!"

"I know!"

Another barrage struck the ship.

Metal groaned. Sparks erupted from an overhead panel. The Twilight dipped sharply before Obi-Wan wrestled it back under control.

On the platform below, Maul stared upward.

"Destroy it!"

The commandos continued firing.

A projectile struck the Twilight hard along its side.

The impact hurled Satine across the compartment.

She struck the wall with tremendous force and collapsed to the deck.

"Satine!"

Obi-Wan looked back, but another explosion against the hull forced his attention to the controls. Warning lights spread across the console. The ship was taking serious damage, but it was still flying.

Satine moved weakly on the floor.

"Go," she managed.

Obi-Wan gripped the controls.

The Twilight climbed away from Sundari while Mandalorian fire continued to rake its hull. Several shots struck the engines. Another blew apart a section of exterior plating.

Yet the ship refused to die.

Maul watched it accelerate.

For a moment, his certainty disappeared.

Then the Twilight vanished into hyperspace.

Inside the ship, Obi-Wan immediately left the controls and hurried to Satine.

She was conscious, but barely. Her breathing was shallow, and the violent collision with the wall had left her badly injured.

"Satine."

Her eyes opened.

"You got us away."

"Don't move."

"I wasn't planning to."

Despite the pain, there was the faintest hint of dry amusement in her voice.

Obi-Wan examined her as carefully as he could in the damaged ship.

"You've been seriously hurt."

"I had gathered that."

He looked toward the cockpit as another alarm sounded.

"And the Twilight isn't doing much better."

Obi-Wan returned to the controls and checked the ship's systems. The damage inflicted by Maul's commandos was extensive. The hyperdrive had engaged, but the engines were unstable, power was failing across several systems, and there was no realistic chance that the Twilight could remain in hyperspace long enough to reach Coruscant.

He searched the navigational display for somewhere closer.

Ossus.

The remote Outer Rim world was nearby. It was sparsely populated and far removed from the major centers of galactic civilization, but it was neither completely uncivilized nor uninhabited.

More importantly, it was close enough for the damaged Twilight to reach.

Obi-Wan altered course.

"We're not going to make Coruscant."

Satine turned her head toward him.

"Where are we going?"

"Ossus."

"Ossus?"

"It's nearby. The Twilight should hold together long enough to get us there."

"Should?"

Obi-Wan glanced back at her.

"I thought you preferred honesty."

"At the moment, I might make an exception."

The ship shuddered again.

Obi-Wan looked back toward the controls.

"Ossus it is."

The Twilight dropped out of hyperspace above the planet with alarms already sounding.

Obi-Wan guided the damaged freighter toward the surface. Smoke streamed behind it as it descended through the atmosphere. The controls responded sluggishly, and the engines were barely functioning by the time he spotted an open grassy clearing below.

"Brace yourself."

Satine grimaced.

"I believe I've done enough of that for one day."

The Twilight came down hard.

Its landing gear struck the clearing, buckled under the force, and sent the ship skidding through the grass. Obi-Wan fought the controls until the freighter finally ground to a halt.

For several moments, neither of them spoke.

Then Obi-Wan exhaled.

"We've landed."

Satine looked around the tilted interior.

"That is a generous description."

Obi-Wan went to her and helped her carefully to her feet.

"We need shelter."

Satine leaned heavily against him as they left the Twilight behind.

The countryside surrounding the clearing was quiet. There were no troops waiting for them, no crowds, no officials, and no sign that anyone had noticed their arrival. The landscape stretched outward in broad fields and patches of vegetation beneath the Ossus sky.

They walked slowly.

Satine's injuries made every step difficult, and Obi-Wan kept one arm around her whenever she needed support.

Eventually they found a house.

It stood beside an old farm, abandoned and silent. No one appeared when Obi-Wan called out. The property showed signs of long neglect, but the structure itself remained usable.

Satine looked at it.

"I've lived in worse places."

Obi-Wan raised an eyebrow.

"When?"

She gave him a tired look.

"I am injured, Obi-Wan. Allow me my exaggerations."

He helped her inside.

There was little choice but to remain there. The Twilight was badly damaged, Satine was in no condition to continue traveling, and the abandoned farm offered them isolation and shelter.

For the moment, they claimed the property as their own.

Satine began her recovery there.

Only after she had been settled inside the house and had enough time to rest did they speak seriously about what came next.

Satine sat carefully, still in obvious pain, while Obi-Wan remained nearby.

"Mandalore is lost," she said.

Obi-Wan's expression tightened.

"For now."

"No."

She looked directly at him.

"Do not soften it for my sake. Maul controls Mandalore. Almec may sit where Maul tells him to sit and say what Maul tells him to say, but we both know who rules the planet."

"Bo-Katan is still resisting him."

"My sister has fighters. Maul has the capital, the government, and the warriors who sided with him. If I return now, I will not retake Mandalore."

Satine shifted and winced from the pain.

"I will be killed."

Obi-Wan said nothing.

Satine continued.

"Maul wanted me because of you. He knew exactly what he was doing when he captured us. If he learns that I survived, he will come after me again."

"Yes."

"But he doesn't know where we are."

Obi-Wan looked toward the window and the empty countryside beyond it.

"No."

"Does anyone?"

"No one knows we came here."

Satine studied him.

"Then that is an advantage."

Obi-Wan understood immediately.

"As long as Maul doesn't know where you are, he can't reach you."

"And as long as the galaxy does not know where I am, neither can anyone who might accidentally lead him here."

Obi-Wan was silent for a moment.

Then he made his decision.

"No one can know."

Satine held his gaze.

"Not the Jedi?"

"No."

"The Republic?"

"No."

"My sister?"

Obi-Wan hesitated, but only briefly.

"No."

Satine looked down.

"Bo-Katan will believe I'm dead."

"If she knows you're alive, then there is another person Maul can interrogate, follow, deceive, or watch."

"I know."

Obi-Wan stepped closer.

"This only works if the secrecy is absolute. Only you and I can know where you are."

Satine nodded.

"Only you and I."

"You can't contact anyone."

"I understand."

"You cannot involve yourself in galactic affairs."

Satine gave him a faint, weary smile.

"You are ordering the Duchess of Mandalore to become a farmer."

"For the moment, I'm asking Satine to stay alive."

Her expression softened.

Obi-Wan continued.

"At least until you've completely recovered from your injuries."

Satine considered the abandoned house around them.

Then she nodded.

"Very well. I disappear."

"No messages."

"No messages."

"No public appearances."

"That seems achievable on Ossus."

"And no attempt to return to Mandalore until we understand the situation."

Satine's expression became more solemn.

"I agree."

For the first time since their escape, they had a plan.

The galaxy would not know Satine Kryze was hiding on Ossus.

Maul would not know.

The Republic would not know.

The Jedi would not know.

Only Satine and Obi-Wan would know the truth.

Once Satine was safely settled in the house, Obi-Wan returned alone to the Twilight.

The damaged ship still rested in the grassy clearing where it had crash-landed. Its hull was scarred by Mandalorian fire, and several systems were beyond immediate repair.

Obi-Wan searched through it until he found one of its escape pods.

To his surprise, the pod was intact.

He inspected its systems.

Power was available.

Life support functioned.

Navigation functioned.

Its engines worked.

The only problem was that the pod had no hyperdrive.

Obi-Wan stared at it for several moments.

Then he smiled faintly.

"That will do."

He opened a communications channel.

"Anakin."

The response came after a moment.

"Master?"

"I require some assistance."

There was a pause.

"What happened?"

"It's a long story."

"Those are usually the ones where you need me to come get you."

"In this case, yes."

"Where are you?"

"Ossus."

Another pause.

"Ossus?"

"The Twilight was damaged."

"How badly?"

Obi-Wan looked around the wrecked interior.

"Badly enough that I would prefer not to attempt another journey in it."

"Send me your coordinates."

Obi-Wan looked at the planetary map.

He deliberately chose a random coordinate approximately halfway around Ossus from the farm and the damaged Twilight.

He transmitted it.

"There."

"I'll come get you."

"Thank you, Anakin."

"Try not to crash anything else before I get there."

"I'll make no promises."

The channel closed.

Obi-Wan looked back toward the direction of the farm.

If Anakin came anywhere near the Twilight, there was a chance he might discover Satine. The crash site was too close to the abandoned house.

That could not happen.

Obi-Wan entered the escape pod.

The pod launched from the Twilight and rose into the sky.

Without a hyperdrive, it could not leave the system in any useful way, but it was perfectly capable of traveling across Ossus itself. Obi-Wan flew around the planet toward the coordinates he had given Anakin, putting half a world between Anakin's arrival point and the place where Satine was hidden.

By the time he reached the designated location, all Obi-Wan had to do was wait.

Eventually Anakin arrived.

When Obi-Wan saw his former Padawan's ship coming down, he felt a measure of relief.

Anakin emerged soon afterward and immediately looked at Obi-Wan.

"You look terrible."

"Thank you."

"I'm serious."

"So am I."

Anakin's attention shifted toward the escape pod.

"Where did that come from?"

"The Twilight."

"I thought you said the Twilight was damaged."

"It is."

Anakin looked from Obi-Wan to the pod.

"So why are you here?"

Obi-Wan adopted the casual tone he had prepared.

"I was bored."

Anakin stared at him.

"Bored."

"Quite."

"You were stranded on Ossus."

"Yes."

"With a damaged ship."

"Yes."

"And because you were bored, you launched an escape pod."

"It has no hyperspace capabilities. I couldn't leave Ossus with it, so I decided to explore the planet while I waited for you."

Anakin folded his arms.

"You explored Ossus in an escape pod."

"I had time."

"And these coordinates?"

"Random."

"Random?"

"I simply selected a location and made certain I reached it before you did."

Anakin looked at him for another moment.

Obi-Wan maintained an expression of perfect innocence.

Finally Anakin shook his head.

"You really need a hobby."

"I thought that was what I had just described."

Anakin gave him an exasperated look and turned toward his ship.

"Come on. The Council is going to want to know what happened on Mandalore."

Obi-Wan followed him.

He had been prepared for Anakin to ask where the damaged Twilight was.

He had been prepared for Anakin to insist on seeing the ship.

He had been prepared to improvise an explanation if Anakin wanted to recover it.

But Anakin did none of those things.

His priority was simple: get Obi-Wan off Ossus and back to Coruscant as quickly as possible.

Obi-Wan concealed his relief.

They left the escape pod behind and departed Ossus.

Only when the planet was far behind them did Anakin finally ask the question Obi-Wan had known was coming.

"What happened to Satine?"

Obi-Wan went very still.

He had already decided what he would say.

Anakin looked over at him.

"Obi-Wan?"

Obi-Wan lowered his eyes.

"Maul's commandos fired on the Twilight as we escaped."

"I know you said the ship was damaged."

"It was hit repeatedly."

Anakin waited.

Obi-Wan continued quietly.

"Satine was seriously injured during the attack."

Anakin's expression changed.

"How serious?"

Obi-Wan allowed several seconds to pass before answering.

"She died from her injuries while we were en route to Ossus."

Anakin stared at him.

"I'm sorry."

Obi-Wan did not answer immediately.

The grief he showed required no deception. Satine was alive, but he had left her injured and alone on an isolated world, hidden from everyone she knew. Mandalore remained in Maul's hands. And from that moment onward, Obi-Wan would have to behave before the entire galaxy as though Satine Kryze were dead.

Eventually he said, "Thank you."

Anakin did not question the story further.

When they reached Coruscant, Obi-Wan repeated the account publicly.

The Duchess of Mandalore, he announced, had escaped Maul's forces with him aboard the Twilight. Maul's commandos had opened fire on the vessel during their escape. Their projectiles had inflicted severe damage on the ship and had seriously injured Satine. Although Obi-Wan had managed to escape Mandalore and reach Ossus, Satine had died from those injuries during the journey.

There was no reason for the galaxy to doubt Obi-Wan Kenobi.

The Jedi knew him as a man of discipline and integrity.

The Republic knew him as one of its most prominent generals.

Those who had followed events on Mandalore knew that Maul's forces had seized control of the planet and that Obi-Wan had gone there alone.

The account was believed.

Word spread that Duchess Satine Kryze was dead.

Eventually, it reached Mandalore.

Maul received the news with satisfaction.

"So," he said, "she died after all."

One of his Mandalorian warriors stood nearby.

"The reports say Kenobi escaped, but the Duchess succumbed to the injuries she suffered during our attack on his ship."

Maul's mouth curled into a smile.

"Kenobi escaped."

He sounded almost pleased by that as well.

His gaze became distant as he remembered Naboo and the Jedi who had cut him down there 13 years ago.

For 13 years, hatred of Obi-Wan Kenobi had endured within him.

Now Maul believed that the debt had finally been paid.

"He took everything from me," Maul said.

His smile widened.

"And now I have taken her from him."

The fact that Satine had died only after escaping Mandalore did nothing to diminish his satisfaction. His commandos had inflicted the wounds. His attack had caused her death. As far as Maul was concerned, the result belonged to him.

At last, he believed he had exacted his revenge upon Obi-Wan Kenobi for what had happened on Naboo 13 years ago.

Far away on Ossus, however, Satine Kryze remained alive.

She recovered in silence inside an abandoned house on an isolated farm, hidden from Maul, hidden from Mandalore, hidden from the Republic, and hidden even from Obi-Wan's closest friends.

To the galaxy, the Duchess of Mandalore was dead.

Only Satine and Obi-Wan knew otherwise.

# Part 2

Polis Massa had become a place of endings.

The Jedi Order had been shattered. The Republic had fallen. Padmé Amidala was dead after giving birth to twins. Yoda, Bail Organa, and Obi-Wan Kenobi stood together in the aftermath, speaking quietly about what was to become of the children.

Leia would go with Bail to Alderaan.

Then Obi-Wan looked toward the infant boy.

"And what of the boy?"

Yoda leaned on his cane.

"To Tatooine. To his family, send him."

Obi-Wan's expression tightened.

"No, we don't know his family on Tatooine very well. I propose that I will raise the boy myself."

Yoda's eyes narrowed.

"Master Kenobi, fit to raise the boy yourself, you are not. Good for the boy, a single parent is not."

Obi-Wan answered immediately.

"I will not raise him as a single parent."

Yoda stared at him.

There was suspicion in his face now.

Obi-Wan did not elaborate.

For several long moments, neither of them spoke.

Too much had happened in too little time. The Jedi were nearly gone. The Republic had transformed into an Empire. Friends had become enemies. Entire worlds were changing allegiance or falling silent. Yoda himself seemed smaller than he had only days before, worn down by grief, defeat, and exhaustion.

At last, he sighed.

"...Fine. Take the boy, you may. Raise him, you may. A good job, please do, and keep him safe. May the Force be with you."

Obi-Wan bowed his head.

"And may the Force be with you as well, Master Yoda."

Not long afterward, Obi-Wan departed Polis Massa with Luke.

He did not fly toward Tatooine.

He went to Ossus.

The journey gave him too much time to think.

Luke slept for much of it, entirely unaware of the ruin that surrounded his birth. Obi-Wan looked at him often during the trip, wondering what the boy would one day understand about Anakin, about Padmé, about the Jedi, and about the burden that might eventually fall upon him.

For now, though, Luke was simply an infant.

That was enough.

When Obi-Wan finally descended through the atmosphere of Ossus, the countryside below looked almost exactly as he remembered it.

The farm remained isolated.

The grassy clearing remained quiet.

He brought the ship down beside the property and stepped outside with Luke cradled carefully in his arms.

For the first time in what felt like an eternity, the world around him was peaceful.

He walked toward the house.

The door opened.

Satine stepped outside.

She looked perfectly healthy.

Whatever lingering effects remained from the injuries she had suffered escaping Mandalore were gone. She crossed the distance quickly, her face lighting up the moment she saw him.

"Obi-Wan! You are back!"

Obi-Wan smiled faintly.

"For good this time, I think."

Satine's expression shifted immediately.

"What happened?"

Obi-Wan hesitated.

There were too many answers to that question.

Before he could decide where to begin, Satine noticed the child in his arms.

She pointed at Luke.

"Who is this?"

Obi-Wan looked down at the infant.

"This is Luke. We are going to adopt him."

Satine blinked.

"Wait, why? Who are his parents? What happened to them?"

"Long story. We'll talk about it later."

He looked at her more carefully.

"First of all, how are you feeling? Still any pain?"

Satine shook her head.

"I think I have fully recovered. Turns out a long time in the countryside is good for your health."

"Great."

Obi-Wan glanced toward the house.

"So have you heard the galactic news about the Republic?"

Satine paused.

"...No. I thought you asked me not to follow galactic affairs."

"I said don't involve yourself in galactic affairs. Not live under a rock."

"I figured that it's easier for me to not involve myself if I don't know at all."

Obi-Wan considered that.

"Fair enough."

They went inside.

Luke was placed in a comfortable position on a bed, carefully supported so he could sleep.

For a while, Obi-Wan and Satine said little.

The reunion itself needed time.

So did everything Obi-Wan had brought with him.

Later, once both of them had rested and the house had gone quiet, they sat down together.

Satine looked at him.

"All right."

Obi-Wan raised an eyebrow.

"All right?"

"You said it was a long story."

"It is."

"I have nowhere else to be."

Obi-Wan leaned back.

"Very well."

He took a breath.

"So, as it turns out, Chancellor Palpatine is a Sith Lord."

Satine stared at him.

"WHAT?! Like Maul?"

"Yes, but far more powerful."

Satine looked genuinely stunned.

"What did he do?"

Obi-Wan let out a tired breath.

"Oh, far too many things."

He looked down for a moment.

"He somehow got all the clone troopers to turn against the Jedi. He killed nearly everyone in the Jedi Order. Even most of the Jedi Council is now dead."

Satine's expression fell.

"And the Republic?"

"It's gone."

She went very still.

Obi-Wan continued.

"He turned it into an Empire with himself as the Emperor. It's no longer a democracy."

Satine whispered, "Oh no..."

"And worst of all, he managed to turn Anakin to the dark side."

Satine looked at him sharply.

"Anakin Skywalker?"

"Yes. The very same Anakin Skywalker."

She shook her head slowly.

"And what did he do after turning to the dark side?"

Obi-Wan's voice became quieter.

"He stormed the Jedi Temple and killed everyone there."

Satine said nothing.

"Then he went to Mustafar. I also went to Mustafar, hoping to stop him."

"Did you?"

Obi-Wan looked away.

"I think so."

Satine waited.

"We duelled, and I won. I left him burning by the shore of a lava river. He almost certainly died from the fire."

Satine's face tightened with horror.

"That is so terrible..."

Obi-Wan nodded once.

For several moments, the only sound in the house came from Luke breathing softly nearby.

Then Obi-Wan looked toward him.

"The boy I brought here, Luke, is the child of Anakin Skywalker and Senator Padmé Amidala."

Satine's eyes widened again.

"Anakin was romantically involved with Senator Amidala? I did not know that..."

"I also didn't know that for sure until very recently, although I had my fair share of suspicions for quite a while now."

"What happened to her?"

Obi-Wan's expression became somber.

"She died during childbirth. It was terrible."

Satine looked toward Luke.

"So Luke is an orphan?"

"I believe so, yes."

She was quiet for a while.

Then she looked back at Obi-Wan.

"If we're going to adopt him seriously, I think maybe we should get married."

Obi-Wan stared at her.

Then a faint smile appeared.

"You're just using this as an excuse to get married, aren't you?"

Satine's expression became almost innocent.

"Maybe..."

Obi-Wan shook his head.

"Well, I suppose why not. There's nobody else around here. So I don't think we need any witnesses. We can just declare ourselves married if that's what you had in mind."

Satine considered that.

"Sure, I guess..."

She held out her hand.

Obi-Wan looked at it.

Then he shook it.

Satine nodded solemnly.

"Okay, we are married now."

Obi-Wan looked down at their joined hands.

"...That is the strangest wedding ceremony I have ever witnessed."

Satine smiled.

"It was efficient."

"I'll give you that."

A few days later, after Luke had settled into the rhythms of the house and both Obi-Wan and Satine had begun adjusting to the reality of caring for him, they returned to the question of his future.

Satine was holding Luke when she asked, "What should we name him? Luke Kryze Kenobi?"

Obi-Wan thought about it.

"...No. I believe he should remain Luke Skywalker."

Satine frowned.

"Why? Are we not going to be his parents?"

"Functionally, yes. But..."

"But?"

Obi-Wan hesitated.

Satine looked down at Luke.

"Is he not going to call us 'mom' and 'dad'?"

"I think I want him to call us 'aunt' and 'uncle'."

Satine looked up.

"Why?"

"Because there's something important about the Skywalker name."

She waited.

"I don't think we should hide from him who his real parents are."

Satine opened her mouth.

"But..."

Obi-Wan raised a hand.

"We are guardians, Satine. Master Yoda gave the boy to me specifically so I can protect and watch over him."

He looked toward Luke.

"Because this is not just any child."

Satine's expression grew more skeptical.

Obi-Wan continued.

"This is the child that may be the last hope for the Jedi. The only one who may eventually be able to destroy the Sith and the Empire."

Satine stared at him.

"Why?"

"There's something special about the Skywalker bloodline. They are said to be the descendants of the Force itself, and Anakin is prophecized to be the Chosen One."

Satine's skepticism deepened.

"Palpatine is extraordinarily powerful," Obi-Wan continued. "Maul is nothing compared to him. We need someone like Luke to destroy him. And so Luke should know about this."

Satine looked at him for several moments.

"How much faith do you have in this prophecy?"

Obi-Wan was silent.

Then he answered with unusual bluntness.

"Honestly, given what Anakin has done recently, not much."

Satine raised an eyebrow.

"But still," Obi-Wan said, "this is our only hope. The Jedi are all but eradicated. Those who are still alive are all in hiding, like me."

He looked back toward Luke.

"Even if there's a sliver of truth about the prophecy... we must cling onto it if we are to hope to destroy Palpatine one day."

Satine rolled her eyes.

She was clearly unconvinced.

Prophecies, bloodlines descended from the Force, Chosen Ones, and destinies tied to ancient Jedi beliefs were not subjects in which she had much experience. Matters concerning the Force were Obi-Wan's specialty, not hers.

She decided not to argue.

Instead, she asked, "What do we tell Luke when he grows up?"

Obi-Wan looked at her.

"About Anakin?"

Satine nodded.

Obi-Wan thought for a moment.

"I don't think he should get the full truth just yet."

Satine waited.

"'He fell to the dark side, and so I had to kill him on Mustafar' is probably too much for him to handle."

"Probably."

"We'll figure it out eventually."

Obi-Wan looked toward Luke again.

"Let's not worry about this for now."

Satine nodded.

"Sounds good."

And so their life on Ossus began in earnest.

Obi-Wan Kenobi, former Jedi General and one of the few surviving members of the Order, disappeared from the affairs of the galaxy.

Satine Kryze, believed dead by Mandalore and everyone who had once known her publicly, remained hidden on the remote farm.

Together, now declaring themselves husband and wife in the most informal ceremony either of them could have imagined, they became the guardians of Luke Skywalker.

To Luke, in time, they would be Aunt Satine and Uncle Obi-Wan.

To themselves, they were a family.

And on a quiet farm far from the new Empire, they began the long work of raising him.

# Part 3

Life on Ossus settled into a quiet rhythm.

For a time, Obi-Wan and Satine allowed themselves to focus on the small realities of the farm, the house, and Luke. Beyond their isolated home, however, the galaxy continued to change under the new Empire.

One day, Obi-Wan and Satine tuned into the HoloNet News.

Emperor Palpatine appeared in the broadcast, speaking before the galaxy with the same deliberate authority he had once used as Chancellor. Beside him stood a tall, masked figure dressed entirely in black.

Palpatine announced that he was sending his trusted officer, Lord Vader, to handle unrest on a rebellious world in the Mid Rim.

Obi-Wan froze.

Lord Vader.

The name immediately dragged him back to the Jedi Temple on Coruscant, to the security recordings he had watched beside Yoda after the Temple massacre.

He remembered seeing Anakin slaughter Jedi.

He remembered Anakin kneeling before Darth Sidious.

He remembered Sidious praising his new apprentice.

And he remembered the words that had followed.

"Now, Lord Vader..."

Later, Yoda had confirmed what Obi-Wan had been unable to accept.

The young man he had trained had been consumed by Darth Vader.

Obi-Wan stared at the masked figure beside Palpatine.

Then he shut off the HoloNet.

"No, it can't be..."

Satine looked at him.

"What?"

Obi-Wan remained motionless for several seconds.

"Anakin survived Mustafar."

Satine's expression changed immediately.

"How do you know?"

"That masked man in the news... that's him. Lord Vader. Darth Vader is Anakin's Sith name."

Satine looked toward the now-dark HoloNet projector.

"Why is he in that suit?"

"Probably because of all the damage I did to him on Mustafar."

Obi-Wan's face hardened.

"I'm surprised he survived those injuries at all."

Satine sat quietly for a moment.

"What now?"

"Well, we hope that he doesn't find us."

Her eyes shifted toward Luke.

"And Luke?"

Obi-Wan answered at once.

"Oh, I'm absolutely not telling Luke that Anakin Skywalker and Darth Vader are the same person."

Satine frowned.

"You're not?"

"At least with a different name and a different appearance, I can make it believable."

"What exactly do you want to tell him?"

Obi-Wan thought for a while.

Finally, he said, "I will still tell him that his real father is Anakin Skywalker. I will tell him that he was a Jedi Knight and a good friend of mine. Then I will tell him that Darth Vader killed him."

Satine stared at him.

"So you want to lie to his face?"

"Trust me, it's for his own good."

"I don't think so."

"Oh well."

Satine folded her arms.

"Fine. If he ever finds out the truth, don't expect me to help you."

"Fair enough."

They left the matter there.

# Part 4

"How else are we going to defeat the Empire?"

Very cautious.

"I will."

"I promise."

# Part 5

As Luke slowly grew up on Ossus, the quiet farm became the center of his entire world.

To him, Obi-Wan and Satine were simply Uncle Obi-Wan and Aunt Satine. The distant Empire was something he heard about in fragments. Mandalore was a world from Satine's stories. The Jedi Order and Galactic Republic belonged to a vanished age that Luke had never personally known.

"Uncle Obi-Wan, can you tell me more about Dad?"

"Is he good at using the Force?"

"So he was your apprentice?"

"Yes. In the Jedi Order we call that 'Padawan'."

Luke thought about that for a moment.

"Did he have his own Padawan?"

"Yes. Her name is Ahsoka Tano. She's very powerful too."

Luke leaned closer.

"So what happened to him?"

Obi-Wan paused.

He remembered the decision he had made years earlier.

He remembered telling Satine exactly what story he intended to give Luke.

Satine, sitting nearby, noticed his hesitation.

Obi-Wan finally answered.

"A Jedi Knight fell to the dark side of the Force and became Darth Vader. Then he killed your father."

Luke's face fell.

"Oh no."

Then he looked back at Obi-Wan.

"Did you know Darth Vader before that?"

Obi-Wan answered automatically.

"Oh yes, he was my Padawan."

Silence.

Obi-Wan realized what he had just said.

"Wait, he was your Padawan too? So you had two Padawans?"

"...Yes. I had two Padawans."

"Do you want to hear about your mother?"

"Yes, please! Tell me all about Mom!"

"What happened to her?"

"She died when she gave birth to you."

Obi-Wan nearly continued.

He almost said, "and your sister."

"Why?"

"Uh... childbirth is medically risky, you know."

"Uh, okay..."

Then he looked back at Obi-Wan.

"What about you, Uncle?"

Luke looked unimpressed.

"That doesn't sound very interesting..."

Obi-Wan raised an eyebrow.

"It's more interesting than it sounds when I say it."

Luke waited.

"You see, the Jedi Temple contains all kinds of different people of all kinds of different species. You get to learn a lot of new things and explore a lot of new planets all the time."

Obi-Wan smiled faintly.

"And you get to help people."

Luke seemed more interested now.

"Who trained you?"

"Master Qui-Gon Jinn. He was a great Jedi. One of the smartest Jedi Masters I have ever known."

Obi-Wan's expression became more solemn.

"He was killed by a Sith Lord named Darth Maul."

"Why do so many Jedi get killed?!"

Obi-Wan paused.

"The Jedi life is a dangerous one, Luke. Being killed is an expected and accepted part of the Jedi way."

"Oh no..."

"Anyway, what about Aunt Satine?"

Obi-Wan smiled.

"Maybe you should ask her yourself."

"Aunt Satine, can you tell me about yourself?"

"I am a Mandalorian. I became the Duchess of Mandalore."

"Does that mean you are the leader of Mandalore?"

"Why are you not the leader of Mandalore anymore?"

Luke immediately answered, "Maybe one day we can be strong enough."

Satine smiled sadly.

"Maybe. But it will take a while."

"I can help!"

Satine chuckled.

"Well, thanks. But you're 5 years old."

Luke straightened proudly.

"When I grow up, I can help!"

"When you grow up, we will see."

"Can you tell me about the Jedi Order?"

"The Jedi Order is an ancient order dedicated to service to the galaxy and connection to the light side of the Force."

Luke stared at him.

"Wow, so complicated. Can you make it easier to understand?"

"The Jedi are people that try to do good things and help other people."

"Okay. But why are only people who can use the Force accepted into the Jedi Order?"

"Because the Jedi are not just any good people. We help people in a very specific way, by using the Force. We use our connection to the Force to do good things that ordinary people can't do."

Luke thought about that.

"Why are there so few Jedi now?"

Obi-Wan's expression became more serious.

"Because the Empire destroyed most of us. Only a few of us are left, and we are all hiding on different worlds."

"Exactly."

Luke nodded slowly.

"Can you tell me about the Republic as well?"

"How do you remove a Chancellor?"

Obi-Wan paused.

"But that's too complicated for you to understand right now."

Luke shrugged.

"Okay then."

"But not every world was in the Republic. For example, Mandalore was not. It was an independent world outside of the Republic."

"Mandalore was once an enemy of the Republic in ancient times. But we eventually got along better. Even then, we still didn't join the Republic."

"Why not?"

"And besides, the Republic was actually deeply flawed."

"What do all of these big words mean?"

"Never mind for now. I will explain these to you when you're a bit older."

"Okay."

"You should go to sleep now, Luke. It's late."

"Alright."

The first thing Obi-Wan gave him was a lightsaber.

Not just any lightsaber.

Anakin Skywalker's.

Now he placed it into Luke's hands.

Luke stared at the hilt.

"This was Dad's?"

"Yes."

Luke held it carefully.

Obi-Wan watched him for a long moment.

"Treat it with respect."

"I will."

He began with meditation.

Luke learned to sit quietly, calm his thoughts, and reach outward with his senses.

Obi-Wan would ask, "What do you feel?"

Luke would answer, "I don't know."

So Obi-Wan taught him to stop searching for a specific sensation.

"Don't force it," Obi-Wan said. "Listen."

"To what?"

"Everything."

Luke frowned.

"That's not very helpful."

"It will be."

Slowly, Luke began to understand.

He learned to sense movement without relying entirely on sight.

He learned to recognize another person's presence through the Force.

He learned to feel changes around him before they became obvious.

Force sensing led naturally into precognition.

Obi-Wan would test him with sudden movements or small objects, teaching Luke to react to what the Force warned him was coming.

Then came telekinesis.

At first, Luke practiced with tiny objects.

A small tool.

A stone.

When one falls, they truly become consumed so thoroughly by the dark side that their previous light-side identity no longer exists. To the Force, that identity is completely gone."

Luke studied him.

"Completely gone? Do you not believe that someone who fell to the dark side can be redeemed?"

Obi-Wan hesitated.

"I can't say for certain, but at least in Vader's case, I don't have high hopes for redemption."

Luke thought about that for a moment.

Then his expression became determined.

"Then I shall prove you wrong!"

Obi-Wan raised an eyebrow.

"And how are you going to try?"

Luke paused.

"...I don't know yet. We shall see."

Obi-Wan sighed.

"In any case, Luke, think about what I said. In time I hope that you will accept it."

The next time the three of them sat down together, Luke was calmer.

"I have thought about what you said for a long time now. I think I can accept it for now."

Obi-Wan looked relieved.

"Thank you, Luke. I really hoped that this would not have come between us."

Satine looked at Luke.

"Luke, you're much more forgiving than I would have been. I respect that a lot."

"Okay, Uncle, you better not have any other secrets that you kept from me. I'm giving you this one chance to reveal anything else you know that I don't. I promise I won't get mad at you if you say it now. But this is your only chance."

Obi-Wan considered that for a long moment.

Then he realized he was truly cornered.

He sighed reluctantly.

"Luke... Princess Leia Organa is your twin sister."

Luke stared at him in absolute shock.

"I have a twin sister?!"

"Your mother gave birth to twins, Luke."

Luke's expression tightened.

"And you kept this from me for all these years?"

Obi-Wan nodded.

Luke felt anger rise inside him again.

Then he remembered the promise he had just made.

He forced himself to calm down.

"So when I told you that I thought Leia might be Force-sensitive..."

Obi-Wan nodded.

"I already knew. I had to pretend to be surprised at your discovery."

Luke sighed.

"Wow, very convincing acting, Uncle."

Then Obi-Wan noticed something else.

Satine had gone unusually quiet.

A familiar feeling of danger settled over him.

"Satine, what's wrong?"

She looked directly at him.

"I did not know that Luke had a twin sister. You didn't tell me either."

Obi-Wan shifted uncomfortably.

"I know. This is not a secret I was supposed to tell other people."

Satine's expression sharpened.

"So 'other people' includes your own wife too?"

Obi-Wan hesitated.

"I... guess so."

Satine raised her voice.

"You hid the fact that your adopted son has a twin sister not only from him, but from your own wife as well?! Obi-Wan, I think you need to rethink some things."

Obi-Wan lowered his head slightly.

"...I'm sorry, Satine."

She was not finished.

"Besides. You deliberately kept Luke separated from his sister for his entire childhood? Why?! Don't you think Luke deserves to grow up alongside her?"

Obi-Wan looked increasingly defensive.

"...Don't get mad at me. It was Master Yoda who came up with the idea of separating the twins. He said it's to protect the twins and ensure that the Empire and the Sith cannot find them. I simply went along with his plan. So if you are still going to be mad, be mad at Master Yoda instead."

Satine stared at him.

"Master Yoda. The Grand Master of a Force religion that prohibits marriage and discourages attachments. What does he know about family? What does he know about how to raise kids and what's good for them? Of course Yoda would want to separate the twins. I bet he has zero understanding of the importance of sibling bonds!"

Obi-Wan winced.

"Satine..."

"No. You should have stood up to Yoda. You should have told him that you will not let the twins be separated. You should have brought back both Luke and Leia here, and we could have raised them together."

Obi-Wan stared at her in disbelief.

"You want me to stand up to Master Yoda?! You want me to directly defy a superior in the Jedi Order?"

"This is not about Jedi hierarchy, Obi-Wan. This is about common sense. I fail to see how raising both Luke and Leia together here on Ossus would impact their safety."

Obi-Wan shook his head.

"Well, if Yoda believes this is safer, he must have his reasons."

"Or he could have just invented that reason. Protection is not his true motive."

Obi-Wan frowned.

"Then what is?"

"Preventing Luke and Leia from becoming attached to each other. Yoda fears attachments, Obi-Wan. He likely saw this as a convenient way to ensure that no attachment could ever form between the twins."

Obi-Wan immediately rejected the idea.

"I don't believe this. Yoda doesn't seem like the type to invent a reason and hide his true motives."

Luke finally cut in.

"Alright, you two. You are being ridiculous right now. Please cut it out. I'm not even mad at Uncle Obi-Wan for keeping my sister from me. Maybe you should stop arguing about it. It's not worth it."

Obi-Wan and Satine both turned and stared at him.

A long silence followed.

At last, Satine relented.

"Alright then. So be it."

Obi-Wan exhaled.

"Thank you."

Luke looked between them.

"What happened in the past already happened. Let's just focus on keeping ourselves happy and safe, okay?"

Obi-Wan nodded.

Satine hesitated, then nodded as well.

The conversation ended there.

# Part 8

After a few more days at the home on Ossus, Obi-Wan approached Luke with a suggestion.

"Master Yoda is getting old, Luke. Maybe you should pay him a visit on Dagobah. You haven't been there before, right?"

"Right. Maybe I should check it out."

"Let me know how Master Yoda is doing."

Luke departed Ossus soon afterward.

A few days later, Obi-Wan received a transmission from him.

"Luke. What's up?"

"Master Yoda... he just became one with the Force."

Obi-Wan lowered his gaze.

"Ah. I guess the time has come."

"I spent some time with him before that. We talked a lot."

Obi-Wan looked at him with pride and seriousness in equal measure.

"Luke, you will be the future of the Jedi. This is a great responsibility. Be careful, but I know you can do it."

Luke nodded.

"Thank you, Uncle."

About a year passed before Luke finally returned to Ossus.

This time, he arrived with news that immediately changed the atmosphere in the farmhouse.

"Great news! I think this war may finally be in our favour now. The Emperor is dead, the second Death Star has been destroyed, and Vader died shortly after he was redeemed."

"Vader was redeemed? What happened?"

Luke sat down.

"Turns out attachments may truly be a good thing. He was too attached to me as a son, and he couldn't stand watching Palpatine cast Force lightning at me. So he decided to betray Palpatine, kill him, and return to the light."

Then he asked, "How did he die, then?"

"Palpatine redirected his lightning onto Vader as soon as he realized Vader was turning on him. And that lightning damaged his life support suit which caused him to die."

"I see."

Luke continued.

"I think I should go back to the Rebels soon. The war is not over yet. We still have much work to do."

Satine immediately shook her head.

"Luke, you should rest for a while first. You have fought really hard for years now. You deserve a break."

Obi-Wan added, "And maybe start thinking about rebuilding the Jedi Order now. The war may still last for a long time. Perhaps you don't need to wait until it's finished before honouring Yoda's final request."

Luke thought about that.

"Hmm. You have a good point. I will think about it."

Over the next few days, Luke wrestled with the decision.

Part of him wanted to return to the war immediately. The Empire still existed, and the Rebellion still had work to do.

But Yoda's final request also weighed heavily on him.

In the end, Luke decided to take the first steps toward rebuilding the Jedi.

He and Obi-Wan eventually sat down to discuss what that might look like.

"I'm thinking about building my new Jedi Temple right here on Ossus," Luke said. "After all, this world is historically important for the Jedi and contains lots of Jedi knowledge."

Obi-Wan nodded.

"Good idea. I agree. But you can't just have a temple. You need students. Do you have anyone in mind?"

"Leia."

Obi-Wan listened.

"I've worked with her for many years by this point, and I think she will be a great Jedi. But she has been so dedicated to the Rebellion and to the war that I haven't been able to get her to agree to Jedi training from me so far. But now that Palpatine and Vader are dead and the second Death Star is gone, maybe I can try to see if she has changed her mind."

Obi-Wan considered that.

"If you want, I can help you rebuild the Jedi."

Luke smiled, but shook his head.

"Thank you, Uncle, but I think this should be my own task. After all, it was me specifically who Yoda gave his final request to. I will ask you for help if I ever need it, but I think I can handle this myself for the most part."

Obi-Wan thought about that for a moment.

Luke was no longer the child he had once trained at the farm. He had survived years of war, faced Vader, helped destroy both Death Stars, and watched the old Jedi Order pass fully into history.

At last, Obi-Wan nodded.

"Alright then. I'll be available whenever you need me."

Luke began scouting Ossus for a suitable location.

He eventually chose an area near several previously discovered stashes of ancient Jedi archives, close enough to the old repositories of knowledge to make them useful to the new Order.

Over several months, he constructed the temple.

When it was finally complete, Luke left Ossus and went to find Leia.

Once he did, he made another attempt to persuade her.

"Leia, I know you have been resisting this for a while already, but I truly think it's time now."

Leia gave him a knowing look.

"Let me guess, this is about Jedi training?"

"Exactly. Look, the Emperor is dead. Vader is dead. The second Death Star is destroyed. Maybe we can slow down a little and get you trained in the Force now."

Leia frowned.

"But the war is still raging! The Rebellion still needs me!"

"I know, Leia. But I think knowing the Force can actually help you even within the context of this war. You will be a better combatant. You will be wiser. And you will become an even better leader."

"You say this like it's so easy for me to just up and leave the Rebellion behind..."

"I understand that Jedi training would require some commitment from you. And yes, I admit that it would probably require you to spend some time away from the Rebellion. But trust me, it will be worth it."

Leia sighed.

"Let me think about it."

Luke nodded.

"I have finished constructing a new Jedi Temple on Ossus. It's all completed now. It's ready for you already. All you need to do is come with me to Ossus, and we can start right away."

Leia looked surprised.

"You finished building the temple already? Wow, you are really dedicated to this, huh?"

Luke smiled.

"Of course. The Jedi is my life now."

Leia took time to think.

Luke's commitment was impossible to miss. He had not merely talked about rebuilding the Jedi Order. He had already built a temple and prepared a place for students before she had even agreed to join him.

Eventually, she made her choice.

"I have made up my mind. I'm coming with you to Ossus."

Luke was ecstatic.

"I can't wait to train you, Leia. I am sure you will be a great Jedi!"

The two of them returned to Ossus.

Before going to the new temple, they first stopped at the farmhouse.

Luke entered with Leia beside him.

"Uncle Obi-Wan, Aunt Satine, I brought my sister home!"

Obi-Wan immediately smiled.

"Leia! I haven't seen you in so long. How has life been?"

Leia answered, "It's been going well. Well, certainly better than when Palpatine was still alive."

Satine stepped forward.

"You will always have a home here, Leia. If you ever need any help, we will always be happy to help."

Leia went quiet.

Bail and Breha Organa had died with Alderaan.

Padmé had died in childbirth.

Anakin, after returning to the light, had now died as well.

Leia had never met Satine before this moment and was not deeply familiar with Obi-Wan either, but among the people still alive, they were now the closest thing she had to parent figures.

She appreciated the offer deeply.

"Thank you, Obi-Wan. Thank you, Satine."

Obi-Wan nodded.

"Good luck on your Jedi training."

Leia looked toward Luke.

"I will do my best to learn."

Luke and Leia then left the farmhouse and headed for the new Jedi Temple on Ossus.

There, Luke formally began training Leia in the Force.

Obi-Wan occasionally assisted whenever Luke specifically asked him to, just as they had agreed, but Luke remained the primary instructor at all times.

Luke was finally living full-time on Ossus again.

But he no longer lived with Obi-Wan and Satine.

Now, nearly all of his time was spent at the Jedi Temple, where the work of rebuilding the Jedi Order had begun.

# Part 9

After Palpatine's death, the Rebel Alliance shifted its focus toward liberating worlds throughout the galaxy from Imperial control.

By this point, the Alliance had reorganized itself into the New Republic. Rather than fight only a handful of major battles, it began supporting liberation campaigns across many different systems. Often, several worlds were fighting for freedom at the same time, and those campaigns gradually spread throughout the galaxy.

Han Solo and Chewbacca had not returned to the Rebellion since transporting Luke, Obi-Wan, Leia, R2, and Threepio to Yavin 4 a few years earlier. Han had collected his reward money and left, taking Chewie with him and returning to the life of a smuggler.

As the liberation campaigns expanded, however, Chewbacca began pulling Han back toward the fight.

One day, Chewie learned that the New Republic was preparing a campaign to liberate Kashyyyk.

That changed everything for him.

Kashyyyk was his homeworld, and Chewie was determined to help free it from Imperial control. He repeatedly nudged and nagged Han until Han finally gave in.

In the end, Han agreed.

Together, Han and Chewie officially joined the New Republic's campaign to liberate Kashyyyk.

Elsewhere, Bo-Katan Kryze reached a similar conclusion about her own homeworld.

For years, Mandalore had remained under Imperial occupation.

Now, with Palpatine dead and worlds across the galaxy rising against the Empire, Bo-Katan decided that the time had finally come for Mandalore to do the same.

She traveled to Ossus and made her way to the farmhouse.

"Obi-Wan, Satine, I think it's now time to liberate Mandalore."

Satine's expression immediately brightened.

"Great. I have been looking forward to this day for a long time."

Bo-Katan looked at her carefully.

"If we succeed, are you still interested in assuming a position in Mandalorian leadership?"

Satine did not answer immediately.

She thought about the question for some time before finally speaking.

"No. If we win, you should be the ruler of Mandalore moving forward, because you have earned it."

Bo-Katan said nothing.

Satine continued.

"Bo-Katan, I still care deeply about Mandalore. I always have. But I have had decades here to reflect, and now I think pacifism may not be the best option for Mandalorian society anymore."

Bo-Katan looked surprised.

"What makes you say that?"

"My views were directly against Mandalorian tradition, and thus they were too divisive. After everything that Mandalore has been through, I would prefer Mandalorians to be united rather than divided again along political lines."

Satine looked directly at her sister.

"Therefore, I think your views and beliefs may be a better fit for Mandalore in the long term."

Bo-Katan was deeply moved.

"Then I will make sure I do a good job as the one leading Mandalore into a new era."

Obi-Wan stepped in.

"I can help you with the liberation."

Before Bo-Katan could respond, Satine cut him off.

"Obi-Wan, you're retired. Stay out of this."

Obi-Wan turned toward her.

"But Mandalore needs..."

"You agreed to the retirement. You need to mean it."

"Can't you make an exception..."

"I'm serious."

Obi-Wan sighed.

Then he fell quiet.

Satine nodded.

"Thank you, Obi-Wan."

Obi-Wan turned back toward Bo-Katan.

"Bo-Katan, since your sister would absolutely not permit me to offer you any direct assistance..."

Satine rolled her eyes.

Obi-Wan ignored her.

"...then perhaps Luke can help you."

Bo-Katan looked interested.

"Luke?"

"He's a very mature Jedi now. He built a temple on this world, and has been training his twin sister Leia for a while now. Maybe you can ask them if they would be interested in helping Mandalore."

Bo-Katan smiled.

"That's great to hear! I will definitely ask them."

Obi-Wan gave her the coordinates of the Jedi Temple.

Bo-Katan was about to leave when Obi-Wan suddenly stopped her.

"One more thing, Bo-Katan."

She turned back.

"Yes?"

"You should take the Darksaber."

Bo-Katan's expression changed.

"Why?"

"Because it will make it much easier to unite the Mandalorian clans and warriors, and for them to all see you as the legitimate ruler of Mandalore."

Bo-Katan immediately shook her head.

"But I cannot just take it from you. By Mandalorian tradition, I must defeat the previous wielder in direct combat in order to earn it."

Obi-Wan sighed.

"Can we skip the Mandalorian superstition? We don't have time for this nonsense. Mandalore needs to be freed, and you need to gain legitimacy. So just take it."

"No. This is not the Mandalorian way."

"Please."

"No."

"At least consider it. Maybe the value that possessing the Darksaber would bring to you and to Mandalore outweighs the problems caused by breaking tradition?"

"No."

Obi-Wan stared at her.

"So you wouldn't even consider it at all?"

"No."

"Look, Bo-Katan. Even if you win against the Empire, you may have trouble uniting all the Mandalorians and establishing yourself as the legitimate ruler of Mandalore if you don't possess the Darksaber."

Bo-Katan's expression hardened.

"You are in no position to tell me that. I know Mandalore far better than you do."

Obi-Wan sighed in resignation.

Then another idea occurred to him.

He extended his hand toward Bo-Katan.

"Bo-Katan, you want to reconsider whether accepting the Darksaber as a gift to facilitate uniting the Mandalorians and gaining legitimacy may be a worthwhile tradeoff to breaking Mandalorian tradition as a one-time exception."

Satine immediately gave Obi-Wan a look.

He ignored her.

Satine considered interfering, then decided against it. She left Obi-Wan and Bo-Katan to settle the matter themselves.

Bo-Katan's expression softened.

"I want to reconsider whether accepting the Darksaber as a gift to facilitate uniting the Mandalorians and gaining legitimacy may be a worthwhile tradeoff to breaking Mandalorian tradition as a one-time exception."

Obi-Wan smiled faintly.

"Thank you, Bo-Katan."

Bo-Katan went into another room in the farmhouse to seriously consider the tradeoff.

A few hours later, she returned.

"Fine. This may actually be worth it. I will accept the Darksaber from you."

Obi-Wan nodded.

"I am glad you have come to this decision."

He presented the Darksaber to her.

Bo-Katan accepted it.

"Good luck."

"Thank you."

With the Darksaber now in her possession, Bo-Katan left the farmhouse and headed toward the Jedi Temple.

When she arrived, she found Luke and Leia sparring.

"Hello, Luke. It's been a while."

"Hello, Bo-Katan. What brings you here?"

"I'm currently organizing a campaign to liberate Mandalore from the Empire. Would you be interested in helping us?"

Luke and Leia immediately perked up.

For both of them, the opportunity offered a direct and meaningful way to rejoin the galaxy's struggle against the Empire.

Leia was especially enthusiastic.

She had already begun feeling guilty about leaving the Rebellion behind in order to pursue Jedi training. Helping liberate Mandalore offered her a chance to continue that training while also returning to the fight against Imperial rule.

Luke answered first.

"Yes."

Leia was even more emphatic.

"We would LOVE to help!"

Bo-Katan nodded.

"Then let's go."

The three of them boarded Bo-Katan's ship.

Soon afterward, they departed Ossus.

Their destination was Mandalore.

# Part 10

Bo-Katan's ship descended toward Mandalore with Luke and Leia aboard.

Below them, the planet was already erupting into open rebellion.

For years, Imperial forces had held Mandalore under occupation. Moff Gideon commanded the occupation and relied not only on stormtroopers, but also on Mandalorian forces willing to fight on behalf of the Empire.

Now the resistance was rising against them.

Bo-Katan landed near the front.

When the ramp lowered, she stepped out first with the Darksaber in hand.

Luke followed, carrying Anakin Skywalker's old lightsaber.

Leia came beside him with the light blue lightsaber she had constructed under Luke's guidance during her training at the Jedi Temple on Ossus.

Behind them came Mandalorian resistance fighters.

Bo-Katan ignited the Darksaber.

Luke's blue blade came alive beside her.

Leia activated her own light blue blade.

Then the resistance advanced.

Imperial stormtroopers opened fire almost immediately.

Luke and Leia moved to the front.

Blaster bolts poured toward them.

Luke caught one bolt on his blade and redirected it into a stormtrooper position. Leia turned another aside, then another, advancing behind the spinning light of her weapon.

Bo-Katan stayed close beside them.

She did not have the Force guiding her movements, but years of Mandalorian combat training and her familiarity with the Darksaber made her formidable. She stayed behind Luke and Leia whenever incoming fire became too dense, then surged forward whenever they created an opening.

"Push!" Bo-Katan shouted.

The Mandalorian resistance followed.

They crashed into the Imperial line.

Luke leapt into the middle of a stormtrooper formation and struck rapidly, cutting weapons apart and forcing soldiers back.

Leia moved with greater discipline than she had when her training first began. She sensed attacks before they came, shifted out of the path of incoming fire, and used short, controlled strikes rather than wasting movement.

Bo-Katan fought differently from both of them.

She used the Darksaber as one weapon among many.

She blocked with it, fired with her other weapons whenever opportunities appeared, and used traditional Mandalorian combat techniques to break through defenders at close range.

Stormtroopers began falling back.

Imperial-aligned Mandalorians moved in to reinforce them.

The fighting became much harder.

Those warriors knew Mandalorian tactics intimately. They could maneuver through the battlefield with the same speed and aggression as Bo-Katan's resistance fighters.

For a time, the front dissolved into dozens of smaller engagements.

Mandalorians fought Mandalorians.

Blaster fire filled streets and corridors.

Jetpacks roared overhead.

Explosions shook defensive positions.

Through it all, the three lightsaber users remained at the center of the advance.

Luke held one flank almost by himself.

A group of Imperial-aligned Mandalorians descended toward him from above.

Luke looked up.

The Force warned him before they fired.

He moved.

Bolts struck the ground where he had been standing.

Luke raised one hand.

One attacker was pulled sharply off balance and slammed into the ground.

Luke immediately turned and redirected another shot toward a second warrior.

Leia joined him from the side.

"Left!"

Luke moved without asking why.

Leia intercepted an incoming attack that would have caught him from behind.

"Thanks."

"You're welcome."

They continued forward.

Farther ahead, Bo-Katan encountered some of the fiercest resistance.

Several Mandalorians loyal to the Empire tried to stop her advance.

One of them shouted, "You have no claim here!"

Bo-Katan raised the Darksaber.

"I disagree."

They attacked.

Bo-Katan met them head-on.

The Darksaber struck against beskar armor and weapons.

She moved aggressively, forcing one opponent backward, then turned suddenly and drove another away with a heavy strike.

Resistance fighters surged in behind her.

The Imperial line broke.

The rebels advanced deeper into the occupied territory.

Word spread rapidly.

Bo-Katan had returned.

She carried the Darksaber.

And the resistance was winning.

More Mandalorians joined the uprising as the battle continued.

Imperial positions fell one after another.

Stormtroopers were driven from fortified streets.

Weapons emplacements were destroyed.

Imperial-aligned Mandalorian units either retreated or were overwhelmed.

At the front, Luke, Leia, and Bo-Katan kept moving.

Luke's lightsaber flashed blue through smoke.

Leia's light blue blade followed beside it.

The Darksaber cut a black line through the battle whenever Bo-Katan raised it.

Eventually the resistance reached the heart of Imperial control.

By then, Moff Gideon understood that the occupation was collapsing.

He ordered a withdrawal.

Imperial forces began abandoning positions wherever escape was still possible.

Gideon himself fled toward his ship.

Resistance fighters pursued him.

Bo-Katan saw the vessel begin to rise.

"He's getting away!"

Luke looked up.

The ship climbed rapidly.

Mandalorian resistance fighters opened fire.

Heavy weapons joined the attack.

The ship took several hits.

It continued upward.

Then another barrage struck one of its engines.

The vessel lurched.

Smoke poured from the damaged section.

A final series of shots hit the ship as it tried to accelerate away.

The craft lost control.

It fell.

The ship crashed and exploded.

Moff Gideon was killed.

With Gideon dead and organized Imperial resistance collapsing, the remaining occupying forces lost what little cohesion they still had.

Some fought until they were defeated.

Others surrendered.

Others fled.

By the end of the battle, Mandalore was free.

The fighting gradually stopped.

Smoke still drifted over damaged streets and shattered defensive positions, but Imperial control was gone.

Mandalorian resistance fighters gathered around Bo-Katan.

Luke and Leia stood nearby.

Bo-Katan looked across the people assembled before her.

For years, Mandalore had been occupied, divided, and ruled by outsiders.

Now it belonged to Mandalorians again.

Bo-Katan raised the Darksaber.

The gathered warriors grew quiet.

"I will not allow Mandalore to be divided again."

Her voice carried across the crowd.

"We have fought too long against one another while others decided our future for us."

She looked across the Mandalorians gathered there.

"That ends now."

She lifted the Darksaber higher.

"I am Bo-Katan Kryze."

Then she made her claim.

"I am Mand'alor."

The response from the gathered Mandalorians spread outward through the crowd.

Bo-Katan had become the new ruler of Mandalore.

At roughly the same time, another liberation campaign was reaching its own conclusion.

On Kashyyyk, New Republic forces fought alongside Wookiees to break Imperial control of the planet.

Han Solo and Chewbacca had joined that campaign because Chewie refused to stand aside while his homeworld fought for freedom.

The battle was fierce, but the combined effort succeeded.

Imperial forces were driven from Kashyyyk.

The planet was liberated.

For Chewbacca, it meant something deeply personal.

His homeworld was free.

Across the galaxy, similar victories continued to accumulate.

World after world escaped Imperial control.

Imperial military strength steadily collapsed.

Systems that had once feared open resistance now declared for the New Republic.

Remaining Imperial forces lost territory, resources, and support.

Not long afterward, the Galactic Civil War finally ended.

The New Republic emerged victorious.

The Empire that had replaced the Republic and ruled the galaxy through fear was defeated.

Mandalore was free under Bo-Katan.

Kashyyyk was free.

Luke had begun rebuilding the Jedi Order on Ossus.

Leia had become one of his first students.

Obi-Wan and Satine remained together at their home on Ossus, watching a galaxy they had spent so many years resisting finally begin to move beyond Imperial rule.

And after decades of war, occupation, secrecy, loss, and rebellion, peace finally had the chance to begin.

The end.
