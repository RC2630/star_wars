# Part 1

Anakin Skywalker urged the swoop bike across the endless dunes of Tatooine with relentless speed. Sand blasted against his goggles and robes as twin suns gave way to darkness. Every instinct within him pulled him toward the Tusken Raider camp. Somewhere beyond the rolling desert, his mother was waiting.

He did not slow.

The distant glow of campfires eventually appeared against the night. The crude village stood silent except for the occasional cry of a bantha and the low murmur of Tusken voices drifting between tents.

Anakin brought the bike to a stop behind a rocky ridge and listened.

He reached out through the Force.

There.

A familiar presence.

"Weak... but alive."

His breathing steadied. Without hesitation, he slipped through the shadows toward the camp.

Avoiding the sentries, he moved between the tents until he reached the one where he sensed her. He quietly lifted the entrance flap and stepped inside.

The dim light of a small fire revealed Shmi Skywalker.

She was bound to a wooden frame, bruised from head to toe. Her breathing was shallow, and dried blood covered many of her wounds.

Her eyes slowly opened.

"Anakin..."

He rushed to her side.

"Mom."

His hands quickly untied the restraints.

"I'm here. You're safe now."

She tried to smile despite the pain.

"I knew... you'd come."

He gently supported her as she collapsed into his arms.

"I'm taking you home," he said firmly. "You're coming back to the Lars homestead."

She nodded weakly.

Anakin wrapped her in a blanket he found inside the tent and carefully lifted her into his arms.

A pair of Tusken voices sounded outside.

Without a word, Anakin ignited his lightsaber.

The blue blade flashed to life.

The two Raiders rushed into the tent in surprise.

Anakin struck swiftly, knocking one unconscious with the Force before disarming the other with a precise slash that sent the gaderffii stick flying.

Neither was allowed another chance to interfere.

Keeping Shmi securely in his arms, Anakin hurried back through the camp.

More cries echoed behind him as several Tuskens noticed the escape.

He leaped onto the swoop bike, settling his mother carefully behind him.

"Hold on."

She wrapped what little strength she had around him.

The engine roared.

The bike shot across the dunes while blaster fire disappeared into the darkness behind them.

By the time the Tusken Raiders organized a pursuit, Anakin was already far beyond their reach.

The following day, under the harsh daylight of Tatooine, the swoop bike came to a stop outside the Lars homestead.

Owen hurried from the moisture farm.

"Anakin!"

Beru gasped as she saw Shmi.

"Oh no..."

Cliegg, seated in his hoverchair near the entrance, stared in disbelief.

"Shmi..."

Anakin carefully lifted his mother from the bike.

"I found her."

Cliegg's eyes filled with tears.

"You brought her home."

"We don't have much time," Anakin said. "She needs proper medical treatment. I'm taking her into Mos Eisley immediately."

Padmé hurried outside after hearing the commotion. She immediately knelt beside Shmi.

"We'll get her help."

Just as everyone prepared to leave, the familiar dome of R2-D2 rolled rapidly toward the homestead from the direction where the Naboo starship had been hidden.

The astromech emitted an urgent series of whistles.

Anakin looked down.

"What is it, Artoo?"

The little droid projected a hologram.

Obi-Wan Kenobi appeared.

"If you're receiving this message, I've tracked the bounty hunter to Geonosis."

The transmission crackled.

"I've uncovered something much larger than expected. The Trade Federation has allied with Count Dooku and is assembling a massive droid army."

The hologram flickered.

"I've been captured. Whatever you do, be careful. They know you're connected to me."

The transmission ended.

Silence settled over the homestead.

Padmé looked directly at Anakin.

"We have to go."

Anakin hesitated.

"My mother..."

Shmi reached out and gently touched his arm.

"Go."

He looked down at her.

"I just got you back."

"And you've already saved me."

Her voice remained weak but steady.

"Obi-Wan needs you."

Padmé nodded.

"We can trust your family to care for her until we return."

Cliegg straightened himself in his hoverchair.

"You have my word."

Owen stepped forward.

"We'll take her into Mos Eisley ourselves."

Beru smiled reassuringly.

"We'll stay with her the entire time."

Padmé removed a small datachip from her belongings and handed it to Cliegg.

"This contains more than enough credits."

He looked surprised.

"Senator, this is too much."

"It's for Shmi's treatment," Padmé replied. "And for you."

He frowned.

"For me?"

"I know you lost your leg. Use these funds to purchase a prosthetic. There's no reason you should continue without one."

Cliegg looked down at the datachip before meeting her eyes.

"I don't know how to thank you."

"You don't have to."

She smiled warmly.

"Just take care of Shmi until we return."

"We will," Owen promised.

Anakin knelt beside his mother one last time.

"I'll come back as soon as I can."

"I know you will," Shmi whispered.

He embraced her carefully before standing.

Moments later, Anakin, Padmé, R2-D2, and C-3PO boarded the Naboo starship.

The sleek vessel lifted into the sky and disappeared toward Geonosis.

Once the ship had vanished beyond the horizon, Owen gently lifted Shmi into a transport.

"We'd better get moving."

Cliegg maneuvered his hoverchair alongside them.

"Let's bring her home for good."

Later that day, the Lars family arrived in Mos Eisley.

Using Padmé's generous funds, they admitted Shmi into the finest medical facility available on the planet.

Doctors immediately examined her injuries.

One physician approached the family afterward.

"She has suffered extensive trauma and severe dehydration. We've stabilized her condition. The bacta tank will greatly accelerate her recovery."

"When can she go in?" Owen asked.

"Immediately."

The family watched as medical staff carefully lowered Shmi into the bacta tank. Suspended within the healing fluid, her wounds began the slow process of repair.

Cliegg remained nearby, relief finally replacing weeks of despair.

A doctor soon approached him as well.

"I understand you're interested in a prosthetic replacement."

Cliegg nodded.

"I lost my leg some time ago."

"We can fit you with an advanced prosthetic immediately."

He glanced toward the datachip Padmé had given him.

"I finally have the means."

The operation was scheduled without delay.

Over the following days, Owen and Beru rarely left the medical facility.

Each morning, Beru visited Shmi's bacta chamber.

"She's looking better today," she would say.

Each afternoon, Owen checked on Cliegg as therapists helped him adapt to his new prosthetic.

"Take another step."

Cliegg carefully shifted his weight.

Then another.

Soon he crossed the room unaided.

He laughed for the first time in many days.

"I'd almost forgotten what this felt like."

About a week later, the doctors gathered the family together.

"The treatments have been successful."

The bacta tank slowly drained.

Shmi stepped out, healthy once more.

The bruises had vanished.

Her strength had returned.

She smiled as Beru wrapped her in a warm embrace.

"It's wonderful to see you standing again."

Shmi laughed softly.

"It's wonderful to be standing."

Across the hall, Cliegg walked confidently into the room using his new prosthetic leg.

He crossed the floor without assistance before stopping in front of Shmi.

She smiled through tears.

"You can walk."

He smiled back.

"And you're home."

The family embraced together, grateful that both recoveries had been successful.

When they finally returned to the Lars homestead, the experience had changed them all.

One evening, Cliegg stood outside with Owen and Beru, looking across the open desert.

"We were fortunate this time."

Owen nodded.

"We can't depend on fortune again."

Beru folded her arms.

"We need stronger defenses."

Cliegg agreed.

"We'll reinforce every entrance."

Owen continued, "We'll expand the perimeter sensors."

"And keep a constant watch," Beru added.

Cliegg looked toward the distant horizon.

"The Tusken Raiders will never again threaten this family."

The others nodded in silent agreement as the suns slowly descended over the desert that they now intended to guard more carefully than ever before.

# Part 2

Three years passed.

The Lars homestead endured beneath the twin suns of Tatooine. Life settled into familiar rhythms. Moisture harvesters required constant maintenance, vaporators demanded repair, and the desert remained as unforgiving as ever.

Yet one question lingered over the family.

Anakin had never returned.

Shmi often stood outside in the evenings, gazing toward the horizon.

"He promised he'd come back," she would quietly say.

Cliegg would gently place a hand on her shoulder.

"He meant that promise."

"I know."

She would smile sadly.

"I only wish I knew why he hasn't kept it."

One afternoon, a lone figure approached the homestead on foot.

Owen noticed him first.

"We've got a visitor."

Everyone stepped outside.

The traveler wore the robes of a Jedi Master. His beard had grown longer over the years, and the hardships of recent times showed plainly on his face.

In his arms rested a sleeping infant wrapped in a blanket.

Shmi immediately recognized him.

"Master Kenobi."

Obi-Wan Kenobi stopped before the family.

Without ceremony, he carefully placed the baby into Shmi's waiting arms.

"This is Luke Skywalker," he said quietly. "Anakin's son. Please take care of him."

Shmi looked down at the peacefully sleeping child before looking back up.

"Who is the mother?"

"Senator Padmé Amidala."

Beru exchanged a glance with Owen before smiling faintly.

"I'm not surprised."

Shmi's expression became troubled.

"What happened to Anakin? And what happened to Padmé? Why can't they raise Luke?"

Obi-Wan lowered his eyes.

"Padmé has unfortunately passed away during childbirth."

A heavy silence followed.

Shmi gently rocked her grandson.

"And Anakin?"

Obi-Wan remained still for several moments.

"As for Anakin..." he finally said, "...Mrs. Skywalker Lars, unfortunately I am afraid that I cannot tell you."

Shmi's voice trembled.

"Master Kenobi, please. I need to know what happened to my son."

Obi-Wan met her eyes with obvious sorrow.

"In time you may learn. But for now, for your own safety, for your own good, it is best that you don't learn."

Shmi sighed deeply.

"I don't understand."

"I know."

Obi-Wan looked down at the child.

"Take care of Luke. No matter what happens, make sure he is safe."

Cliegg stepped forward.

"We will."

He placed a reassuring hand on Shmi's shoulder.

"Master Kenobi, you have our word."

Obi-Wan inclined his head.

"Thank you."

Without another word, he turned away from the homestead and walked back into the desert until the shimmering heat swallowed him from sight.

Shmi looked down once more at the infant sleeping peacefully in her arms.

She softly brushed a finger across his tiny hand.

"Hello, Luke."

The baby wrapped his fingers around hers.

She smiled through tears.

"Welcome home."

The years passed quickly.

Luke Skywalker grew up surrounded by a family that devoted themselves completely to raising him.

Shmi loved him as only a grandmother could.

Cliegg became a steady grandfather whose patience seemed endless.

Owen taught Luke responsibility from the moment he could carry tools.

Beru filled the home with warmth, encouragement, and kindness.

Although Luke had lost both of his parents before he could know them, he never lacked for love.

When Luke reached four years old, his curiosity blossomed.

One afternoon, while helping Shmi sort supplies inside the homestead, he looked up at her with a puzzled expression.

"Grandma?"

"Yes, sweetie?"

"Why aren't Uncle Owen and Aunt Beru my dad and mom?"

Shmi smiled gently.

"Because Uncle Owen is Grandpa's son and my stepson. Your dad is my son and Grandpa's stepson."

Luke blinked.

"Wow... that sounds confusing."

She laughed softly.

"Don't worry about it too much, sweetie."

Luke nodded thoughtfully before another question entered his mind.

"So... what was my dad like then?"

Shmi's face brightened.

"He was kind, brave, generous, and he was always trying to help people."

Luke smiled.

"What did he like?"

"Oh, many things."

She leaned back in her chair.

"He loved tinkering with droids."

"And he loved podracing."

Luke's eyes widened.

"Podracing? Is that the one where they zoom really fast in a pod?"

Shmi smiled.

"Yes, Luke, that one. Remember we took you to watch one last year?"

Luke nodded enthusiastically.

"It was loud!"

"It certainly was."

Luke thought quietly for a moment.

"So where's my dad now?"

The smile slowly faded from Shmi's face.

She looked toward the distant desert beyond the doorway.

"We... don't know."

Luke waited silently.

"He just disappeared one day."

Her voice softened.

"He promised me that he would come back, but he never did."

She looked back at Luke.

"We are still waiting for him to return."

"How long?"

"It's been seven years since we last saw him."

Luke's cheerful expression disappeared.

"Oh..."

He lowered his head.

"That's so sad."

Shmi gently hugged him.

"Try not to think about it too much, sweetie."

She kissed the top of his head.

"But know this."

Luke looked back up.

"We will always be here for you."

As Luke grew older, the questions never stopped.

"Grandma, what was Dad like when he was little?"

"He was endlessly curious."

"What did he do for fun?"

"He could take apart almost anything and somehow put it back together even better than before."

"Did he ever get into trouble?"

She laughed.

"Oh, certainly."

Another evening, Luke asked, "How did Dad become a Jedi?"

Shmi smiled at the memory.

"When he was nine years old, a Jedi Master named Qui-Gon Jinn came to Tatooine."

"What happened?"

"He recognized something extraordinary in your father."

"So Dad left?"

"Yes."

She nodded.

"Master Qui-Gon took him away to Coruscant so he could train as a Jedi."

Luke leaned forward eagerly.

"Weren't you scared?"

"I was."

She looked thoughtfully toward the stars.

"But I knew Anakin had a bigger path ahead of him."

As the years continued, Luke's questions became deeper.

When he reached his teenage years, Shmi decided he was old enough to understand parts of her own past that she had once shielded him from.

One quiet evening outside the homestead, Luke sat beside her.

"Grandma... you once said life wasn't always like this."

"No."

She folded her hands together.

"It wasn't."

She took a slow breath.

"When your father and I lived here long ago, we were slaves."

Luke stared.

"Slaves?"

She nodded.

"We belonged to a Toydarian junk dealer named Watto."

Luke frowned.

"He owned people?"

"He believed he did."

She looked out across the desert.

"We weren't free."

Luke remained silent.

"There were explosive slave transmitters placed inside our bodies."

His eyes widened.

"What?"

"If a slave tried to escape..."

She paused.

"...the transmitter could be detonated."

Luke struggled to process what he had heard.

"That's horrible."

"It was."

She rested a comforting hand on his shoulder.

"But your father never stopped believing that someday we would be free."

Luke looked at her with admiration.

"He never gave up?"

"Never."

She smiled warmly.

"That was who Anakin was."

Over the years, Luke heard countless stories.

He learned about Anakin building C-3PO from spare parts.

He learned how his father had loved machines and could repair almost anything.

He heard stories of podracing, of dusty workshops filled with scavenged components, and of a boy whose determination seemed greater than the harsh desert around him.

He listened as Shmi described Anakin's compassion, his eagerness to help strangers, and the unwavering hope that had carried him through difficult days.

From Owen and Beru, Luke learned practical lessons about responsibility, hard work, and protecting family.

From Cliegg, he learned perseverance and quiet strength.

But it was Shmi who preserved the memories of the young Anakin Skywalker.

She answered every question she could.

Whenever Luke wanted to know more about his father, she welcomed the conversation.

The questions that no one could answer remained the same.

What had happened after Anakin left Tatooine that final time?

Why had Obi-Wan refused to explain?

Why had Anakin never returned after promising that he would?

No one at the Lars homestead knew.

And so Luke grew into adulthood carrying with him a detailed picture of his father's early life, shaped by the memories of the grandmother who had known him best during his childhood.

Everything after Anakin departed Tatooine with Padmé for Geonosis remained a mystery that none of them could solve.

# Part 3

Luke Skywalker was 19 years old when a Jawa sandcrawler rumbled slowly across the Jundland Wastes and came to a halt near the Lars homestead.

The massive vehicle groaned as its enormous treads churned through the sand.

Outside the homestead, Owen looked toward the towering machine.

"They're selling droids again, aren't they?"

He turned toward Luke.

"Luke, go check them out."

"Right."

Luke climbed into his landspeeder and drove toward the sandcrawler.

A short while later, he returned with an excited expression.

"Grandpa, they have an astromech and a protocol droid that may be of great assistance to our moisture farm."

Cliegg stood up from his chair.

"Let me take a look."

"I'll come too," Shmi said.

Together they walked toward the sandcrawler.

The Jawas eagerly showed off their merchandise.

One tall, golden protocol droid stepped forward politely.

"Good day, honored customers."

The moment Shmi saw him, her eyes widened.

"Thr... Threepio?! Is that you? I haven't seen you in so long!"

The protocol droid tilted his head.

"I'm terribly sorry, but... may I please ask who I have the pleasure of talking with?"

Shmi blinked in surprise.

"Threepio... you don't remember me?"

"I am afraid not, ma'am."

She lowered her head with a quiet sigh.

"It's alright, Threepio."

She looked toward Cliegg.

"Dear, we should buy him."

Cliegg nodded immediately.

"Absolutely."

Before the transaction could be completed, Threepio raised one finger politely.

"And may I suggest this R2 unit right here..."

He gently tapped the blue-and-white astromech's dome.

"...is in perfect condition and is greatly willing to assist you. If I may be so bold, I would advise you to buy him as well."

R2-D2 emitted an enthusiastic series of beeps.

Shmi studied the astromech closely.

"Hmm..."

She narrowed her eyes.

"I think I have seen that droid before once. Any clues?"

Cliegg rubbed his chin.

"Let me think..."

After a moment, recognition appeared on his face.

"I believe that's the astromech that Anakin and Padmé brought when they last visited us."

Shmi's expression softened.

"I think you are right."

She gently rested a hand on R2's dome.

"Wow... that was 22 years ago."

She smiled sadly.

"A long time ago..."

Cliegg looked toward the Jawas.

"Alright, Threepio. We'll buy him as well."

Threepio bowed politely.

"Thank you, sir."

R2 beeped happily as the sale was completed.

Not long afterward, the two droids entered the Lars homestead.

Luke examined them with interest while Owen and Beru prepared a place for them to recharge.

Shmi stood before Threepio with a thoughtful expression.

"Threepio, what's the earliest thing you remember?"

The protocol droid considered the question.

"Captain Antilles of Alderaan was polishing me."

He paused briefly as though searching his memory.

"I believe that was 19 years ago."

Shmi nodded.

"And what did you do after that?"

"I served Queen Breha Organa of Alderaan in her palace as a protocol droid."

He smiled politely.

"Later I served her daughter, Princess Leia Organa."

"I see."

Shmi offered a gentle smile.

"Thank you, Threepio."

Cliegg folded his arms.

"I believe the droid's memory has been wiped."

"I'm afraid so," Shmi replied quietly.

She turned toward the astromech.

"Let's try R2 then."

She crouched beside him.

"R2, what's the earliest thing you remember?"

The little astromech gave an innocent sequence of electronic beeps.

Threepio translated.

"Artoo says... he cannot say."

Shmi frowned.

"R2, do you remember Anakin Skywalker?"

R2 emitted another innocent whistle.

Threepio translated once more.

"Artoo says... he cannot say."

"What about Padmé Amidala?"

Again, the same cheerful beeping.

"Artoo says... he cannot say."

Shmi exchanged puzzled glances with the rest of the family.

She tried again.

"Were you with my son after he left here?"

R2 answered with another series of chirps.

"Artoo says... he cannot say."

No matter how many questions they asked, the answer never changed.

He simply would not reveal anything about the past.

After several minutes, Owen finally shook his head.

"I think someone has explicitly instructed R2 to keep quiet about the past for some reason."

Shmi looked thoughtfully at the astromech.

"Hmm..."

She nodded slowly.

"I think you may be onto something."

Cliegg crossed his arms.

"Obi-Wan won't say anything about the past."

He looked down at R2.

"Now R2 also won't say."

He sighed.

"There's definitely something suspicious going on."

Shmi looked at Luke.

"Forget it for now."

She smiled gently.

"Luke, can you go clean up these droids?"

Luke nodded.

"Yes, right away."

He wheeled the two droids toward the workshop.

"Come on, you two."

Threepio followed obediently.

"What a delightful workshop."

R2 whistled as he rolled behind them.

Luke spent the next several hours removing layers of dust and sand that had accumulated on the droids.

Threepio chatted constantly.

"I must admit, this is the cleanest I have been in quite some time."

Luke laughed.

"I can tell."

R2 answered with a series of playful whistles.

"You'll have your turn too."

As evening approached, Luke stepped away for only a few moments to retrieve additional cleaning supplies.

When he returned, Threepio stood alone.

Luke looked around.

"Where's R2?"

Threepio looked toward the open doorway.

"Oh dear."

"He appears to have left."

Luke hurried outside.

"R2!"

There was no answer.

Only the wind blowing across the desert.

The astromech was gone.

Luke climbed into his landspeeder.

"We've got to find him."

Threepio climbed into the passenger seat.

"Oh my. I certainly hope he hasn't wandered too far."

The landspeeder sped away from the homestead, disappearing into the desert in search of the missing astromech.

Back at the Lars homestead, the family waited.

Minutes turned into hours.

The suns drifted lower in the sky.

Beru looked toward the horizon.

"They've been gone a long time."

Owen folded his arms uneasily.

"They should have been back by now."

Shmi stood quietly outside the homestead, staring across the endless dunes.

"I hope they're alright."

No landspeeder appeared on the horizon.

Luke and Threepio did not return.

# Part 4

Meanwhile, aboard the Imperial Star Destroyer *Devastator*, Darth Vader stood on the command bridge, silently watching the stars streak past the viewport.

An Imperial officer approached and saluted.

"My lord."

Vader turned slightly.

"Report."

"Sir, an escape pod has been observed leaving the *Tantive IV*. No life-forms have been detected aboard it."

Vader remained silent for only a moment.

"Then perhaps there were droids in there."

He turned fully toward the officer.

"Send a squad of stormtroopers to track them down immediately."

"Yes, my lord."

Vader began walking toward the bridge exit.

"And I think I am coming myself as well."

The officer looked surprised.

"My lord?"

"Place the stormtroopers under my direct command."

"It will be done, my lord."

Some time later, an Imperial shuttle descended through the atmosphere of Tatooine.

Its landing ramp lowered onto the desert.

Darth Vader strode down the ramp, followed by a squad of stormtroopers.

The group soon came upon a cluster of frightened Jawas standing beside their sandcrawler.

Vader stopped before them.

"Have you seen any droids around here recently?"

The Jawas nervously shook their heads.

"Utinni... utinni..."

Vader's helmet tilted slightly.

He stretched out one black-gloved hand.

The nearest Jawa suddenly stiffened as Vader reached into his mind through the Force.

"LIAR!!"

The Jawa cried out in terror.

Vader's invasive Force-based mind probe tore through the frightened scavenger's memories.

Moments later, Vader lowered his hand.

"They just sold some droids to the homestead over in that direction."

He pointed across the desert.

"Come on. Let's go."

"Yes, my lord," the stormtroopers answered in unison.

Not long afterward, the Imperial force arrived at the Lars homestead.

The family looked on uneasily as the armored soldiers surrounded the property.

Vader remained near the edge of the courtyard.

He gestured toward the house.

"Go request the owners to surrender all droids in their possession right away, and report back to me."

The stormtroopers marched toward the entrance.

A few minutes later, they returned.

One of them saluted.

"My lord, the owners say that they don't have any droids in their possession at the moment."

Vader's mechanical breathing grew heavier.

"LIARS!"

He pointed toward the buildings.

"If they will not cooperate, burn the entire place down!"

"Yes, my lord."

The stormtroopers raised their blaster rifles and prepared to carry out the order.

Just then, the front door opened.

Shmi Skywalker Lars stepped outside.

She looked at the black-armored figure standing in the courtyard.

For the first time since arriving, Vader froze.

The stormtroopers waited for his command.

Several long seconds passed.

Finally, Vader spoke.

"Wait."

The troopers lowered their weapons.

"Stormtroopers, do NOT burn anything."

The soldiers looked at one another.

"Leave at once and go back to my ship."

"My lord?"

"I will handle everything myself from here."

"Yes, my lord."

The stormtroopers immediately obeyed.

Within minutes, they had departed the homestead, leaving only Vader standing before the Lars family.

Owen cautiously stepped forward.

"Lord Vader, what business do you have here? As far as we are concerned, we have not broken any Imperial laws..."

Vader slowly raised one hand, silencing him.

"I am only asking for you to hand over your droids."

Cliegg answered firmly.

"We have no droids."

"You are lying."

Vader's voice remained cold.

"And I know it."

Shmi slowly approached.

"Please, Lord Vader, believe us and spare us."

Her hands trembled.

"We are just a little family running a moisture farm in the Outer Rim."

She lowered her eyes respectfully.

"We have nothing against the Empire. We..."

Vader interrupted her.

"Do you know who I am?"

Shmi nodded.

"Yes, I know."

She swallowed nervously.

"You are one of the most respected officers of the Galactic Empire..."

Vader's respirator echoed across the courtyard.

"No."

His voice became quieter.

"I am your son."

Everything became still.

Shmi stared at the black mask before her.

Unable to speak.

Vader took one slow step forward.

"Search your feelings."

His mechanical voice softened almost imperceptibly.

"You know it to be true."

Shmi's lips trembled.

"A... Ani?"

Tears filled her eyes.

"Anakin?"

Vader remained motionless.

"That name no longer has any meaning for me."

Shmi's breathing became unsteady.

"Ani..."

She looked at the armor that concealed every part of him.

"What... what have you become?"

Before Vader could answer, the sound of an approaching landspeeder echoed across the desert.

Everyone turned.

Luke's landspeeder raced toward the homestead before slowing to a stop.

Luke climbed out enthusiastically.

"I've found R2!"

R2-D2 rolled happily down the ramp.

C-3PO followed close behind.

"And I brought Old Ben back as well!"

Obi-Wan Kenobi stepped calmly from the landspeeder.

Shmi's face brightened with relief.

"Well done, Luke."

She looked toward the elderly Jedi.

"Master Kenobi..."

At that precise moment, Obi-Wan and Vader locked eyes.

Neither spoke for several seconds.

Luke looked back and forth between them, confused by the sudden silence.

Obi-Wan finally broke it.

"Leave them alone."

Vader turned fully toward his former master.

"You shall not tell me what I can or cannot do with my own family!"

Obi-Wan's expression remained calm.

"They are no longer your family."

His voice carried quiet conviction.

"By embracing the ways of the Sith, you have abandoned them."

Vader took another deliberate step forward.

"Who are you to tell me this?"

Obi-Wan did not move.

"I have been protecting and watching over this family for two decades."

He glanced briefly toward Shmi, Cliegg, Owen, Beru, and Luke.

"And I will continue to do so, even if the threat is you."

His gaze returned to Vader.

"Especially if the threat is you."

Vader's gloved hand lowered toward his lightsaber.

"I will not permit this."

He unclipped the weapon from his belt.

"We shall settle this with a duel then."

With a sharp snap-hiss, a crimson blade burst from Vader's lightsaber.

Obi-Wan calmly drew his own weapon.

A brilliant blue blade ignited with a familiar hum.

The members of the Lars family instinctively stepped back.

Luke stared in stunned silence.

The two former Jedi stood facing one another in the fading light of Tatooine's twin suns.

Without another word, the duel began.

# Part 5

The clash of blue and red lightsabers echoed across the Lars homestead.

Again and again, Obi-Wan Kenobi and Darth Vader exchanged blows.

The family watched from a safe distance, shielding Luke as the duel carried across the courtyard.

Vader attacked with immense strength, each strike heavy enough to drive Obi-Wan backward through the sand.

Obi-Wan answered with patience and precision, deflecting each blow while waiting for an opening.

The duel continued.

Gradually, the balance shifted.

Vader's powerful attacks became increasingly frustrated as Obi-Wan anticipated them one after another.

Their blades locked.

Obi-Wan twisted aside and forced Vader off balance.

The Sith Lord stumbled back several steps.

Before Vader could recover, Obi-Wan pressed the advantage.

His blue blade drove Vader steadily backward across the courtyard.

Another exchange.

Another retreat.

Vader blocked desperately as Obi-Wan's attacks became faster and more relentless.

The crimson blade barely kept pace.

The Sith Lord found himself yielding ground again and again.

Realizing the battle had turned against him, Vader abruptly disengaged.

He thrust Obi-Wan backward with the Force, creating several precious moments of distance.

Without another word, he turned and sprinted toward the Imperial shuttle.

The boarding ramp lowered.

Vader disappeared inside.

Moments later, the shuttle lifted off, carrying him away from the Lars homestead.

It climbed into the Tatooine sky before disappearing toward the waiting Imperial vessel in orbit.

Only after the engines had faded into silence did everyone slowly gather inside the Lars home.

No one spoke for several moments.

Finally, Shmi looked directly at Obi-Wan.

"Master Kenobi..."

Her voice was calm despite the tears still in her eyes.

"I think you owe us an explanation."

Obi-Wan lowered his head.

He let out a long sigh.

"I guess..."

He looked around the room at every member of the family.

"...it's not possible to hide this any longer."

Luke sat quietly beside his grandmother.

Obi-Wan continued.

"Anakin fell to the dark side of the Force and became the Sith Lord Darth Vader."

The words hung heavily in the room.

Shmi shook her head slowly.

"But how...?"

She struggled to understand.

"How could Anakin do such a thing?"

Obi-Wan answered gently.

"He feared that Padmé would die."

He looked down.

"His fear led him down the path of the dark side, and eventually he caused his own fear to come true."

Shmi's eyes widened.

"Anakin killed Padmé?"

"Not exactly."

Obi-Wan's voice was quiet.

"Not directly."

He paused.

"But he did choke her."

No one interrupted.

"Padmé then died while giving birth."

Shmi covered her face as tears streamed down her cheeks.

"My poor Ani..."

Beru wrapped an arm around her shoulders.

After several moments, Shmi looked up again.

"And why is he in that suit?"

She swallowed.

"Why won't he show any part of his own body?"

Obi-Wan's expression grew heavy with painful memories.

"I didn't have any other choice."

He closed his eyes briefly.

"I was forced to confront him on Mustafar."

He spoke slowly.

"We had a brutal duel there."

He looked toward the floor.

"He ended up severely burned and injured and had to be placed in that suit to stay alive."

Shmi quietly nodded.

"I don't blame you, Master."

She wiped away her tears.

"I don't like it..."

She took a slow breath.

"...but you did what you had to do."

Obi-Wan inclined his head respectfully.

"Thank you for understanding."

Luke had remained silent through the entire conversation.

Finally, he spoke.

"How many people know what really happened to my father?"

Obi-Wan answered immediately.

"Not many."

He folded his hands inside his robes.

"Very few people, in fact."

He glanced toward R2-D2.

"And out of the people right here, only Artoo and I know."

Owen pointed toward the little astromech.

"So Artoo really does know."

He shook his head.

"I knew it."

"I knew he was hiding something from us."

R2 emitted a small, apologetic whistle.

Beru looked at Obi-Wan.

"So you were the one who instructed R2 to not talk about the past?"

"I was one of the people who gave that instruction," Obi-Wan replied. "Yes."

Luke frowned.

"But why would you do that?"

"I didn't want the truth to come out just yet."

Obi-Wan looked around the room.

"We thought it would be wiser to hide it in order to better protect all of you."

Luke sighed deeply.

The room became quiet again.

At last, Obi-Wan spoke once more.

"I think it's clear now that your father has fallen beyond redemption."

His voice remained steady.

"He must be killed if we are to have any chance at stopping the Empire."

Luke immediately stood.

"But he's my father!"

His voice cracked with emotion.

"We can't just kill him..."

Before Obi-Wan could answer, Shmi spoke.

"Master Kenobi..."

Everyone looked toward her.

"I would like to respectfully disagree with you here."

She looked toward the doorway through which Vader had departed only a short while earlier.

"I believe that there is still good in him."

Luke turned toward his grandmother.

"Ben..."

He looked back at Obi-Wan.

"I think Grandma is right."

He nodded with conviction.

"If she says there is still good in him, then I believe it too."

Obi-Wan closed his eyes for a moment before opening them again.

He sighed.

"Luke..."

His tone softened.

"You are still young."

"And you are still quite naive."

He rested a hand on Luke's shoulder.

"In time I think you will see the truth for what it is."

He then looked toward R2-D2.

"But for now..."

A faint smile appeared.

"We have a droid to deliver to Alderaan..."

Luke finished the thought.

"...and a princess to rescue."

Obi-Wan nodded.

"Exactly."

He turned toward Shmi, Cliegg, Owen, and Beru.

"If you will excuse us, we must leave at once."

He gestured toward the two droids.

"We must take R2 and Threepio with us."

Owen exchanged an uncertain glance with Beru.

Neither looked entirely comfortable with Luke leaving.

Shmi simply raised a hand and gave a gentle nod.

"I understand."

She walked over and embraced Luke tightly.

"Luke, stay safe."

She smiled through lingering tears.

"And please come back."

Luke hugged her in return.

"I will, Grandma."

He embraced Cliegg next.

"I'll be back before you know it."

Cliegg smiled warmly.

"We'll be waiting."

Luke hugged Owen.

"Take care of the farm."

Owen chuckled.

"I was about to tell you the same thing."

Finally, Beru wrapped him in a long embrace.

"Be careful."

"I will."

Outside, Obi-Wan climbed aboard the landspeeder.

R2-D2 rolled into position behind him.

C-3PO settled into his seat.

Luke took one final look at the family that had raised him.

Shmi stood beside Cliegg.

Owen and Beru stood together near the doorway of the homestead.

Luke raised a hand.

"I'll see you all soon."

"We'll be here," Shmi called back.

The landspeeder's engine came to life.

With Obi-Wan driving, Luke beside him, and R2-D2 and C-3PO riding behind, the vehicle sped away across the desert toward Mos Eisley.

The four figures at the Lars homestead watched until the landspeeder disappeared into the shimmering horizon.

Only then did they quietly return inside, hoping that Luke's journey would bring him safely home once again.

# Part 6

Four years passed.

The Lars homestead once again settled into a quiet routine beneath the twin suns of Tatooine.

But unlike before, this silence was filled with worry.

Luke had never returned.

No messages arrived.

No travelers brought news.

No one in the family heard anything about him.

As the years wore on, Shmi, Cliegg, Owen, and Beru gradually began to fear the worst.

Many evenings, Shmi would stand outside the homestead just as she had done decades earlier while waiting for Anakin.

Now she waited for Luke.

"I hope you're safe," she would whisper into the desert wind.

Then, one afternoon, a familiar landspeeder appeared on the horizon.

Owen shaded his eyes.

"Someone's coming."

The vehicle drew closer.

Beru gasped.

"It's Luke!"

The landspeeder came to a stop outside the homestead.

Luke climbed out first.

R2-D2 rolled down behind him.

C-3PO followed.

A young woman with dark hair stepped out next.

Finally, Luke carefully lowered a black-armored body from the landspeeder.

The unmistakable life-support suit of Darth Vader lay still and silent.

Shmi, now 76 years old, slowly walked forward beside Cliegg, who was now 86.

Owen and Beru followed close behind.

"Luke..."

Shmi embraced him tightly.

"You came back."

"I promised I would, Grandma."

She smiled through tears.

"I never stopped believing."

After everyone had settled inside the homestead, Luke looked around the room.

There was much to explain.

He began quietly.

"Grandma..."

He looked toward the black armor resting nearby.

"You were right."

Shmi waited silently.

"There really was still good in my father."

Luke smiled sadly.

"He redeemed himself."

He lowered his head.

"He died a Jedi."

Shmi closed her eyes.

For several moments she said nothing.

Then she whispered,

"I am proud of you, Ani..."

Silence filled the room.

Luke continued.

"When the Emperor started to torture me with Force lightning, my father let go of the dark side completely and threw the Emperor into a reactor shaft, killing him."

Everyone listened without interrupting.

"Unfortunately," Luke continued, "he sustained too many injuries from the Force lightning himself in the process and died shortly after."

He looked toward Vader's body.

"I brought him here so he can be laid to rest with his family."

Shmi nodded gratefully.

"Thank you, Luke."

Cliegg looked toward the young woman standing beside Luke.

"And may I ask..."

He smiled kindly.

"Who is this girl?"

She stepped forward.

"Hello."

She offered a respectful nod.

"My name is Leia Organa."

Luke looked toward his grandparents.

"She's your granddaughter."

Shmi blinked.

"Granddaughter?"

Luke nodded.

"It turns out that my parents had twins."

He sighed.

"Obi-Wan was still keeping secrets from us even after confirming that my father became Vader."

He shook his head.

"He never told me about my twin sister until very recently."

Luke smiled toward Leia.

"So yes."

"It's her."

"Leia is my twin sister."

Before Leia could react, Shmi wrapped her in a firm embrace.

Leia froze for a moment before slowly returning the hug.

"It's wonderful to meet you," Shmi whispered.

Owen spoke next.

"So what happened to Ben then?"

Luke's smile faded.

"Unfortunately..."

He paused.

"...he encountered Vader again shortly after the duel that you witnessed."

Everyone listened quietly.

"That time Obi-Wan did not perform as well."

Luke lowered his head.

"Vader killed him."

Beru sighed sadly.

"That is so sad."

Luke nodded.

"It is."

A faint smile returned.

"However..."

"He mastered the ability to preserve his consciousness after death."

The family looked surprised.

"Even after losing his physical life, he still occasionally appeared to me as a Force spirit, and we could even still hold conversations sometimes."

Luke looked thoughtfully toward the sky beyond the doorway.

"The Force is truly mysterious..."

He smiled.

"Ah, it can be so powerful when you know how to use it."

Over the following hours, the family remained gathered inside the homestead.

Luke, Leia, R2-D2, and C-3PO described everything that had happened during the previous four years.

They spoke about joining the Rebel Alliance.

They told stories about the first Death Star, about Yavin, about Hoth, about Dagobah, about Cloud City, and about the many friends they had made along the way.

Shmi listened intently to every word.

Occasionally she would ask a question, and Luke or Leia would happily answer it.

Eventually their stories reached the daring mission to rescue Han Solo from Jabba's Palace.

Shmi tilted her head.

"You returned to Tatooine."

Luke nodded.

"We did."

She folded her arms.

"And you couldn't even pay us a visit?"

Luke looked genuinely remorseful.

"I'm sorry, Grandma."

He sighed.

"We were really busy and really stressed at the time."

"We had to get Han out fast, and then we still had Rebel work to do."

Shmi's disappointment remained visible for a moment.

Then she gave a small nod.

"I understand."

She chose not to press the matter further.

The stories continued.

Luke and Leia described the Battle of Endor and the destruction of the second Death Star.

Finally, Luke looked toward Vader's motionless body.

"Then we brought my father's body all the way from Endor to here."

He glanced toward Leia.

"Leia was initially skeptical about it."

He smiled gently.

"She doesn't like Vader at all."

He looked at his sister.

"She still doesn't like him."

Leia nodded honestly.

"I only learned that he was my father when Luke and I learned that we were siblings."

She took a slow breath.

"Luke had known that Vader was his father all this time, but since neither of us knew that I was his twin, I had no way of knowing who my birth father was."

She looked toward the black armor resting quietly nearby.

"Since I only learned this recently, I am still processing this."

She smiled faintly at Luke.

"Luke has had years to process it, while I had... maybe a few days at most."

Luke nodded.

"So I ask that we all understand what Leia is going through right now."

He rested a reassuring hand on her shoulder.

"It's quite a miracle that she actually agreed to come with me to bring my father's body back here."

Shmi and Cliegg both nodded.

Shmi stepped toward Leia once more.

"Dear..."

She smiled warmly.

"Take your time."

She gently took Leia's hands.

"Just know that you now have a family here."

Her eyes shone with kindness.

"We will always welcome you to stay with us."

Cliegg smiled broadly.

"Welcome to the family!"

Leia's eyes became misty.

"Thank you."

She looked around the room at the people who had loved her father long before she had ever known his name.

"It means more than I can say."

Outside, the desert wind swept softly across the moisture farm.

The Empire still existed.

Its power had been shaken, but it had not yet fallen.

Even so, hope had returned to the galaxy.

And on a small homestead on Tatooine, a family that had been separated by decades of silence, loss, and unanswered questions had finally been reunited to the best of its ability.
