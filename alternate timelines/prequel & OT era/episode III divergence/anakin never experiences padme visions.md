# Part 1

Anakin and Padmé had fallen asleep together in Padmé’s apartment, the lights of Coruscant glowing beyond the enormous windows as traffic streamed through the night.

Not long before, after Anakin’s return from the fighting above Coruscant, Padmé had secretly met him inside the Senate Office Building and revealed that she was pregnant. The news had stunned him for only a moment before joy overcame the uncertainty surrounding them. Their marriage remained hidden from the Jedi Order and from the public, and both of them knew the child would eventually force difficult decisions about Padmé’s position in the Senate and Anakin’s place among the Jedi.

For that night, however, Anakin had refused to let those worries overshadow what the news meant to him.

He was going to be a father.

He had called it the happiest moment of his life.

Now Padmé slept beside him, and Anakin slept with her until morning.

Some time later, Chancellor Palpatine summoned Anakin to the Galaxies Opera House.

Anakin arrived during the performance and entered Palpatine’s private box. The Chancellor first gave him encouraging news concerning General Grievous, then quickly turned the conversation toward the Jedi Council.

Palpatine suggested that the Council did not trust either him or Anakin. He pressed Anakin about the uncomfortable position the Jedi had placed him in and correctly guessed that they had asked Anakin to observe the Chancellor on their behalf.

Anakin did not confirm it directly, but his silence told Palpatine enough.

Palpatine then challenged the moral certainty with which the Jedi viewed themselves. He argued that those who possessed power naturally feared losing it and suggested that the Jedi were not as different from their enemies as they claimed.

Anakin objected.

“The Jedi use their power to serve others.”

“And yet they distrust you,” Palpatine replied. “They place you on their Council, refuse you the rank you have earned, and then ask you to deceive someone who has trusted you for years.”

“That doesn’t make them Sith.”

“No,” Palpatine said calmly. “But perhaps the distinction between those who seek power for good and those who seek it for themselves is not always as simple as the Jedi would have you believe.”

That bothered Anakin considerably more than he allowed himself to show.

Palpatine watched him for a moment before changing direction.

He asked whether Anakin had ever heard the tragedy of Darth Plagueis the Wise.

Anakin had not.

Palpatine described an old Sith legend concerning a Dark Lord whose knowledge of the Force had become so great that he could manipulate life itself. Plagueis, he explained, had supposedly discovered methods by which death could be delayed or even prevented for those he cared about. Yet all his power had failed to save him from betrayal by his own apprentice.

Anakin stared at Palpatine.

For a few moments, only the distant music from the performance filled the box.

Then Anakin said, “If that story is true, what Plagueis did was an abuse of the Force.”

Palpatine’s expression remained mild. “An abuse?”

“The Force isn’t something you bend to whatever purpose you want simply because you’re powerful enough to do it. Trying to control life and death like that…” Anakin shook his head. “That is exactly why the Jedi warn us about the dark side.”

Palpatine studied him more carefully now.

“You find no value in such knowledge?”

“I find it dangerous.”

“Even if it could accomplish things the Jedi cannot?”

“That doesn’t make it right.”

Palpatine leaned back slightly.

For the first time during the conversation, Anakin had the distinct impression that the Chancellor had expected a different response.

Palpatine recovered almost immediately.

“Perhaps,” he said. “Though ancient legends have a tendency to become distorted over time. One should not confuse an old story with an endorsement of its subject.”

Anakin continued looking at him.

“You seem to know a lot about it.”

Palpatine smiled faintly.

“I have spent many years surrounded by diplomats, historians, scholars, priests, generals, and politicians from thousands of worlds. One accumulates unusual pieces of knowledge.”

“That’s one way of putting it.”

Palpatine gave a soft laugh and turned his attention back toward the performance.

“Forgive an old politician for indulging his interest in obscure history.”

The subject was dismissed soon afterward.

Anakin left the opera house with considerably more on his mind than General Grievous.

He did not seriously suspect the Chancellor of being anything other than what he appeared to be. Palpatine had led the Republic for years, had access to information from across the galaxy, and had always possessed an extraordinary knowledge of history and politics.

Still, parts of the conversation troubled him.

The Chancellor’s familiarity with Sith philosophy had been strange.

Stranger still was the ease with which Palpatine had spoken of the Jedi and the Sith as though their differences were matters of perspective rather than fundamental principle.

Anakin eventually pushed the questions aside.

Palpatine had always been intellectually curious, and the man had spent years at the center of galactic government. Knowing an obscure Sith legend hardly made him dangerous.

Yet Anakin did not forget the conversation.

Later, when Obi-Wan Kenobi engaged General Grievous, Anakin went to the Chancellor’s office to deliver the report personally.

“Chancellor,” Anakin said as he entered, “we’ve received word from Master Kenobi. He has found Grievous and engaged him.”

Palpatine regarded him thoughtfully.

“Then we must hope Kenobi proves equal to the task.”

“I should be there with him.”

“And yet the Council chose otherwise.”

Anakin’s jaw tightened.

Palpatine rose and walked slowly across the office.

“It continues to trouble me, Anakin, how little they seem to value what you have accomplished.”

Anakin said nothing.

“They deny you the rank of Master. They exclude you from discussions. They send Kenobi after Grievous while keeping you here.”

“I don’t understand all of their decisions,” Anakin admitted.

“Of course you don’t. They do not trust you enough to explain them.”

Anakin looked toward him.

Palpatine continued.

“They know how powerful you have become. Perhaps that frightens them.”

“Or perhaps they think I still have things to learn.”

Palpatine paused.

“That sounds remarkably charitable.”

“They’re the Jedi Council.”

“The same Council that asked you to spy on me.”

Anakin’s expression hardened.

“I didn’t say I agreed with that.”

“But you obeyed them.”

“I’m a Jedi.”

“And how long will loyalty alone be enough for you?”

Anakin frowned.

“What is that supposed to mean?”

Palpatine approached him slowly.

“You have spent years proving yourself. You have fought their war, risked your life countless times, and done things most Jedi could never accomplish. Yet whenever you approach the position your abilities deserve, the Council finds another reason to hold you back.”

Anakin looked away.

Some of the words struck close to resentments he had already voiced privately.

Palpatine saw it.

“They underestimate you.”

“They don’t always understand me,” Anakin said. “That isn’t the same thing.”

“They distrust you.”

“They distrust you too.”

The answer came quickly enough that Palpatine went still.

Anakin turned back toward him.

“And lately you seem very interested in reminding me of everything I dislike about them.”

Palpatine’s face revealed nothing.

“I am interested in seeing you treated fairly.”

“I know.”

Anakin hesitated.

“You’ve always supported me. I haven’t forgotten that.”

Palpatine gave a small, approving nod.

“But?”

Anakin thought back to the opera house.

“To be honest, Chancellor, sometimes I don’t know what you want me to think about the Jedi.”

Palpatine regarded him in silence.

“You tell me they’re afraid of losing power. You compare them to the Sith. You tell me they’re deliberately keeping knowledge from me.” Anakin shook his head. “Maybe they’ve made mistakes with me. Maybe I’ve made mistakes with them. But I’m still one of them.”

Palpatine’s expression softened into that of the patient mentor Anakin had known for years.

“Of course you are.”

Anakin watched him carefully.

Palpatine continued in a quieter voice.

“I have no desire to turn you against anyone. I merely dislike seeing someone of your ability diminished by those who should recognize his accomplishments.”

Anakin relaxed slightly, though not completely.

“I appreciate that.”

“And perhaps I have spoken too strongly.”

Anakin almost smiled.

“That would be a first.”

Palpatine chuckled.

“Even Chancellors are occasionally guilty of excessive conviction.”

The tension eased.

Anakin inclined his head.

“I should return to the Temple. If Master Kenobi sends another report, the Council will need to hear it.”

“Naturally.”

Anakin started toward the door.

“Anakin.”

He stopped and looked back.

Palpatine gave him the familiar, reassuring smile he had worn throughout so many years of their friendship.

“Whatever disagreements you may have with the Council, never doubt your own abilities.”

Anakin considered that for a moment.

“I don’t.”

Then he left.

The doors closed behind him.

Palpatine remained alone in the office, motionless and silent.

For the moment, he had said enough.

# Part 2

Palpatine remained alone in his office long after Anakin had gone.

For years, he had wanted Anakin Skywalker at his side. He had cultivated the young Jedi’s trust, encouraged his ambitions, fed his frustrations, and watched his power grow. Anakin’s potential was extraordinary, and Palpatine still desired to make use of it.

But desire was not necessity.

Their recent conversations had made one fact increasingly difficult to ignore. However much Anakin resented the Jedi Council, however deeply he believed they underestimated him, his loyalty to the Jedi remained intact. Each time Palpatine had pushed, Anakin had eventually defended them. If Palpatine continued pressing too aggressively, he risked provoking suspicion before Anakin was prepared to abandon the Order.

That risk was becoming unacceptable.

Palpatine had spent too many years constructing his plans to endanger everything over one prospective apprentice, no matter how powerful.

Anakin was someone he wanted.

He was not someone he needed.

After a long period of contemplation, Palpatine made his decision. He would stop trying to force the issue. He would proceed without Anakin.

Reports soon reached Coruscant that Obi-Wan Kenobi had successfully defeated General Grievous on Utapau.

With Count Dooku and General Grievous both dead within the span of only a few days, the military position of the Separatists had become increasingly hopeless. The end of the Clone Wars could no longer be postponed indefinitely.

Palpatine knew what that meant for him as well.

Over the course of the war, the Senate had granted him several emergency powers. He had repeatedly assured the Republic that those powers were temporary and that they would be relinquished once the crisis had passed. In particular, he had allowed it to be understood that the defeat of General Grievous would mark the point at which those extraordinary authorities were no longer necessary.

Now Grievous was dead.

Sooner or later, senators would begin demanding that Palpatine fulfill those assurances.

He therefore had little time left.

Yet he did not immediately move against the Jedi.

Palpatine wanted the Jedi Order discredited before it was destroyed. If the public were to believe that the Jedi had become traitors, then the Jedi themselves had to appear to make the first move.

So Palpatine waited.

A few days passed.

He made no announcement surrendering his emergency powers. He offered no timetable for restoring the powers of the Senate. The authority accumulated by the Chancellor’s office remained exactly where it was.

Inside the Jedi Temple, concern hardened into action.

The Council had already discussed what would happen if Grievous were defeated and Palpatine still refused to relinquish his wartime powers. Mace Windu had warned that the dark side surrounded the Chancellor and that he sensed a plot against the Jedi. Ki-Adi-Mundi had argued that, should Palpatine refuse to surrender his emergency authority after Grievous’s defeat, he would have to be removed from office. Mace had acknowledged that such a step could require the Jedi Council to assume temporary control of the Senate in order to secure a peaceful transition. Yoda had warned that such thinking led toward a dangerous place and urged great caution.

Now the condition they had discussed had come to pass.

Grievous was dead.

Palpatine remained in possession of his emergency powers.

The Jedi decided that the time had come to act.

Mace Windu, Kit Fisto, Agen Kolar, and Saesee Tiin departed the Jedi Temple and headed for the Chancellor’s office.

Palpatine sensed them approaching through the Force.

He rose from his chair.

The office’s security cameras went dark.

Then Palpatine prepared an audio recorder and waited.

A few moments later, the doors opened.

The four Jedi Masters entered.

Mace stepped forward.

“General Grievous is dead. The war is ending. The emergency powers granted to your office must now be returned to the Senate.”

Palpatine looked calmly at him.

“And what if I refuse?”

Mace held his gaze.

“Then, in that case...”

Palpatine activated the audio recorder.

Mace continued.

“...we are going to remove you from office, and we will take control of the Senate.”

Palpatine’s expression hardened.

“You have no right to do that, Master Jedi! I will not allow myself to be removed from my rightful office of Supreme Chancellor.”

“Then you leave us with no other choice.”

Mace Windu ignited his lightsaber.

Kit Fisto, Agen Kolar, and Saesee Tiin did the same.

The sharp snap-hiss of four blades filled the office.

Palpatine stared at them.

“It’s treason, then.”

He switched off the recorder.

A lightsaber dropped into his hand.

A crimson blade erupted from it.

For an instant, all four Jedi were shocked.

The Chancellor of the Republic stood before them holding a red lightsaber.

But decades of Jedi training asserted itself almost immediately. Their reflexes, already honed through years of discipline and sharpened still further through three years commanding troops in the Clone Wars, overcame their surprise.

Palpatine attacked.

Agen Kolar and Saesee Tiin fell almost immediately. Neither was able to withstand the speed and ferocity of Palpatine’s assault.

Kit Fisto survived for several seconds longer.

Then Palpatine cut him down as well.

Only Mace Windu remained.

The two fought on.

Their blades crashed together again and again as the duel carried across the Chancellor’s office. Mace met Palpatine strike for strike, and for a long time neither man was able to force a decisive advantage.

Eventually, Palpatine prevailed.

Mace Windu was defeated and killed.

Palpatine did not pause.

He moved immediately to the HoloNet transmitter and opened a military channel to the clone commanders of the Grand Army of the Republic.

His voice went out across the galaxy.

“Execute Order 66.”

The transmission ended.

Palpatine then sent a copy of the audio recording from his office to the Senate.

Immediately afterward, he opened a public broadcast to the entire Republic.

His image appeared across countless HoloNet receivers throughout the galaxy.

“Good evening, fellow citizens of the Galactic Republic! I have an important announcement to make. The Jedi have just betrayed me. They tried to remove me from my office and take control of the Senate by force. They tried to stage a coup against me and against the Republic. They are traitors of the Republic! They are now enemies of the Republic!”

Palpatine paused.

“Fortunately, their attempt failed, and the Jedi who came to try to remove me have been neutralized. I have preserved a recording of the coup attempt, which I invite you to listen to now.”

The broadcast switched to the recording.

Mace Windu’s voice came first.

“We are going to remove you from office, and we will take control of the Senate.”

Then Palpatine’s.

“You have no right to do that, Master Jedi! I will not allow myself to be removed from my rightful office of Supreme Chancellor.”

Mace answered.

“Then you leave us with no other choice.”

The unmistakable sounds of four lightsaber blades igniting followed.

Then Palpatine’s final words on the recording:

“It’s treason, then.”

The recording ended.

Palpatine reappeared.

“We must act quickly to eliminate all remaining Jedi. If we don’t, they will simply try again soon, and if we don’t stop them once and for all, then eventually they may succeed, and the galaxy will fall into a tyranny led by the Jedi Order! Thank you for your attention, and may peace and victory be upon us!”

The broadcast ended.

# Part 3

Meanwhile, after leaving Chancellor Palpatine’s office that day, Anakin returned to the Jedi Temple.

He would have preferred to remain on Coruscant.

Padmé was there, carrying their child, and every departure from the capital now felt different from those that had come before. For years, Anakin had accepted that war meant being sent wherever the Republic needed him. Now, whenever he looked toward another campaign, part of him could only think about the family waiting for him at home.

But the Clone Wars had not yet ended.

Not long afterward, the Jedi Council ordered Anakin back into the field.

He went.

The force under his command was smaller than the 501st Legion he had once led as a whole. Not long before, Anakin and Ahsoka Tano had divided the legion into two separate forces. Ahsoka had taken one half, reorganized as the 332nd Division, along with Captain Rex, to Mandalore in an attempt to liberate the planet from Maul’s control.

Anakin retained the other half under Clone Commander Appo.

With them, he returned once more to war.

Their next campaign brought them to Travyx Prime, where a large Separatist droid army had entrenched itself against the Republic advance.

The battle was already raging across the surface when Anakin led the 501st forward.

Blasterfire streaked through the air from both directions. Republic artillery pounded the Separatist lines while ranks of battle droids advanced through smoke and debris, firing in disciplined volleys. Clone troopers returned fire from shattered defensive positions and behind armored vehicles, their white armor stained by dust and scorched by the fighting.

Anakin was where he had been countless times before: at the front.

His blue lightsaber flashed through the haze as he moved ahead of his men, batting aside incoming bolts and carving through any battle droids that came within reach.

“Push them back!” Anakin shouted. “Appo, move the left flank forward!”

“Yes, General!”

Commander Appo signaled to the nearby squads.

The clones obeyed immediately.

They had fought beside Anakin for years. He knew how they moved, how they reacted under fire, how quickly they could reorganize when a position collapsed. They knew him just as well. When Anakin charged ahead, they followed. When he pointed toward an opening in the enemy line, they trusted that he had seen something worth exploiting.

It was as familiar to him as anything in the war could still be.

Then, in the middle of the battle, something changed.

Anakin felt it before he understood it.

A sudden warning surged through the Force.

His body moved on instinct.

He spun.

The clone troopers nearest him had turned away from the Separatist lines.

Every blaster was pointed at him.

Anakin’s eyes widened.

They fired.

His lightsaber snapped around in a blur.

Red bolts struck the blade one after another, ricocheting away from him in all directions. Some vanished harmlessly into the ground. Others scorched the wreckage nearby. One deflected shot slammed into a battle droid behind the clones and blew through its torso.

Anakin stumbled backward, stunned more by the source of the attack than by the attack itself.

“What are you doing?”

No one answered.

Commander Appo raised his weapon.

So did every clone around him.

They fired again.

Anakin deflected another storm of blaster bolts.

“Appo!”

Nothing.

Not even hesitation.

The clones who had obeyed his commands moments earlier were now trying to kill him.

There was no warning, no accusation, no explanation. Their expressions were hidden behind their helmets, and their movements were cold and immediate.

More blasterfire erupted from the Separatist lines.

Anakin twisted aside as droid fire joined the barrage.

For a few chaotic seconds, he found himself caught between both armies.

The battle droids were still attacking the Republic.

The clones were still fighting the Separatists.

But every clone who had a clear shot at Anakin was firing at him as well.

Anakin slashed through another incoming volley and threw out one hand. The Force hurled several nearby clones backward, knocking them from their feet without killing them.

“What is wrong with you?”

Again, there was no response.

The clones simply began getting back up.

Anakin’s confusion lasted only another moment.

Then survival took over.

Whatever had happened, he could not solve it here.

If he remained where he was, either the clones would eventually overwhelm him or the Separatist droids would catch him in the crossfire. There were too many weapons aimed in too many directions, and more clone units were already turning toward him.

He had to get away.

Now.

Anakin turned and ran.

The Force surged through his body.

His first few strides carried him beyond ordinary human speed. Then he pushed harder.

The battlefield blurred around him.

He sprinted across broken ground, leaping over debris and shattered machinery as blasterfire streaked past. Bolts struck the earth behind his boots. Clone troopers pursued him while battle droids continued advancing through the confusion, firing at both Anakin and the Republic forces.

Anakin did not look back.

He concentrated on speed.

Smoke whipped past him. The noise of the battle began to diminish as he widened the distance between himself and the main engagement.

He crossed a low ridge and kept going until the blasterfire behind him had become distant enough that he could no longer distinguish individual shots.

Only then did he slow.

He could still sense pursuit.

Not close.

But close enough.

There was nowhere obvious to hide. The terrain offered no structure, vehicle, or intact emplacement he could trust. If the clones were searching for him from the air, simply crouching behind a ridge would accomplish nothing.

Anakin looked down.

Then an idea came to him.

He extended both hands toward the ground.

The Force struck the earth.

Dirt, stone, and broken layers of compacted ground erupted outward as a deep hole opened beneath him. Anakin widened it rapidly, forcing the debris aside until he had created enough space below the surface to conceal himself.

He jumped down.

Above him, the opening remained exposed to the sky.

Anakin reached upward with the Force.

The displaced earth shifted.

Chunks of stone slid back into place. Loose soil poured over them. Anakin carefully pulled more debris across the opening until the surface was sealed over him.

Darkness swallowed the space.

He stood beneath the battlefield, buried beneath a roof of dirt and stone.

For several seconds, he heard nothing but his own breathing.

Then the distant battle returned through the ground as faint vibrations.

Anakin extinguished his lightsaber.

He waited.

Above, the clones searching for him eventually lost his trail.

The battle droids understood even less of what had just happened. They had been fighting the Republic forces before Anakin disappeared, and nothing about their orders had changed. To them, the clone army remained the enemy.

So the Separatist attack continued.

With Anakin gone, the clones turned their full attention back toward the droids.

The battle for Travyx Prime resumed without its Jedi commander.

Republic formations reorganized under Commander Appo. The Separatists pressed their attack. Blasterfire once again crossed the battlefield in uninterrupted volleys, and the fighting dragged on without Anakin directing the clone advance from the front.

Even so, the clones continued to fight with the discipline and coordination drilled into them throughout the war.

Eventually, they broke the Separatist army.

The victory was narrow, but it was complete.

The last organized droid formations were destroyed. Remaining units were hunted down until resistance ceased across the battlefield.

Once the fighting had ended, Commander Appo issued his next orders.

Every Separatist ship still present on Travyx Prime was to be destroyed.

The clones carried out the command.

One after another, the remaining Separatist vessels were rendered unusable, leaving no functioning enemy transport behind.

Then the surviving clone forces withdrew.

They boarded the Republic ships that had brought them to Travyx Prime and departed the planet.

They left Anakin behind.

Hours passed before he finally decided it was safe to emerge.

Beneath the surface, Anakin had little means of knowing precisely what was happening above him. He could sense that the violence had gradually diminished, then stopped. He waited longer anyway.

At last, he raised one hand.

The makeshift roof above him began to shift.

Stone lifted. Dirt peeled away. A narrow opening formed, and daylight spilled into the concealed chamber.

Anakin climbed out.

The battlefield was silent.

Smoke drifted across the devastated ground.

Destroyed battle droids lay scattered everywhere. Some had been sliced apart during the earlier fighting, while others had been shattered by blasterfire or explosions. Burned-out vehicles stood motionless among the wreckage.

There were no clone formations.

No transports.

No Republic ships waiting for him.

Anakin looked toward the places where the Separatist vessels had been positioned.

Those were gone as well.

Or rather, what remained of them was useless.

The clones had destroyed every Separatist ship before leaving.

Anakin slowly turned, taking in the empty battlefield.

The battle had ended.

The Republic had won.

And the 501st had departed without him.

He was stranded on Travyx Prime.

For the first time since the attack had begun, Anakin had time to think about what had actually happened.

Every clone around him had turned against him at almost exactly the same moment.

Not one had questioned it.

Not one had demanded his surrender.

Not one had tried to explain what crime he had supposedly committed.

They had simply raised their weapons and opened fire.

Appo had done it too.

This had not looked like panic, confusion, or some battlefield misunderstanding.

It had looked like an order.

Anakin’s thoughts returned to Coruscant.

To the Galaxies Opera House.

To Palpatine telling him that the Jedi Council did not trust him.

To the Chancellor arguing that the Jedi feared losing power.

To his unsettling comparison between the Jedi and the Sith.

Then Anakin remembered their later conversation in the Chancellor’s office.

Palpatine had again pressed at every grievance between Anakin and the Council. He had insisted that the Jedi underestimated him, distrusted him, and deliberately held him back.

And when Anakin had finally challenged him—

*They distrust you too.*

Palpatine had backed away.

At the time, Anakin had accepted his explanation.

Palpatine had always supported him. He had been Anakin’s friend for years. Even the Chancellor’s strange interest in Sith history had seemed explainable. A man who had spent so long at the center of galactic politics would naturally have accumulated knowledge of obscure subjects.

Standing alone amid the wreckage of Travyx Prime, those conversations no longer felt quite so easy to dismiss.

Anakin looked toward the empty sky where the Republic ships had disappeared.

He still had no idea why his own soldiers had suddenly tried to kill him.

He did not know what order they had received.

He did not know who had given it.

But for the first time, a possibility entered his mind that he could no longer simply brush aside.

Somehow, Chancellor Palpatine might be behind this.

# Part 4

Across the galaxy, the command spread almost instantaneously.

Clone troopers who had fought beside Jedi generals for years suddenly turned their weapons upon them.

The betrayal came without warning.

On Mygeeto, Ki-Adi-Mundi was leading his troops through the freezing ruins when the clones behind him stopped advancing. He turned at the sudden silence, only to find their blasters already raised. He managed to deflect the first shots, but the sheer volume of fire overwhelmed him within moments.

On Cato Neimoidia, Plo Koon never had the opportunity to defend himself. Clone pilots opened fire on his starfighter in the middle of flight, blasting it from the sky.

On Saleucami, Stass Allie was killed when the soldiers serving under her command suddenly fired upon her speeder, causing it to explode.

Across dozens of battlefronts, similar scenes unfolded. Jedi who had trusted their clone troops with their lives were shot from behind, surrounded, blasted from vehicles, or overwhelmed by entire units acting with sudden and absolute coordination.

Yet not every target died.

On Kashyyyk, Yoda felt the catastrophe through the Force before the clones near him could carry out their orders. The deaths of Jedi throughout the galaxy struck him like a great wave of pain. That warning gave him the instant he needed. When the clones turned on him, Yoda was already moving, and their attack failed.

On Utapau, Obi-Wan Kenobi had only recently survived his confrontation with General Grievous when Commander Cody and the troopers of the 212th Attack Battalion suddenly pointed their weapons at him.

Obi-Wan narrowly escaped.

Neither Jedi initially understood the cause, but both knew that a disaster is unfolding.

Yoda and Obi-Wan immediately began making their way back toward Coruscant.

At the Jedi Temple itself, only two members of the Council remained: Shaak Ti and Coleman Kcaj.

Around them were a sizeable number of other Jedi Masters and Knights, along with Padawans and a large group of younglings. Many of the Order's most experienced members were away commanding Republic forces, leaving those who remained to protect the Temple and those inside it.

Then Palpatine's public broadcast appeared across the HoloNet.

The Jedi listened as the Supreme Chancellor accused their Order of attempting to overthrow him and seize control of the Senate. They heard the recording of Mace Windu declaring that Palpatine would be removed from office and that the Jedi would take control of the Senate. They heard four lightsabers ignite.

Then came Palpatine's declaration that the Jedi were enemies of the Republic.

Confusion swept through the Temple.

No one there knew precisely what had happened inside the Chancellor's office.

But they understood what Palpatine's announcement meant.

Danger was coming.

Shaak Ti and Coleman Kcaj immediately organized the Temple's defenses. Jedi took positions throughout the halls and entrance chambers. Knights and Masters prepared to meet whatever forces might arrive. Padawans were gathered together, while the younglings were moved away from the main approaches.

They did not have long.

Palpatine gathered several units of clone troopers stationed on Coruscant and personally led them toward the Jedi Temple.

The assault began soon afterward.

Clone troops poured into the Temple beneath heavy covering fire, meeting lightsabers almost immediately. Jedi defenders cut down the first ranks, deflecting blaster bolts back into the attacking formations and forcing the clones to fight for every corridor.

But the clones were not alone.

Palpatine entered the Temple with them.

His red lightsaber came alive in his hand.

Any hope that the Jedi defenders might hold their ground vanished quickly.

Jedi Knights who could have carved through ordinary soldiers found themselves facing a Sith Lord of terrifying speed and power. Palpatine tore through defensive positions before the Jedi could reorganize. His blade flashed from one opponent to another, and every attempt to surround him collapsed beneath the ferocity of his attacks.

The clones advanced behind him.

Shaak Ti and Coleman Kcaj fought with the other Masters in defense of the Temple, but neither they nor the Jedi around them could stop him.

The fighting spread deeper into the building.

Blasterfire echoed through ancient halls that had stood as the center of the Jedi Order for generations. Lightsabers ignited and went dark. Clone boots thundered across polished floors. Doors were blasted open. Chambers became battlefields.

The defenders fell one after another.

Masters.

Knights.

Padawans.

Younglings.

By the time the fighting ended, no Jedi remained alive inside the Temple.

Palpatine stood amid the devastation.

The Temple had been secured.

He no longer needed the clone troops.

He ordered them away.

The soldiers departed, leaving Palpatine alone within the conquered heart of the Jedi Order.

He walked slowly through its halls.

Smoke drifted through the corridors. Scorch marks covered walls and pillars. Bodies lay where Jedi had made their final stands.

Palpatine moved through it all with undisguised satisfaction.

For years, the Jedi Temple had stood at the center of an Order that had opposed the Sith for generations.

Now Palpatine walked through it alone.

The Temple belonged to him.

By the time Yoda and Obi-Wan reached Coruscant, they had already exchanged everything they knew.

During their return journeys, they had made contact with each other, and each had described the moment the clones turned against him.

Their separate experiences told them enough.

This was no isolated mutiny.

The clones had turned against the Jedi on Kashyyyk.

They had turned against the Jedi on Utapau.

If the same thing was happening elsewhere, then something catastrophic had occurred throughout the Republic.

They arrived on Coruscant at roughly the same time and quickly met.

Neither wasted words.

Together, they headed for the Jedi Temple.

They saw the smoke before they reached it.

The great structure loomed ahead of them, damaged and blackened, smoke pouring from parts of the complex.

Threepio approached more cautiously.

Then Leia.

“I must admit, they are kind of cute.”

His expression brightened.

“Failed to stop the Sith Lord, we have. Almost defeated us, he has. Strong, he is. Confront him, we cannot. Confront him, you should not try either.”

“He is that strong?”

He stared.

“Who is that?”

“You have a daughter?!”

Obi-Wan stared at him.

Yoda stared at him.

Anakin waited.

Anakin secretly having a wife and two children barely registered on the same scale.

“...Well, I definitely suspected you had something going on with Senator Amidala.”

“You did?”

“No, you WILL be less safe than them. They are not married to a Jedi. You are.”

“What if you die?”

“And the children? They will grow up without their mother.”

“Listen, Padmé. I know where you are coming from. But Bail and Mon are neither married to a Jedi nor new parents of two babies. Don’t compare yourself to them. You’re not in the same position as them.”

“...Maybe you are right.”

“Stay here with me, Padmé. Stay here with Luke and Leia. Don’t go back to Coruscant. Please, promise me this.”

Padmé looked at him for a long moment.

“...Fine. You have a point.”

“Promise?”

“Promise.”

He and Padmé were together near the hut when he finally said it.

“Yoda and Obi-Wan said I’m not ready to face Palpatine. When do you think I will be ready?”

Padmé looked at him carefully.

“Not anytime soon, I think.”

“If there was only one thing that Palpatine may have gotten right, it’s that the Jedi consistently underestimate my powers.”

“Just because they can’t beat Palpatine doesn’t mean I can’t.”

Padmé continued.

“You heard Yoda and Obi-Wan loud and clear. Palpatine is unlike anyone either of them have faced before. If you go and try to confront him, you may not come back alive.”

“...Not even, actually.”

“What?”

“We need to at least wait until public sentiment changes.”

“When?”

“I don’t know.”

Padmé looked out toward Nymeve Lake.

“But if Palpatine’s regime turns out to be cruel and tyrannical, then sooner or later public opinion will turn against him. People will start reminiscing about the Republic as ‘good old times’.”

“THEN it may be smart to give killing him a shot. Because then if it succeeds, the galaxy may actually celebrate you as a hero or a liberator, rather than a traitor.”

Anakin considered the argument carefully.

“Good point. You definitely think much more about the political side of things than I do.”

Padmé smiled.

“That comes with being a politician, I guess.”

Anakin chuckled.

“I guess.”

“For now, just focus on raising Luke and Leia.”

Anakin looked toward the twins.

“Once they are older,” Padmé continued, “maybe you can train them in the Force. Then, perhaps, when the people of the galaxy do one day turn against Palpatine and rise up against the Empire, you wouldn’t need to face Palpatine alone.”

“You mean Luke and Leia?”

“You are proposing sending our children to a mission against the most powerful Sith Lord we know about?”

Padmé was definitely not one of the people underestimating his powers.

“Exactly. Train them first, then we can revisit this again one day.”

Nearby, Luke and Leia lay quietly.

Being infants, they had heard the entire conversation.

They simply had no idea that their parents were discussing their future fate.

And possibly the fate of the galaxy.

# Part 7

It did not take long for the galaxy to notice Padmé Amidala’s disappearance.

Her last public appearance had been at the special session of the Senate in which Palpatine announced the end of the Republic and the creation of the First Galactic Empire. Padmé had attended that session, listened to the proclamation, and then vanished from public life without explanation.

No resignation was announced.

No public statement followed.

No one seemed to know where she had gone.

Before long, rumors began spreading across the HoloNet.

Some claimed Padmé had withdrawn permanently from politics. Others suggested that she had suffered some sort of breakdown following the destruction of the political system she had spent so much of her life defending.

Then one explanation began attracting more attention than the others.

Padmé Amidala, according to increasingly widespread reports, had died from profound grief over the fall of the Republic.

The media quickly found a dramatic phrase for it.

“Heartbreak over the Republic.”

The theory gained traction precisely because Padmé’s devotion to democracy had never been a secret. Throughout her years in the Senate, she had openly resisted the concentration of power in the Chancellor’s office, defended constitutional government, and argued repeatedly against measures she believed threatened the Republic’s democratic foundations.

To many citizens, the story sounded almost plausible.

Palpatine was less convinced.

He knew Padmé too well to accept a convenient rumor without question.

By that point, Bail Organa had returned to Coruscant and resumed his place in the Senate. Mon Mothma had remained there as well.

Palpatine knew both were close friends and political allies of Padmé.

He summoned them.

Bail and Mon appeared before him together.

Palpatine regarded them calmly.

“Senator Amidala has not been seen since the special session.”

Neither answered immediately.

“There are rumors that she is dead,” Palpatine continued. “Do either of you know where she is?”

Bail shook his head.

“No, Emperor. I haven’t seen Padmé since the session.”

Mon gave the same answer.

“Neither have I.”

Palpatine watched them both carefully.

“You have received no messages from her?”

“No,” Bail said.

“No,” Mon repeated.

Palpatine remained silent for several moments.

Then he dismissed the matter.

“Very well.”

He allowed them to leave.

In the end, Palpatine chose to believe them.

Perhaps Padmé truly had died.

Perhaps she had simply disappeared somewhere beyond public view.

Either way, he saw little reason to spend further effort on the question. Padmé had abandoned Coruscant, vanished from politics, and ceased to pose any meaningful threat to his rule.

As far as Palpatine was concerned, she was no longer worth pursuing.

On Takodana, Anakin and Padmé learned about the rumors through the HoloNet.

The two sat together inside their lakeside hut as a news report discussed Padmé’s disappearance and repeated the increasingly popular claim that the former senator had died from “heartbreak over the Republic.”

Anakin looked over at her.

“‘Heartbreak over the Republic’? Well, that does sound like you.”

Padmé gave him a look.

Anakin raised his hands slightly.

“No, I’m serious.”

“You really think I would die over the Republic no longer existing?”

“Knowing you, probably.”

Padmé stared at him.

“I think that’s a bit extreme.”

Anakin grinned.

“Yes, I’m very sad about what happened,” Padmé continued. “But definitely not to the point of dying. I think the news is being overly dramatic about me.”

“Well, if the rumors have gained so much traction that they’ve made it into the news, maybe you really gave off the kind of vibes that suggest that you would die of ‘heartbreak over the Republic.’”

Padmé considered that.

“Hm.”

Anakin waited.

“Perhaps true,” she admitted. “I guess I’ve always come off as someone who is overly attached to my senatorial work.”

“Exactly. That’s my point.”

Padmé shook her head, though the beginnings of a smile appeared.

They left the matter there.

Privately, both found the rumor somewhat amusing.

Underneath that amusement, however, was relief.

If much of the galaxy genuinely believed Padmé might already be dead, then fewer people would be searching for her. Her disappearance became easier to explain. Her continued absence attracted less suspicion.

For someone trying to remain hidden, being mistakenly mourned had its advantages.

A few days later, Padmé’s comlink signaled an incoming call.

She checked the source.

Her expression changed immediately.

“It’s my parents!”

Anakin looked over.

“They have probably heard about the rumors and are worried about me,” Padmé said. “I think I should pick up.”

She reached toward the transmitter.

“Wait, Padmé.”

Her hand stopped.

Anakin frowned thoughtfully.

“I just realized we have never really discussed who we should reveal ourselves to, and who we should give our location to.”

Padmé slowly lowered her hand.

“Our location, and even our survival at all, should be a heavily guarded secret,” Anakin continued. “I’m not saying your parents shouldn’t know, but we should take some time to discuss exactly who should know, and how much they should know, first. So just hold off on that call.”

Padmé glanced at the still-ringing transmitter.

Then back at him.

“...Good point. Let’s do that.”

Eventually, the transmitter stopped ringing.

Anakin leaned back.

“Okay. So Obi-Wan, Yoda, and Bail already know we are alive and hiding on Takodana. So since they already know everything, there’s not much to discuss about them.”

“Exactly.”

“I think I want Ahsoka and Rex to join this group. I trust them enough that I think giving them our location so they can visit us in person is fine.”

Padmé hesitated.

“Wouldn’t Rex have betrayed the Jedi just like all the other clones?”

Anakin’s expression changed.

“Good point.”

He thought for a moment.

“I don’t know. Ahsoka would know, since she would presumably have been with him when the galaxy-wide clone betrayal happened. I guess we can ask her about Rex later.”

“Okay. So if Rex turns out fine, then we can let him in on our secrets.”

Padmé paused.

“If he turns out to be like all the other clones...”

She did not finish.

She did not need to.

Anakin understood.

“And to be honest,” he said, “I think that’s it. That should be everyone who knows our location. Nobody else.”

“So my parents won’t know.”

“Yeah. I think your family should probably not know about Takodana.”

“So we’re settled on these being everyone who should know about our survival?”

“To summarize, Obi-Wan, Yoda, Bail, and Ahsoka know both our survival and our location. Rex as well, if he hasn’t betrayed the Jedi.”

“Then, my family, Sabé and Dormé, Jar Jar, and Mon know our survival but not our location.”

“It’s great to see you too, Padmé. Where have you been? The entire galaxy has been looking for you.”

“You can’t tell anyone else about this, but we’re hiding.”

Jobal leaned into view beside him.

“But you’re not a Jedi.”

“Married?”

“We didn’t know you were married.”

“...Right. You don’t know.”

“Yes, I married Anakin Skywalker.”

Ruwee’s expression shifted with recognition.

“That Jedi that you brought to our home three years ago? After those assassination attempts on you?”

“I remember telling you that the two of you looked like you were in love with each other, but both of you vehemently denied it at the time. You said he was only your Jedi bodyguard, assigned to protect you.”

“Mom, it was complicated at the time, okay?”

“Dear, stop teasing her. She’s probably not in the mood for it right now.”

“Well, thanks for telling us now. At least we can understand why you would need to be hiding now.”

“Is he with you?”

“Hello.”

Jobal smiled immediately.

“Oh, hi Anakin! Nice to see you.”

“Nice to see you too.”

“Please take care of our daughter.”

Anakin nodded.

“Of course. I will.”

Padmé smiled.

“By the way, you have two more grandchildren now.”

Ruwee blinked again.

“What?”

“Meet Luke and Leia.”

Anakin disappeared briefly from view, then returned with both children.

He carefully positioned them where Padmé’s parents could see.

Jobal’s face lit up.

“Wow, so cute! How old are they?”

Ruwee looked at the twins for a long moment.

“I’m sure they will grow up in a happy family.”

“Thank you.”

“By the way, where are you hiding? Can we come visit you in person?”

7-year-old Pooja followed closely.

“Auntie Padmé!!!”

“Then we will see each other again soon.”

# Part 8

A few days later, Anakin finally contacted Ahsoka.

She answered almost immediately.

Her familiar face appeared above the transmitter, and Anakin smiled with obvious relief.

“Ahsoka! How did Mandalore go? Did the clones do... anything to you?”

“Yes, they did. And I think I know why.”

“He’s okay now, but for a moment he lost himself too.”

Ahsoka rubbed the side of her head.

“Okay, this is really complicated. Let me think about how to tell it to you...”

Ahsoka looked up.

“We’re hiding on Takodana now,” Anakin continued. “So just come here and we can talk all about it. Take Rex with you.”

“Hang on. Let me add him to this call.”

“Captain Rex. Good to see you.”

Ahsoka answered before Anakin could.

“Anakin wanted to invite you to come to Takodana. He has been hiding there since Order 66.”

“What’s Order 66?”

Ahsoka glanced toward Rex.

“We’ll explain once we get to your place.”

Anakin nodded.

“So are you coming, Rex?”

“Yes, of course, General.”

“Call me Anakin. Please. The war is over.”

Rex paused.

Then the corner of his mouth lifted slightly.

“My pleasure... Anakin.”

Anakin described the lakeside home and transmitted its coordinates to both of them.

The call ended shortly afterward.

Ahsoka reached Takodana first.

Her ship descended toward Nymeve Lake and settled near the coordinates Anakin had provided.

She climbed out, looked around, and frowned.

There was no hut.

There was no sign of Anakin or Padmé.

There was only the lake.

Ahsoka stared at the coordinates again.

Then she looked across the water.

“Oh, come on, Skyguy, you gave me the wrong coordinates!”

She considered contacting him immediately.

Instead, she decided to look around first.

Anakin had specifically said that he was living beside Nymeve Lake. If the coordinates were wrong but the lake itself was correct, perhaps she could simply follow the shoreline until she found the hut.

So she started walking.

The shore stretched onward through the forest, peaceful and largely empty. Ahsoka continued for some time before an unexpected structure appeared ahead.

An ancient-looking castle rose beside the lake.

Ahsoka slowed.

She certainly had not expected to find anything like that here.

Curiosity won.

She approached the castle and stepped inside.

Within, she soon encountered an elderly alien woman who regarded her with kind, perceptive eyes.

The alien walked over to greet her.

“Hello, young traveller. What brings you here?”

“I’m just here to meet a friend.”

“Oh, a friend? On this planet? Not many people live here.”

“I know.”

Ahsoka looked around the castle again.

“Who are you, by the way?”

“My name is Maz Kanata.”

Ahsoka smiled.

“Nice castle you’ve got here! How long have you lived here?”

Maz seemed amused by the question.

“I have been living in this castle for a long, long time already. Longer than you have been alive, probably.”

Ahsoka’s eyes widened.

“Wow!”

Then she felt something.

She looked at Maz more closely.

There was something familiar in the sensation surrounding the old woman.

“The Force feels... strong in you.”

Maz’s expression sharpened with interest.

“Do you know the Force?”

“Know the Force?” Maz replied. “Oh, I know much more about the Force than you think. I may be no Jedi, but I’m certainly no less acquainted with the Force!”

Ahsoka laughed softly.

“Well, I guess we’re in the same club then. I’m also not a Jedi. Well, no longer one. I used to be one, though.”

“Interesting.”

Maz studied her.

“Not many Force-sensitives come through here often. Good to meet one!”

“I’m definitely going to tell my friend about you. I’m sure he will like you.”

“Well, I’ll be looking forward to that.”

Ahsoka glanced deeper into the castle.

“Do you mind if I stay here for a bit?”

“Of course! Everyone is free to stay here for as long as they need!”

Ahsoka smiled.

“Wow, that is very generous of you. Thank you!”

She spent some time exploring the castle before contacting Rex.

His image appeared.

“Rex, I think Anakin gave us the wrong coordinates. It’s still by the lakeside, but not right beside his hut. Come to Maz Kanata’s castle instead. We will go find Anakin together.”

“No problem, Commander.”

Palpatine praising him.

Encouraging him.

Taking an interest in his frustrations.

“Qui-Gon was different. He never forgot the true calling of the Force. He truly understood what it means to be a good Jedi.”

# Part 10

Six years passed.

On Takodana, life beside Nymeve Lake settled into a rhythm that had once seemed impossible for the Skywalker family.

Luke and Leia were now 6 years old.

They had grown up far from Coruscant, far from the institutions that had defined their parents’ earlier lives, and far from the Empire’s attention. Their earliest memories were of the hut by the lake, the surrounding forests, their parents, and the two droids who had been part of the household for as long as they could remember.

Eventually, Anakin decided that the twins were old enough to begin learning about the Force in earnest.

He started slowly.

The first lessons were meditation.

Luke found sitting still difficult at first. Leia was better at remaining physically motionless, though her thoughts wandered just as easily. Anakin taught them to quiet themselves, to pay attention to the world around them without immediately reacting to it, and to recognize the difference between ordinary senses and impressions that came through the Force.

From there came Force sensing.

Anakin had them close their eyes and identify objects placed around them. He moved quietly through the hut while they tried to determine where he was without looking. Sometimes he stood behind them holding one object in each hand and asked them which one he intended to drop.

Eventually, the lessons moved toward precognition.

Anakin tossed small objects toward them without warning and taught them to react before conscious thought caught up. He made simple games out of it, trying to keep the training interesting while gradually increasing the difficulty.

Then came physical practice.

He gave Luke and Leia sticks and taught them how to stand, how to move their feet, how to keep their balance, and how to spar without simply swinging wildly at one another.

That last instruction took some time.

The twins were competitive.

Very competitive.

Anakin also taught them how to fly the family skiff.

At first, they only sat beside him and watched. Later, under close supervision, each child was allowed to take the controls for short periods. They learned how to keep the vessel level, how to make gentle turns, how to read basic instruments, and how to approach a landing without frightening Padmé.

Basic telekinesis came next.

Small stones.

Tools.

Pieces of fruit.

Anything light enough that a mistake would not cause much damage.

Over time, both children learned to lift objects from the floor, guide them through the air, and set them down again without dropping them.

Eventually, Anakin decided they were ready for something more important.

One morning, he gathered both children together.

“Luke, Leia, we’re going to Ilum!”

Luke looked up at him.

“What’s Ilum?”

“An ancient Jedi world where there are all sorts of kyber crystals. There, you will find the crystal that calls to you, and that crystal will become your very own lightsaber.”

Leia’s eyes widened.

“Wow! Sounds very exciting! Let’s go!”

Anakin decided to make it a whole-family trip.

Padmé came.

Luke and Leia came.

Artoo and Threepio came.

The family boarded the Naboo skiff, lifted away from Takodana, and entered hyperspace toward Ilum.

Far away, Ilum itself had changed dramatically over the years.

The Empire had been strip-mining the planet for kyber crystals, extracting enormous quantities from beneath the surface. The crystals were being gathered to power the massive superweapon whose construction continued gradually elsewhere under Imperial supervision.

On Coruscant, Palpatine summoned several senior Imperial officers to his office.

He regarded one of them from behind his desk.

“Officer, how is the progress with the kyber crystal extraction?”

“It is on schedule, Your Majesty.”

Palpatine smiled.

“Good, good. We shall have the galaxy’s most powerful superweapon soon!”

Some time later, the Skywalker family’s skiff dropped out of hyperspace above Ilum.

Almost immediately, the instruments began warning Anakin of nearby contacts.

Imperial ships.

A heavy orbital patrol surrounded the world.

The moment the patrol detected the unauthorized vessel moving toward the planet, starfighters broke formation and opened fire.

Green laser bolts streaked past the skiff.

Anakin’s hands snapped onto the controls.

Years of wartime piloting experience returned instantly.

He rolled the skiff hard to one side as another volley passed beneath them. The vessel dove, climbed, then twisted between converging lines of fire as Imperial fighters closed from several directions.

Padmé gripped the side of her seat.

“Ani, turn back! Ilum has been occupied by the Empire! We can’t afford to keep going!”

“Padmé, don’t worry. I got this.”

“Ani!! You are endangering the kids!! This is not funny!!”

Anakin pulled the ship sharply upward as another fighter crossed their path.

“Padmé, please. Just trust me. I’ve flown through way worse than this before.”

“Leia Skywalker?”

Beru approached.

“Maybe one of Shmi’s relatives?”

Threepio turned toward her.

“Oh, she is Shmi’s granddaughter through Anakin.”

Owen’s eyes widened further.

“The same Anakin that came here decades ago and took you away?”

“Yes, Master Owen.”

“I did not know he has a daughter.”

Threepio became more urgent.

“She has been captured. We need help!”

Beru stepped closer.

“What can we do?”

“Do you have a ship we can use?”

“No, we only have a landspeeder.”

“Do you have enough credits for us to buy a ship?”

Owen shook his head.

“No, we are moisture farmers, Threepio. We don’t have many credits.”

“Enough credits to buy a one-way ride to Takodana?”

“Still no. We really don’t have a lot of money, Threepio.”

Artoo suddenly beeped.

Threepio turned toward him.

“Oh, that could actually work.”

Then he looked back at Owen.

“Can you take us to Mos Eisley in your landspeeder?”

Owen looked puzzled.

“Not sure how that would help, but sure, I guess.”

Owen took Artoo and Threepio to Mos Eisley and dropped them off there.

“Good luck.”

“Thank you, Master Owen.”

Artoo beeped gratefully.

Owen returned to his landspeeder and left for the homestead.

Threepio watched him disappear.

Then he turned toward Artoo.

“So what’s your plan exactly?”

Artoo gave a long series of beeps.

Threepio listened.

“Okay.”

The two droids entered Chalmun’s Spaceport Cantina.

The room was crowded.

Threepio rolled his shoulders back, stepped into the middle of the cantina, and shouted as loudly as possible:

“IS ANYBODY HERE GOING TO MAZ KANATA’S CASTLE ANYTIME SOON?”

Several patrons looked over.

A few clearly recognized Maz Kanata’s name.

One patron waved the droids toward him.

He was a Rodian.

Threepio approached, and the two began conversing in Rodese. The Rodian introduced himself as Salvo and explained that he visited Maz Kanata regularly for advice about the underworld. Threepio told him that both he and Artoo were also friends of Maz and urgently needed transportation to Takodana.

As Salvo had already been planning to visit Maz himself, he agreed to take them.

Not long afterward, Salvo’s ship left Tatooine.

He carried Threepio and Artoo all the way to Takodana and eventually landed near Maz Kanata’s castle.

Maz came to greet him.

“Hello, Salvo! I haven’t seen you in a while.”

Then she noticed the two droids standing behind him.

Her expression changed.

“Threepio! Artoo! What are you two doing here?”

Threepio hurried forward.

“We need to go back home immediately. Leia’s been captured by the Empire.”

Maz’s face became serious.

“Oh, that is terrible. I’ll contact Anakin right away.”

She initiated a holocall across the lake.

Anakin answered from the Skywalker hut.

“What’s up, Maz?”

“Your droids are with me. Come pick them up right away. It’s urgent.”

Anakin clearly had questions.

He decided not to ask them yet.

“I am coming right away.”

The call ended.

A few minutes later, the family skiff arrived at the castle.

Threepio and Artoo boarded with Anakin, and the three returned to the hut.

Once they were inside, Threepio explained everything.

“The Rebels got their hands on the Death Star plans, which were successfully transmitted to Leia. She originally planned to take those plans to Alderaan for safekeeping and analysis, but the Empire attacked. Leia fought Virex but lost. She has been captured and is now presumably imprisoned on the Death Star according to what Virex said to her. Artoo now has possession of the Death Star plans as well as Leia’s lightsaber.”

Artoo opened his compartments.

The Death Star plans emerged.

Then Leia’s lightsaber.

Anakin took both and decided to keep them with him.

Padmé looked toward the droids.

“So then how did you guys end up in Maz’s castle?”

Threepio continued.

“After Leia’s capture, Artoo took the two of us out of the ship in an escape pod. We ended up on Tatooine, which was the closest planet at the time. Artoo proposed that we go find your stepfamily, since they know us and might be able to help us. Turns out, they have almost nothing, so they could only take us to Mos Eisley. Once there, Artoo suggested that we use our friendship with Maz Kanata to hitch a ride to Takodana. It somehow worked, and that’s how we ended up in her castle.”

Anakin looked toward both droids with obvious pride.

“Well done, Artoo and Threepio. You guys really saved the day.”

Artoo beeped proudly.

“Thank you, Master Anakin.”

Anakin’s attention returned to the situation.

“I need to go save Leia.”

Padmé looked at him.

“By yourself?”

“No. I’ll need Han’s help. I can’t just approach the Death Star in our skiff. Its weapons are too weak. The *Millennium Falcon* is much better suited for this.”

Anakin immediately contacted Han.

Han answered.

“Hey, what’s up, buddy?”

“Are you and Chewie free right now? I really need your help for something.”

“Well, you’re in luck. We just finished delivering some cargo a few minutes ago, and haven’t accepted another job yet. So yes, we are free for now.”

“Can you come to Takodana right now? We need to infiltrate the Death Star.”

Han’s expression changed.

“That sounds like a nightmare. Why would I willingly get anywhere close to the Death Star?”

Anakin answered immediately.

“It concerns Leia, Han. She has been captured by the Empire and is currently held there.”

Han’s attitude transformed at once.

“Leia? Captured? I’m coming over right away!”

The call ended.

Padmé looked toward Anakin.

“I think Han has feelings for Leia. Look how fast he changed his tune after you told him it’s about Leia.”

Anakin sighed but said nothing.

A few hours later, the *Millennium Falcon* arrived on Takodana.

Anakin was already waiting outside the hut.

The Falcon’s ramp lowered.

Anakin promptly stepped aboard.

Padmé watched him from outside.

“Ani, please come back.”

Anakin looked back at her.

“I will. I promise.”

The ramp closed.

The *Millennium Falcon* lifted away from Takodana, climbed through the atmosphere, and disappeared into hyperspace.

Only then did something catastrophic occur to Anakin.

He realized they had overlooked one crucial problem.

“Wait, where IS the Death Star?”

Han turned toward him.

“Why are you asking me? How am I supposed to know?”

Anakin sighed.

In the urgency of Leia’s capture, he had never stopped to consider that none of them actually knew where the Death Star currently was.

But they were already in hyperspace.

Then Anakin remembered the plans he carried.

He also remembered what Threepio had said.

Leia had originally intended to take them to Alderaan.

Anakin looked toward Han.

“Let’s go to Alderaan then. We can deliver the Death Star plans there, just as Leia originally intended.”

Han frowned.

“So we’re not rescuing Leia anymore?”

“We can’t, until we find out where the Death Star is.”

Anakin paused.

“She is still alive; I can feel it through the Force. So we still have time.”

Han looked unconvinced.

“You better make sure this doesn’t end in a disaster.”

“She will be okay, Han. Don’t give up.”

Chewie let out a loud roar of encouragement.

Han looked toward the controls.

“Alright. Alderaan it is.”

Han and Chewie adjusted the *Millennium Falcon*’s destination.

The freighter raced through hyperspace toward Alderaan.
