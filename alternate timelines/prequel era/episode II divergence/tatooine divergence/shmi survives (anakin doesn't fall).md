# Part 1

Anakin Skywalker urged the swoop bike across the endless dunes of Tatooine with relentless speed. Sand blasted against his goggles and robes as twin suns gave way to darkness. Every instinct within him pulled him toward the Tusken Raider camp. Somewhere beyond the rolling desert, his mother was waiting.

He did not slow.

The distant glow of campfires eventually appeared against the night. The crude village stood silent except for the occasional cry of a bantha and the low murmur of Tusken voices drifting between tents.

Anakin brought the bike to a stop behind a rocky ridge and listened.

He reached out through the Force.

There.

A familiar presence.

"Weak... but alive."

His breathing steadied. Without hesitation, he slipped through the shadows toward the camp.

Avoiding the sentries, he moved between the tents until he reached the one where he sensed her. He quietly lifted the entrance flap and stepped inside.

The dim light of a small fire revealed Shmi Skywalker.

She was bound to a wooden frame, bruised from head to toe. Her breathing was shallow, and dried blood covered many of her wounds.

Her eyes slowly opened.

"Anakin..."

He rushed to her side.

"Mom."

His hands quickly untied the restraints.

"I'm here. You're safe now."

She tried to smile despite the pain.

"I knew... you'd come."

He gently supported her as she collapsed into his arms.

"I'm taking you home," he said firmly. "You're coming back to the Lars homestead."

She nodded weakly.

Anakin wrapped her in a blanket he found inside the tent and carefully lifted her into his arms.

A pair of Tusken voices sounded outside.

Without a word, Anakin ignited his lightsaber.

The blue blade flashed to life.

The two Raiders rushed into the tent in surprise.

Anakin struck swiftly, knocking one unconscious with the Force before disarming the other with a precise slash that sent the gaderffii stick flying.

Neither was allowed another chance to interfere.

Keeping Shmi securely in his arms, Anakin hurried back through the camp.

More cries echoed behind him as several Tuskens noticed the escape.

He leaped onto the swoop bike, settling his mother carefully behind him.

"Hold on."

She wrapped what little strength she had around him.

The engine roared.

The bike shot across the dunes while blaster fire disappeared into the darkness behind them.

By the time the Tusken Raiders organized a pursuit, Anakin was already far beyond their reach.

The following day, under the harsh daylight of Tatooine, the swoop bike came to a stop outside the Lars homestead.

Owen hurried from the moisture farm.

"Anakin!"

Beru gasped as she saw Shmi.

"Oh no..."

Cliegg, seated in his hoverchair near the entrance, stared in disbelief.

"Shmi..."

Anakin carefully lifted his mother from the bike.

"I found her."

Cliegg's eyes filled with tears.

"You brought her home."

"We don't have much time," Anakin said. "She needs proper medical treatment. I'm taking her into Mos Eisley immediately."

Padmé hurried outside after hearing the commotion. She immediately knelt beside Shmi.

"We'll get her help."

Just as everyone prepared to leave, the familiar dome of R2-D2 rolled rapidly toward the homestead from the direction where the Naboo starship had been hidden.

The astromech emitted an urgent series of whistles.

Anakin looked down.

"What is it, Artoo?"

The little droid projected a hologram.

Obi-Wan Kenobi appeared.

"If you're receiving this message, I've tracked the bounty hunter to Geonosis."

The transmission crackled.

"I've uncovered something much larger than expected. The Trade Federation has allied with Count Dooku and is assembling a massive droid army."

The hologram flickered.

"I've been captured. Whatever you do, be careful. They know you're connected to me."

The transmission ended.

Silence settled over the homestead.

Padmé looked directly at Anakin.

"We have to go."

Anakin hesitated.

"My mother..."

Shmi reached out and gently touched his arm.

"Go."

He looked down at her.

"I just got you back."

"And you've already saved me."

Her voice remained weak but steady.

"Obi-Wan needs you."

Padmé nodded.

"We can trust your family to care for her until we return."

Cliegg straightened himself in his hoverchair.

"You have my word."

Owen stepped forward.

"We'll take her into Mos Eisley ourselves."

Beru smiled reassuringly.

"We'll stay with her the entire time."

Padmé removed a small datachip from her belongings and handed it to Cliegg.

"This contains more than enough credits."

He looked surprised.

"Senator, this is too much."

"It's for Shmi's treatment," Padmé replied. "And for you."

He frowned.

"For me?"

"I know you lost your leg. Use these funds to purchase a prosthetic. There's no reason you should continue without one."

Cliegg looked down at the datachip before meeting her eyes.

"I don't know how to thank you."

"You don't have to."

She smiled warmly.

"Just take care of Shmi until we return."

"We will," Owen promised.

Anakin knelt beside his mother one last time.

"I'll come back as soon as I can."

"I know you will," Shmi whispered.

He embraced her carefully before standing.

Moments later, Anakin, Padmé, R2-D2, and C-3PO boarded the Naboo starship.

The sleek vessel lifted into the sky and disappeared toward Geonosis.

Once the ship had vanished beyond the horizon, Owen gently lifted Shmi into a transport.

"We'd better get moving."

Cliegg maneuvered his hoverchair alongside them.

"Let's bring her home for good."

Later that day, the Lars family arrived in Mos Eisley.

Using Padmé's generous funds, they admitted Shmi into the finest medical facility available on the planet.

Doctors immediately examined her injuries.

One physician approached the family afterward.

"She has suffered extensive trauma and severe dehydration. We've stabilized her condition. The bacta tank will greatly accelerate her recovery."

"When can she go in?" Owen asked.

"Immediately."

The family watched as medical staff carefully lowered Shmi into the bacta tank. Suspended within the healing fluid, her wounds began the slow process of repair.

Cliegg remained nearby, relief finally replacing weeks of despair.

A doctor soon approached him as well.

"I understand you're interested in a prosthetic replacement."

Cliegg nodded.

"I lost my leg some time ago."

"We can fit you with an advanced prosthetic immediately."

He glanced toward the datachip Padmé had given him.

"I finally have the means."

The operation was scheduled without delay.

Over the following days, Owen and Beru rarely left the medical facility.

Each morning, Beru visited Shmi's bacta chamber.

"She's looking better today," she would say.

Each afternoon, Owen checked on Cliegg as therapists helped him adapt to his new prosthetic.

"Take another step."

Cliegg carefully shifted his weight.

Then another.

Soon he crossed the room unaided.

He laughed for the first time in many days.

"I'd almost forgotten what this felt like."

About a week later, the doctors gathered the family together.

"The treatments have been successful."

The bacta tank slowly drained.

Shmi stepped out, healthy once more.

The bruises had vanished.

Her strength had returned.

She smiled as Beru wrapped her in a warm embrace.

"It's wonderful to see you standing again."

Shmi laughed softly.

"It's wonderful to be standing."

Across the hall, Cliegg walked confidently into the room using his new prosthetic leg.

He crossed the floor without assistance before stopping in front of Shmi.

She smiled through tears.

"You can walk."

He smiled back.

"And you're home."

The family embraced together, grateful that both recoveries had been successful.

When they finally returned to the Lars homestead, the experience had changed them all.

One evening, Cliegg stood outside with Owen and Beru, looking across the open desert.

"We were fortunate this time."

Owen nodded.

"We can't depend on fortune again."

Beru folded her arms.

"We need stronger defenses."

Cliegg agreed.

"We'll reinforce every entrance."

Owen continued, "We'll expand the perimeter sensors."

"And keep a constant watch," Beru added.

Cliegg looked toward the distant horizon.

"The Tusken Raiders will never again threaten this family."

The others nodded in silent agreement as the suns slowly descended over the desert that they now intended to guard more carefully than ever before.

# Part 2

A few days after their quiet wedding on Naboo, Anakin Skywalker stood beside Padmé Amidala as the Naboo starship lifted into hyperspace once more.

"I made my mother a promise," Anakin said as he guided the ship. "After everything that happened, I told her I'd come back."

Padmé smiled.

"Then let's go see her."

R2-D2 chirped happily from behind the cockpit while C-3PO looked out one of the viewports.

"Oh my," the protocol droid said. "Back to Tatooine. I do hope the sand will be less... enthusiastic this time."

Before long, the familiar deserts of Tatooine stretched beneath them.

The Lars family greeted them warmly as they arrived at the homestead.

Shmi stepped forward, healthy and smiling.

"Anakin."

He embraced her tightly.

"You look wonderful."

"I feel wonderful."

She laughed softly.

"The doctors in Mos Eisley performed miracles."

Cliegg walked over with a confident stride, his prosthetic leg moving naturally beneath him.

"And this works better than I ever imagined."

Anakin smiled.

"I'm glad."

Owen shook his hand.

"It's good to have you back."

Beru greeted Padmé with a warm embrace.

"It's nice to see you again, Senator."

"And it's nice to see all of you doing so well," Padmé replied.

As the afternoon wore on, the family shared a meal together, catching up on everything that had happened since Geonosis.

Later, Shmi found an opportunity to speak privately with her son outside the homestead.

She folded her arms and smiled knowingly.

"You and Padmé seem very close."

Anakin looked at her.

"We are."

Shmi tilted her head.

"Is there something you'd like to tell me?"

He hesitated.

"This stays between us."

"You have my word."

Anakin took a slow breath.

"Padmé and I were married."

Shmi's eyes widened.

"Married?"

He nodded.

A broad smile spread across her face.

"Oh, Anakin..."

She embraced him.

"I'm happy for you."

When they separated, she looked at him curiously.

"Why didn't you invite me to the wedding?"

Anakin gave an apologetic smile.

"It was very small."

"How small?"

"Just me, Padmé, the officiant... and R2-D2 and Threepio."

She blinked.

"That was everyone?"

"Yes."

Shmi frowned gently.

"Why?"

Anakin looked away toward the desert.

"We had to keep it secret."

"Secret?"

He remained silent.

"Anakin?"

He finally sighed.

"The Jedi don't allow marriage."

Shmi's expression changed immediately.

Her smile faded into quiet concern.

"So your marriage has to remain hidden."

He nodded once.

"For now."

She was silent for several moments.

Finally she said, "Anakin, I've always wanted you to live an honest life."

"I know."

"I don't like hearing that my son has chosen a path built on secrecy and deception."

Anakin looked down at the ground.

"It's... complicated."

"Perhaps."

She gently placed a hand on his shoulder.

"But honesty matters, no matter how complicated life becomes."

He was quiet.

"I'll think about it."

Shmi searched his face.

"I hope you do."

Although Anakin answered respectfully, she could tell that her words had not truly reached him.

Not long afterward, Anakin and Padmé said their goodbyes and departed Tatooine to continue their honeymoon.

Shmi watched their ship disappear into the sky.

That evening she remained awake long after everyone else had gone to bed.

Cliegg noticed her sitting alone.

"Something on your mind?"

She nodded.

"I love my son."

"I know."

"I also can't bear the thought of him living a dishonest life."

Cliegg listened quietly.

"I tried talking to him."

"And?"

"He heard me... but he didn't truly listen."

She lowered her eyes.

"If I do nothing, he'll continue hiding his marriage indefinitely."

Cliegg asked softly, "What are you going to do?"

Shmi looked toward the stars.

"I'm going to force the truth into the open."

Within the next day, she composed a brief anonymous message addressed to the Jedi Temple on Coruscant.

It contained only a single sentence.

"Anakin Skywalker is married to Padmé Amidala."

She sent it anonymously, leaving nothing that could identify its sender.

Several days later, while Anakin and Padmé were still enjoying their honeymoon together, an urgent transmission arrived from the Jedi Temple.

Anakin activated the message.

A Temple administrator appeared.

"Padawan Skywalker, the Jedi Council requests your immediate presence. Your attendance is required without delay."

The transmission ended.

Padmé looked at him.

"That didn't sound routine."

"No."

He frowned.

"Something's wrong."

Without delay, they set course for Coruscant.

Upon arriving at the Jedi Temple, Anakin proceeded alone into the Council Chamber.

The members of the Jedi Council were already waiting.

Mace Windu spoke first.

"Padawan Skywalker, we recently received an anonymous communication."

Yoda nodded.

"Serious, the contents are."

Ki-Adi-Mundi activated a small holoprojector.

A single sentence appeared.

"Anakin Skywalker is married to Padmé Amidala."

Anakin stared at it.

After a long silence he asked, "Who sent this?"

Mace answered calmly.

"We do not know."

"It was transmitted anonymously," said Plo Koon. "There is no reliable way to identify its source."

Anakin's thoughts immediately turned toward Tatooine.

Could it have been his mother?

No...

That didn't seem like something she would do.

At least, not in his experience.

Mace Windu looked directly at him.

"We require a direct answer."

Obi-Wan remained silent, watching his former Padawan carefully.

Mace continued.

"Is the message true?"

Anakin closed his eyes briefly.

If he denied it now, suspicion would remain over him indefinitely. Every action would be questioned. Every absence examined.

There was no point.

He opened his eyes.

"Yes."

The chamber remained silent.

"I am married to Padmé Amidala."

No member of the Council appeared surprised.

Yoda spoke first.

"Honest with us, you have been."

Mace nodded.

"We appreciate your honesty."

Obi-Wan finally spoke.

"Anakin."

His voice carried disappointment, but also understanding.

"You knew what the rules required."

"I did."

After several moments of discussion, Mace addressed him again.

"The Jedi Order cannot permit one of its members to remain while openly choosing marriage."

Anakin nodded.

"I understand."

"However," Mace continued, "this need not end poorly."

Plo Koon added, "If you cooperate, we are prepared to offer you a graceful departure."

"You may keep your lightsaber," Mace said.

"You will always have permission to visit the Jedi Temple," Ki-Adi-Mundi continued.

"But you will no longer be a member of the Jedi Order."

Anakin listened quietly.

Then Obi-Wan stood.

"Masters."

The Council turned toward him.

"I have one recommendation."

Mace inclined his head.

"Speak."

"Anakin has displayed extraordinary courage during the Battle of Geonosis."

Obi-Wan looked at his former apprentice.

"He fought bravely under impossible circumstances."

He turned back toward the Council.

"I recommend that, before his departure, he be granted the rank of Jedi Knight."

Several members exchanged thoughtful glances.

Obi-Wan continued.

"He has earned that distinction through his service."

"He should leave this Order as Former Jedi Knight Anakin Skywalker, not Former Jedi Padawan Anakin Skywalker."

Silence followed.

The Council deliberated.

After several minutes, Yoda spoke.

"Earned a knighthood, perhaps he has."

Mace nodded.

"Regardless of his decision to marry, his service has demonstrated qualities worthy of consideration."

One by one, the Council agreed.

Finally Mace rose.

"Anakin Skywalker."

Anakin stepped forward.

"By decision of this Council, you are hereby granted the rank of Jedi Knight."

Obi-Wan smiled faintly.

"Congratulations... Knight Skywalker."

Anakin bowed respectfully.

"Thank you."

Mace then continued.

"Knight Skywalker, we accept your resignation from the Jedi Order."

Anakin unclipped his lightsaber.

For a moment he looked at it.

"I wasn't expecting to keep this."

"It belongs to you," Mace replied.

"You leave with our respect."

Yoda nodded.

"Peace be with you."

Anakin bowed deeply before every member of the Council.

"Thank you... Masters."

He turned and walked from the chamber.

His departure was solemn, but without anger or hostility.

A few days later, Anakin and Padmé returned once more to Tatooine.

The Lars family welcomed them again.

During the evening, Anakin approached his mother.

"Can we talk?"

Shmi nodded.

"Of course."

The two walked a short distance away from the homestead.

Anakin folded his arms.

"I've left the Jedi Order."

Her eyes widened.

"You have?"

"They found out about my marriage."

She looked at him quietly.

He continued.

"They received an anonymous message."

Another silence passed.

Anakin finally asked, "Mother..."

She already knew what was coming.

"Were you the one who sent it?"

She did not look away.

"Yes."

He let out a slow breath.

"I thought so."

"I won't deny it."

"Why?"

Shmi answered softly.

"Because I meant everything I told you."

She looked him in the eyes.

"I don't want my son living a dishonest life."

"I know."

"I tried to convince you myself."

"You did."

"And when I realized I couldn't..."

She lowered her head.

"...I decided to take matters into my own hands."

Anakin was quiet for a long moment.

Finally he sighed.

"I can't say your reasoning doesn't make sense."

She looked up.

"I knew you would be upset."

"I was."

He gave a small, tired smile.

"But at least your logic is sound."

"I never wanted to hurt you."

"I know."

"I wanted you to be free of a secret that would follow you for the rest of your life."

Anakin thought back to the Council chamber.

To the moment he had admitted the truth.

To the respect with which he had been treated afterward.

He slowly nodded.

"It isn't the path I expected."

"No."

"But it's an honest one."

Mother and son stood together beneath the evening sky.

After a while, Anakin smiled.

"I don't want this to come between us."

"Neither do I."

He embraced her.

"I'm still glad I came back."

"So am I."

When they separated, the tension between them had faded.

Neither carried lasting resentment.

The difficult conversation had ended with understanding instead of bitterness.

The following morning, Anakin and Padmé said their farewells once more.

Cliegg shook Anakin's hand.

"Take care of yourselves."

"We will."

Beru hugged Padmé.

"Enjoy the rest of your trip."

"We intend to."

Owen smiled.

"You'll always have a place here."

Anakin looked once more at his mother.

"I'll see you again soon."

Shmi smiled warmly.

"I'll be waiting."

The Naboo starship rose into the bright Tatooine sky, carrying Anakin, Padmé, R2-D2, and C-3PO back toward the peaceful honeymoon they had interrupted only briefly.

# Part 3

A few weeks passed.

Across the Republic, reports of growing conflict arrived almost daily. More systems declared for the Separatist cause, while clone forces were deployed to defend loyal worlds. The newly formed Grand Army of the Republic expanded its operations with increasing urgency.

Watching the Republic prepare for war while remaining on the sidelines steadily wore on Anakin Skywalker.

One evening, he stood beside the window of the apartment he shared with Padmé on Coruscant, staring out over the endless city.

"They won't let me serve as a Jedi General because I'm no longer a Jedi."

He folded his arms.

"But I assure you, I am perfectly capable of doing that job as well as any Jedi."

Padmé looked up from the documents she had been reviewing.

"I know, Ani."

She smiled gently.

"I don't doubt your skills."

"They won't let me because of a technicality."

"It's more than just a technicality..."

"They must realize that they need every trained Force user they have, right?"

He turned to face her.

"This is a war. Technicality doesn't matter. Practicality does."

Padmé set her datapad aside.

"I agree with you in principle, but if the Republic won't authorize you to command clones, what good will you accomplish by complaining to me here?"

Anakin gave her a slightly snarky look.

"Well, you are a politician, are you not?"

She immediately recognized the tone.

"Oh no..."

"Why don't you go introduce a motion in your good ol' Senate to grant permission for non-Jedi to command clones on a case-by-case basis?"

Padmé blinked.

"Ani, that's not how this works..."

"Of course that's how this works!"

He pointed dramatically toward the Senate District visible through the window.

"I know how the Senate works!"

Padmé crossed her arms.

"First of all, no, you don't."

Anakin opened his mouth to object.

She raised a finger.

"But... all right. Let's say I can do this."

She leaned back in her chair.

"What exactly do I say?"

She spread her hands theatrically.

"'My husband wants to join the war effort but your current laws don't allow it, therefore I propose to completely restructure how the Grand Army of the Republic works?'"

Anakin immediately shook his head.

"No, of course not."

He began pacing as ideas formed in his mind.

"You phrase it like this."

He cleared his throat.

"'The Republic desperately needs more trained and competent generals. Former Jedi are as capable as current Jedi to serve in that capacity. Therefore, we should not rule out allowing former Jedi to serve just because they are no longer Jedi.'"

Padmé considered the wording.

After a moment she nodded slowly.

"Okay..."

She looked at him.

"That actually sounds somewhat plausible."

A hopeful smile spread across Anakin's face.

"So you'll do it?"

"I'll see what I can do."

She pointed a finger toward him.

"But I can't guarantee this will work."

He nodded eagerly.

"And..."

Her expression became mischievous.

"You're coming with me to the Senate."

Anakin frowned.

"I am?"

"Yes."

She smiled innocently.

"So if it doesn't work out, the Senate can laugh at you."

Anakin stared at her.

"...Wow."

"You are a mean one sometimes."

Padmé answered only with a satisfied smirk.

A few days later, Anakin and Padmé entered the Galactic Senate building together.

The vast chamber buzzed with debate as hundreds of senators discussed military appropriations and emergency measures related to the growing war.

Anakin took a seat in the visitors' gallery overlooking the chamber while Padmé joined the Naboo delegation below.

When the presiding officer recognized her, Padmé rose.

"Esteemed colleagues," she began, "the Republic now faces a conflict unlike any in recent history."

The chamber gradually quieted.

"Our military has expanded rapidly, and with it comes an increasing need for capable leadership."

Several senators nodded.

"The Jedi have answered this call admirably."

She paused.

"However, there exists another group whose abilities should not be overlooked."

She continued with the proposal Anakin had suggested.

"Former Jedi possess the same training, discipline, and battlefield experience as current members of the Order. The Republic desperately needs more trained and competent generals. Former Jedi are as capable as current Jedi to serve in that capacity. Therefore, we should not rule out allowing former Jedi to serve just because they are no longer Jedi."

A murmur spread throughout the chamber.

Some senators approved.

Others exchanged uncertain looks.

One senator stood.

"Senator Amidala."

Padmé inclined her head.

"I agree that former Jedi are as capable as current Jedi."

He paused before continuing.

"But do you know why we trust current Jedi in such a capacity?"

Padmé listened carefully.

"They possess internal hierarchy, accountability, and a chain of command."

He gestured toward the chamber.

"If a Jedi General acts improperly, the Jedi Council has the authority to discipline them."

Several senators voiced agreement.

The senator continued.

"If a former Jedi serving as a general does something out of line..."

He looked directly at Padmé.

"...who will keep them accountable?"

The chamber fell quiet.

Padmé remained silent for several moments.

Finally she answered honestly.

"That is a fair question."

She lowered her head slightly.

"I have not considered this."

The debate continued for hours.

Some senators argued that excluding trained Force users during wartime weakened the Republic unnecessarily.

Others worried that creating military commanders outside the Jedi Order would introduce dangerous uncertainty.

Eventually discussion shifted toward compromise.

Late in the session, the presiding officer called for a final proposal.

After further amendments, the Senate reached a provisional decision.

The motion would pass on a limited basis.

Rather than immediately opening command positions to all former Jedi, the Republic would establish a new military title.

The office would be known as Republic General.

Its authority and responsibilities would be equivalent to those of a Jedi General, but it would be reserved specifically for non-Jedi appointed by the Senate.

Furthermore, the Senate agreed that only a single appointment would be made for the time being.

Anakin Skywalker would serve as the Republic's first Republic General.

His performance would determine whether similar appointments would ever be authorized again.

One senator then rose.

"There remains the matter of accountability."

The chamber quieted once more.

He turned toward Padmé.

"Senator Amidala."

"Yes, Senator."

"As you have brought this motion into this body, it would only be fair to ask that you oversee General Skywalker in his new capacity as Republic General."

Padmé listened intently.

"You are to follow General Skywalker on his campaigns and personally report his actions and performance to the Senate."

The chamber remained silent.

The senator's voice grew more serious.

"If General Skywalker ever causes any harms to the Republic, you may be held personally responsible for it and may be subject to consequences."

He allowed the weight of the statement to settle before asking,

"Do you understand?"

Padmé did not answer immediately.

She glanced toward the visitors' gallery, where Anakin watched in stunned silence.

After a pause, she faced the Senate again.

"Yes, Senator."

The presiding officer struck the gavel.

"The motion is adopted as amended."

The chamber gradually emptied as senators departed to their next duties.

Outside the Senate chamber, Anakin caught up with Padmé.

"You agreed to that."

She gave him a tired look.

"I did."

"They're making you responsible for me."

"They are."

He rubbed the back of his neck.

"I... wasn't expecting that."

"Neither was I."

She smiled faintly.

"So you'd better behave yourself."

Anakin laughed.

"I usually do."

Padmé raised an eyebrow.

"Usually?"

"...Most of the time."

She shook her head with a quiet laugh.

"I suppose that's the best promise I'm going to get."

Not long afterward, the Republic formally issued Anakin's commission.

He was granted the rank and authority of Republic General, a position equivalent in duties and command authority to a Jedi General while remaining distinct from the Jedi Order.

His first assignment followed immediately.

Republic General Anakin Skywalker received command of the 479th Division of clone troopers.

With his commission complete and his command established, Anakin prepared to lead the 479th Division into the growing conflict, while Senator Padmé Amidala prepared to accompany him, carrying both her responsibilities to the Republic and the obligation the Senate had placed upon her.

# Clovis Interlude: Part 1

Not long after Anakin Skywalker received his commission as a Republic General, an urgent summons arrived at the apartment he shared with Padmé Amidala.

Anakin read the message twice.

"Oh no," he muttered. "What have we done?"

Padmé looked over from her desk.

"I don't think we've done anything wrong."

She stood and took the datapad from him.

"Let's just go see what they have to say."

The following day, Anakin and Padmé entered a small meeting room inside the Senate building. Several senators were already waiting for them.

At the head of the table sat Senator Adrian Dabello, chairman of the Senate Intelligence Oversight Committee.

Dabello studied the two of them for a moment before speaking.

"Relax, Senator Amidala and General Skywalker. You have done nothing wrong."

Anakin's shoulders relaxed slightly.

Padmé gave him a brief glance.

Dabello continued.

"This meeting is not about you."

He paused.

"It's about... Senator Rush Clovis."

Anakin and Padmé exchanged a look.

Anakin leaned forward.

"Clovis?"

Dabello nodded.

"Recently, our committee has found leads suggesting that Senator Clovis may be collaborating with the Separatists."

Anakin's expression immediately became serious.

"Then why are we the ones called in instead of him?"

Dabello turned toward Padmé.

"Senator Amidala, it is my understanding that you and Senator Clovis have been quite close in the past, correct?"

Padmé hesitated only briefly.

"Yes. We were."

Dabello folded his hands.

"We called you in because we want you to investigate Senator Clovis."

Padmé listened carefully.

"Spy on him. Figure out what he is actually up to, and whether he is collaborating with the Separatists."

Anakin frowned.

"Why us?"

"I believe that Senator Amidala's personal past relationship with Senator Clovis will make it significantly easier for her to get close to him in order to uncover the truth."

Padmé considered the request.

Then she nodded.

"Very well, Senator Dabello. We accept this mission."

Dabello's expression remained stern.

"Remember, Senator. The closer you can get to him, the better."

Padmé nodded.

"I understand. I will do whatever is necessary to succeed in my mission."

Anakin immediately added,

"Then I'll do whatever I have to do to protect her."

Dabello gave them both a firm nod.

"Thank you, Senator Amidala and General Skywalker. Good luck to both of you."

The meeting ended shortly afterward.

As Anakin and Padmé walked back toward their apartment, neither spoke for several minutes.

Eventually Anakin broke the silence.

"What exactly does Dabello mean by 'quite close in the past'?"

In Huttese.

Padmé.
